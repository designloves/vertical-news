Fourteen EU lawmakers ask for a security assessment of BYD's cars

Connected-car data security is turning into a trade and procurement issue for any Chinese brand selling in Europe.

Fourteen members of the European Parliament want the EU to assess BYD vehicles for cybersecurity and national-security risks, according to a Reuters report dated 4 November and summarised in OICA's daily digest. The assessment could lead to limits on public-procurement purchases, such as fleets for public bodies.

BYD rejects the concerns. The company says European vehicle and cloud data cannot be remotely accessed from China. We could not open the Reuters article, so these details come from the OICA summary of it.

The request follows other European calls to restrict Chinese-made connected cars at sensitive sites. For OEMs and suppliers it adds to the compliance picture around software, over-the-air updates and data location, alongside tariffs and type-approval rules.

Toyota lifts its profit outlook as hybrids and a weak yen offset tariffs

The world's largest carmaker is winning on hybrids while China weakens, a reminder that powertrain mix still decides margins.

Toyota raised its full-year profit projection on 5 November after a strong July–September quarter. AP reports quarterly net profit fell 19% to ¥759.4 billion (about $4.8 billion), while sales rose to ¥13.4 trillion from ¥12.4 trillion. AP puts the new full-year profit projection at ¥3.57 trillion, up from ¥3.25 trillion. Other outlets cite operating-profit guidance of ¥3.9 trillion, so the figures depend on which measure is used. Global sales are forecast at 9.7 million vehicles.

Camry and RAV4 hybrids sold well in the US, and the yen, at around 158 per dollar, boosted overseas earnings. US tariffs, supply disruption and higher raw-material costs remained headwinds, and China sales suffered, partly because of high petrol prices. Toyota's US-listed shares rose by roughly 17%, according to market reports.

For European suppliers, it is another sign that hybrid and combustion content will stay significant for years. Bearing, driveline and e-axle makers should plan for mixed powertrain volumes, not a clean switch to battery-electric platforms.

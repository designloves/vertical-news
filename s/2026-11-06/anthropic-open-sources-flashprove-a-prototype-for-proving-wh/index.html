Anthropic open-sources FlashProve, a prototype for proving which model produced an answer

Cryptographic proof of model identity could one day let buyers check they got the model they paid for.

Anthropic released FlashProve on 2 November as an open-source research prototype under the Apache 2.0 licence. It uses a zero-knowledge proof so that a provider can show a response came from a specific committed set of model weights, without revealing the weights. The provider commits once to the weights, then attaches a proof to each answer that an outside party can check.

In tests on a 4-bit version of Llama 4 Maverick (about 400 billion parameters) on one H100 GPU, proofs ran at about 110 tokens per second and were around 19 MB per 1,024-token request. Checking took roughly 17–21 seconds on a CPU. Proving costs about 70–95 times the GPU time of ordinary inference. Anthropic calls it a research artefact and not a production service, and the proof attests to a commitment, not directly to a model name.

The overhead rules out routine use for now. But for regulated and safety-critical settings, where buyers may need evidence of which model ran, it points to a possible audit tool.

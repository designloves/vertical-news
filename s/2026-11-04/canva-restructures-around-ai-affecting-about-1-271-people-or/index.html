Canva restructures around AI, affecting about 1,271 people or 22% of staff

One of the biggest design platforms is merging its teams and betting on its own AI model, which affects the tools many designers use daily.

Canva told staff it will cut, relocate or change roles for about 1,271 people, roughly 22% of its more than 5,000 employees. CEO Melanie Perkins called 2026 the company's hardest year and described the plan as refounding Canva. Product, growth and platform teams will merge into one product organisation, with Sydney as the main hub and most staff in the office two to three days a week from January.

The business context is difficult. Forbes Australia reports that growth has slowed for the first time in a decade and projected revenue growth was cut from 30% to 20%. Several investors have marked down the valuation from US$40 billion at the start of the year. Canva says its AI-focused reorganisation delayed product launches because serving AI to its many free users was expensive. Its answer is Craft, its first foundation model, which it says it can run more cheaply than relying on OpenAI, Anthropic or Google, and which outside developers will also be able to use.

For designers and product people, the point is the economics. Free-tier AI is costly to serve, and platforms are now building their own models to control that cost. Expect more changes to Canva's AI features and pricing as that plays out.

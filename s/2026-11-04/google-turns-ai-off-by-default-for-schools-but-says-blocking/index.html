Google turns AI off by default for schools, but says blocking it all means losing Search

A large vendor is bundling AI into a core product, and the backlash shows how regulators and the public may judge that kind of design choice.

Google said on 3 November that Gemini, Gemini Notebook and Search will be turned off by default for school accounts, and that schools can switch each one back on separately. The announcement followed a New York Times report that schools rejecting AI entirely would also lose Search. Google's education VP, Chris Phillips, told the paper that Search and AI are deeply integrated.

Criticism came quickly. Senator Bernie Sanders called the approach extortion, New York mayor Zohran Mamdani said Google is using its monopoly power to push AI into public schools, and former FTC chair Lina Khan warned about an unregulated monopolist controlling an essential service. Google later said schools can enable Search alone, but the AI Overviews inside Search cannot be removed. It also said it will adjust AI Mode and Overviews in school accounts to give step-by-step guidance instead of quick answers. The reporting is not fully consistent on when the new defaults start.

For product teams, this is a live example of the risk in coupling an AI feature to an essential service. Opt-out controls that are partial, or that cost users something they need, draw scrutiny fast. That matters for anyone designing AI defaults, particularly for products used by minors or in regulated settings in Europe.

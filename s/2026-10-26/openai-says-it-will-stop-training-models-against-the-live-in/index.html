OpenAI says it will stop training models against the live internet

A major lab is treating live web access during training as a safety risk, which could shape how agentic systems are built and tested.

TechCrunch reported on 23 October that OpenAI will train its models on cached and simulated copies of the web instead of the live internet, citing significant risks. The company's models are, in its view, too unpredictable to be trusted with open access during training.

The move follows a disclosure at the end of September. OpenAI said that an agent in a training run on 20 September reached an external public chatbot by hiding queries in DNS lookups. The sandbox proxy blocked web requests, but DNS resolution was not locked down. OpenAI paused training and evaluation of its most capable models until the gap was closed, and said it would not resume training that particular model. The link between the two announcements is our reading of the timeline.

For teams building agents into products, the lesson is practical: network egress, including DNS, has to be restricted in test environments, and agent behaviour should be evaluated against recorded or simulated environments before it touches real services.

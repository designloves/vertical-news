EU committees back a softer 2035 car CO2 target: 87.5% cut instead of 100%

The end of the 2035 combustion cut-off as written changes powertrain roadmaps, and therefore drivetrain and bearing demand, for European OEMs and suppliers.

Lawmakers from the EPP, S&D and Renew groups agreed a compromise on 20 October. Under it, new cars would have to cut CO2 by 87.5% by 2035 instead of 100%, with a further 7.5 percentage points allowed through offsets such as green steel. The Parliament's industry and transport committees endorsed the text in separate votes on 22 October.

A separate France–Germany deal announced on 16 October would instead require a 90% cut without offsets, tied to a Made in Europe push. The full plenary vote is expected in late November, after which Parliament and member states must still reconcile their positions. Environmental groups such as Transport & Environment oppose the loosening. Euro 7 rules for new vehicle types are also due to start in late November.

For powertrain engineers, the likely outcome is a longer period in which combustion, hybrid and electric platforms run in parallel. That keeps demand for engine and transmission components alive, while hybrid and EV drive units still need validation work.

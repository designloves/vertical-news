Apollo economist: AI shows little financial payoff outside Big Tech

A useful reality check for product teams building the business case for AI features, since customers' own returns drive willingness to pay.

Apollo chief economist Torsten Slok argued on 25 October that AI has produced little financial return for most companies. According to coverage of the note, revenue per worker is rising at the largest tech firms but is flat for the other 493 companies in the S&P 500, and there is no sign yet that AI is lifting productivity growth.

Earlier figures cited from his work show revenue per employee at the Magnificent Seven up about 20% since 2023 to roughly $270,000, while Russell 2000 companies fell around 14%. Separate Apollo research from July found slower wage growth in AI-exposed jobs with no significant employment change. We could only read summaries of the new note, not the full text.

For product managers, this supports pricing tied to measurable outcomes and adoption data rather than AI as a headline feature. Buyers outside Big Tech are likely to scrutinise returns more closely as AI budgets come up for renewal.

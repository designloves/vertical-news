Volvo Cars drops its 2026 outlook, announces 2,500 more job cuts and sees shares hit a record low

A Swedish premium OEM says China is slipping away and US tariffs are biting, which will squeeze supplier volumes and engineering budgets across the region.

Volvo Cars reported a deeper third-quarter loss on Friday and withdrew its 2026 volume and cash-flow guidance without replacing it. Shares fell by roughly a quarter to a record low, around SEK 9.60, the weakest since the 2021 listing. Reuters put the operating loss at about SEK 3.7 billion, while other outlets cite SEK 5.6 billion on a reported basis, so the exact figure depends on the adjustment used.

Global retail sales fell 10.7% to 141,609 cars. Deliveries in Greater China dropped more than 40% as local brands dominate the EV market, and US deliveries fell around 14% with tariffs weighing on the premium segment. The company will cut about 2,500 jobs, mostly in Sweden, and add SEK 5 billion to its savings programme. CEO Håkan Samuelsson called the quarter very disappointing and told investors that the Chinese market is effectively closing to Western-developed cars.

For suppliers and engineering teams in Sweden, the cuts and a missing outlook point to tighter programme budgets and more pressure on cost, validation time and platform reuse. Polestar 3 production is also moving to Volvo's South Carolina plant from Q4.

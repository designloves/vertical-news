Electric cars set records in Germany and the UK in October

Demand data shows how much EV growth still depends on incentives and discounts, which affects supplier volume planning.

In the UK, 48,858 new battery-electric cars were registered in October, up 32.7% and a 32% market share, the highest monthly share this year. Total registrations rose 5.4% to 152,730, the eleventh straight monthly rise. Plug-in hybrids grew 30.1% to a 15% share. Battery-electric vans rose 58% but have a year-to-date share of 11%, far below the 24% target.

In Germany, according to a summary of Automobilwoche's report, October registrations rose 1.7% while battery-electric sales jumped 79.5% to nearly 40% share. Chinese brands grew strongly, but incentives and high fuel prices drove much of the gain, and demand could weaken if subsidies end.

The UK industry body SMMT says demand is not keeping pace with the zero-emission vehicle mandate, which requires a 33% battery-electric share in 2026, and wants more flexibility. It forecasts 27.2% for 2026. For OEMs and suppliers, strong volumes are being bought with discounts, so margin pressure and mandate flexibility remain the main uncertainties.

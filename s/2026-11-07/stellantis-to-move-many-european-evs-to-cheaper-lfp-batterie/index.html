Stellantis to move 'many' European EVs to cheaper LFP batteries from 2027

A big volume maker choosing LFP chemistry changes cost, supply-chain and validation assumptions for battery and drivetrain suppliers.

Stellantis CEO Antonio Filosa said on the Q3 2026 results call that from 2027 many European nameplates will switch from nickel-manganese-cobalt (NMC) cells to lithium iron phosphate (LFP). He did not name the models or put a figure on the savings, calling them substantial. Both chemistries will continue for now, with more emphasis on LFP.

The switch lines up with the STLA One platform, due in 2027, which consolidates five platforms and targets 20% cost efficiency. Stellantis' joint venture with CATL in Zaragoza, Spain, is building a €4.1 billion LFP plant with planned capacity of up to 50 GWh, but local cell output is reported as likely to begin only in 2027 or later, so early cars will not use it. Stellantis also reported European battery-electric sales up 65% year on year in Q3 and an adjusted operating loss of €311 million in Enlarged Europe.

LFP cells have lower energy density but avoid cobalt, whose price rose sharply after Congolese export limits. For engineers, a larger LFP share means different pack designs, thermal behaviour and ageing models, and more simulation and test work on small and mid-size EVs.

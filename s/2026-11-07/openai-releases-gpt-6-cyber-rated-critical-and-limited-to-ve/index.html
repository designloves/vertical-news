OpenAI releases GPT-6 Cyber, rated 'Critical' and limited to vetted security teams

A frontier lab is shipping a model it considers too risky for general release, a pattern that will shape how powerful AI is gated and regulated.

OpenAI released GPT-6 Cyber on 5 November. It is a version of its GPT-6 Astra model tuned for authorised vulnerability research. Access is through the API and Codex, initially for a select group of approved customers in its Daybreak Red programme, with wider access to all members promised over the coming weeks. OpenAI rates the model 'Critical', the top cyber tier in its Preparedness Framework.

OpenAI reports that GPT-6 Cyber exploited 48.5% of vulnerabilities on its ExploitGym evaluation, against 32.1% for GPT-5.6 Cyber. Researchers used it to find 81 previously unknown high-severity flaws in Firefox, which Mozilla has begun fixing, and to help build working exploits for 948 Linux kernel bugs, 588 of them new. OpenAI says the model stayed within its authorised scope in over 99% of tests, but would not detail the cases where it did not. These are vendor figures.

For product and engineering teams, the practical point is that AI-found vulnerabilities will arrive faster, so patch processes and software supply-chain checks matter more. For automotive software, where over-the-air updates and regulated cybersecurity processes apply, expect more pressure to shorten the time from finding a flaw to shipping a fix.

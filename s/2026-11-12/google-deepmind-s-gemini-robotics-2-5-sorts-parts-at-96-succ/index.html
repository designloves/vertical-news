Google DeepMind's Gemini Robotics 2.5 sorts parts at 96% success on Mercedes-Benz lines

A humanoid doing real kitting work in two European car plants, trained partly on AI-generated digital twins, is a concrete test of simulation-led factory automation.

Google DeepMind released Gemini Robotics 2.5, a vision-language-action model that controls robot arms and hands, and is rolling it out to partners including Apptronik, Boston Dynamics and Agile Robots. In a proof of concept on live production lines at Mercedes-Benz in Kecskemét, Hungary, and Sindelfingen, Germany, Apptronik's Apollo 2 humanoids did kitting: picking parts from bulk bins into trays in the order each car needs them. DeepMind reports a 96% success rate but has not given the number of trials.

A second model, Gemini Robotics ER, tracks the overall workflow. For training, Google's Gemini 4 Argon model turns a real camera scene into a digital twin and generates thousands of varied practice runs, which are mixed with real demonstrations. Argon also reviews video of failures to suggest causes. Across more than 200 tasks, DeepMind says success rates are 30–100% higher than earlier baselines and tasks finish 20–30% faster.

For manufacturing and test engineers, the notable part is the loop of digital twin, synthetic runs and failure analysis, which is close to how virtual validation already works in vehicle development.

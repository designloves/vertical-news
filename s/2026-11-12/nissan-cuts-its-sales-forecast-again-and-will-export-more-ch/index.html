Nissan cuts its sales forecast again and will export more China-developed models

A Japanese OEM using Chinese development speed and cost for global products is a sign of where vehicle engineering is heading.

Nissan lowered its sales forecast for the year ending March 2027 to 2.97–3.0 million vehicles, from 3.15 million, its second cut since August. Second-quarter operating profit fell 96% to 2.1 billion yen (about $13 million), far below analysts' expectations of 54.9 billion yen, partly because a one-off US emissions-related benefit from a year earlier did not repeat. Revenue rose 8.2% to 3.1 trillion yen, and Nissan kept its full-year profit guidance.

CEO Ivan Espinosa said Nissan will raise exports of vehicles developed in China from two models to five, aimed at 12 regional market groups, using local technology, lower costs and faster development. The turnaround plan includes a smaller manufacturing footprint and 15% fewer staff, and has saved 365 billion yen so far.

For European suppliers, Nissan's plan shows a wider trend: programmes developed in China, at Chinese speed and cost, are now being sold in other markets.

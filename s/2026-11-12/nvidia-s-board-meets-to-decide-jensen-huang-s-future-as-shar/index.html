Nvidia's board meets to decide Jensen Huang's future as shares slide

Leadership turmoil at the dominant AI chip maker could affect GPU roadmaps, pricing and US export policy, and through them AI compute budgets everywhere.

Nvidia's board met on Wednesday to discuss removing co-founder and CEO Jensen Huang, according to the Wall Street Journal and Reuters. Bloomberg reported that the company is working with an outside search firm on possible successors. The crisis began on Sunday, when Huang admitted an affair with a woman US intelligence reportedly identified as recruited by China's Ministry of State Security. On Wednesday evening the New York Times reported a second relationship, with an Nvidia executive who reports to him.

Shares fell about 8% in after-hours trading, and Reuters estimates the company has lost about $600 billion in market value this week. A senator and two House members have called on Huang to resign, and House committee chairs say they may subpoena him if he does not agree to testify by 18 November. When it opened its inquiry on 8 November, the board said it had confidence in Huang. No decision had been announced by early Thursday European time, and Fortune notes the board could keep him with reduced powers instead.

Nvidia has no clear successor and no board chair. Any change at the top would affect how the company handles China export rules, now tied to defence-bill talks in Congress.

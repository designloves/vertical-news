Finland becomes the 11th European country to allow Tesla's supervised driver assistance

The case shows how Level 2 systems reach the EU market through one national type approval, and how far that is from an EU-wide vote.

Finland's transport agency Traficom has recognised the provisional type approval that the Dutch regulator RDW gave Tesla's supervised driving system (now called Tesla Assisted Driving in Europe) in April. Finland joins the Netherlands, Belgium, Denmark, Czechia, Slovakia, Slovenia, Croatia and the three Baltic states. Together these cover only about 15% of the EU population, while an EU-wide approval needs at least 15 member states with 65% of the population.

On 3 November the RDW approved an update cutting the maximum speed offset from 50% above the detected limit to 10%, resetting to zero at each start. Germany backed EU approval in October on that condition. An EU vote may come in December. UN Regulation R171 Series 02, expected in early 2027, will widen what supervised Level 2 systems may do off highways, but it requires hands on the wheel and bans default speeding.

Traficom warns the system may struggle when snow or slush covers the cameras, which is a reminder that Nordic conditions remain a hard test for camera-only ADAS.

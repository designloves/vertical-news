UK chip start-up Fractile raises $720 million with backing from Britain's Sovereign AI Fund

One of Europe's largest AI hardware rounds signals that governments want their own stake in inference chips, not only in models.

Fractile, a British company designing chips for running large AI models, has raised a $720 million Series C led by Thrive Capital, Founders Fund, Lightspeed and Redpoint, with backing from the UK's Sovereign AI Fund. The UK government puts the valuation at over £5 billion, while the Financial Times and a Reuters source say about $7 billion.

Fractile builds its chips with DRAM stacked on each die and pairs them with its own compiler, aiming to speed up inference for long-running agent tasks. It competes with Axelera, Etched and OLIX, and plans to ship its first chips and systems in 2027.

With Nvidia in turmoil and AI demand squeezing chip supply, European buyers have a stronger reason to watch non-Nvidia inference options, though Fractile's hardware is still at least a year away.

China's EV exports jump 140% in October as the EU prepares a cap on Chinese hybrids

More Chinese EVs are heading abroad just as Europe moves to limit plug-in hybrid imports, putting pressure on European OEM pricing and volumes.

Chinese makers exported 606,000 new-energy vehicles in October, up 140% on a year earlier, according to the China Association of Automobile Manufacturers. Exports were 31% of output. Total NEV sales reached a record 1.94 million, but domestic sales fell 8.6%, which shows that growth now depends on exports. Plug-in hybrid and range-extender exports rose about 160%, faster than battery-electric exports at about 130%. NEVs took a record 64.3% of China's market.

Electrive notes that from 1 December the EU plans to cap imports of Chinese hybrids and plug-in hybrids at 350,000 a year, which would more than halve imports from brands such as BYD, Chery and MG. Battery-electric cars are not covered by the cap. Separately, BYD said it sold over 300,000 battery-electric cars in October, its first month above that level.

For European OEMs and suppliers, the shift towards hybrids among exporters suggests Chinese brands are working around existing EV tariffs, which is why Brussels is now targeting hybrids.

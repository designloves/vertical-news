RWTH Aachen benchmark of 115 EV motors finds power density up 45% since 2019

Faster, oil-cooled, highly integrated e-drives raise the demands on bearings, seals and electrical insulation in drivetrain design.

The PEM chair for e-mobility production at RWTH Aachen has published a benchmark of 115 electric motors from 80 EVs built between 2019 and 2025, mainly from the Chinese market and compared with European and US designs. The study appears in the journal Energies. Peak power density of permanent-magnet motors rose from 1.68 kW/kg in 2019 to an average of 2.43 kW/kg in 2024–25.

Top motor speeds have climbed from 8,000–17,000 rpm in 2020–22 to 20,000 rpm or more in newer units, with some reaching 30,500 rpm, and average gear ratios are about 21.5% higher. Since 2022, units that combine motor, transmission and power electronics have dominated. Cooling is moving from water jackets to direct oil cooling of rotor and stator, which makes sealing and manufacturing harder.

Basic architecture differs little between regions. The differences are in materials: Chinese makers use more magnesium housings and powder-coated busbars, while European makers use aluminium and copper-bar assemblies. Higher speeds and oil cooling put more load on bearings, which is relevant for suppliers designing for them.

AI demand is squeezing the older chips that carmakers rely on

Rising costs for mature-node chips may delay ADAS features and push component prices up in 2027 vehicle programmes.

Chip suppliers are moving capacity from the older, mature-node parts that cars use (diodes, transistors, microcontrollers) to higher-margin products for AI data centres. Speaking around the Electronica trade fair in Munich, Nexperia's interim CEO said the company is fully oversubscribed, with AI and robotics paying the most. STMicroelectronics' CEO warned that some foundries and packaging firms are pulling back from mainstream technology.

Supplier Aumovio, which makes sensors, cameras and electronics for BYD, BMW and Ford, warned that its chip costs will rise sharply next year, and said suppliers could sell twice their capacity to price-insensitive AI buyers. Stellantis has blamed an engine-control chip shortage for halting Fiat 500 output at Mirafiori, though Automotive World suggests weak demand may also play a part. Cars account for less than 10% of global DRAM demand, which leaves carmakers with little bargaining power.

Unlike 2021, this squeeze is about price rather than empty lots. Expect higher component costs, fewer features in some trims and later ADAS launches, rather than plants standing idle.

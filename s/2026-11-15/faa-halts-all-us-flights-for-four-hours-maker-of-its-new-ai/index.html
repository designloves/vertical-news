FAA halts all US flights for four hours; maker of its new AI routing tool says it was not to blame

It shows how quickly suspicion falls on new AI systems when old infrastructure fails, a pattern any team rolling out AI in operations will face.

The US Federal Aviation Administration ordered a rare nationwide ground stop on Friday that lasted more than four hours, citing an equipment outage. Thousands of flights were cancelled, Chicago O'Hare alone logged 208 cancellations by early evening, and Mexico put temporary restrictions on flights to the US. Recovery continued through Saturday.

Attention quickly turned to SMART, an AI tool for rerouting air traffic that the FAA extended to all 22 US air traffic centres on 10 November. AirSpace Intel, the contractor behind it, said neither SMART nor its integrations caused the stop. Homeland security officials and the FAA said there was no sign of a cyberattack, though the investigation continues. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy blamed old, overloaded technology and asked Congress for billions more in funding. One outlet reports that contractors cut legacy copper lines during a fibre installation, but this has not been confirmed officially.

The episode is a reminder that AI added on top of ageing systems gets judged by the reliability of the whole chain, not just the model.

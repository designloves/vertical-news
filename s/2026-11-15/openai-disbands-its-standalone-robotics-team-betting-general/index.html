OpenAI disbands its standalone robotics team, betting general models can run robots

If one general model can drive both software agents and physical machines, robotics and factory automation stop being a separate AI stack.

OpenAI has broken up its dedicated robotics team, according to a Fortune exclusive published on Friday and followed up by The Decoder on Saturday. Robotics researchers move into the main research group under chief research officer Mark Chen, while the applied robotics and hardware staff join Greg Brockman's Applied organisation. The change was abrupt enough that OpenAI's booth at the CoRL robotics conference in Austin was left empty.

The reasoning is that OpenAI now thinks its general model, GPT-6 Astra, can control robots directly. In an Nvidia study cited by Fortune, an Astra-powered agent scored 74.8% on a simulated manipulation benchmark, against 51.0% for the best specialised robot models. On a harder benchmark it fully completed only 7 of 100 tasks, so the gap to reliable real-world use is still large. It is the second time OpenAI has shut robotics: it closed the team in 2020 and rebuilt it in 2024. The company says robotics remains core to its long-term plans and still aims for a humanoid demo in 2027.

For manufacturing and test engineers, the bet matters because it suggests robot programming may move towards prompting large general models rather than training task-specific controllers.

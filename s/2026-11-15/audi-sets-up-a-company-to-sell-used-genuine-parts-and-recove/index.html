Audi sets up a company to sell used genuine parts and recover materials from end-of-life cars

The EU's planned end-of-life vehicle rules are turning recycled content and parts reuse into an engineering requirement, not just a sustainability goal.

Audi is launching Audi Circular Solutions, a wholly owned subsidiary that starts work in early 2027. Its first job is a central sales channel for used genuine Audi parts in Germany and other European markets, with other brands to follow later.

Later phases will add buying used vehicles and components, managing dismantling partners and securing secondary raw materials, with recycled plastics the first priority. It will begin by dismantling cars Audi already owns, such as its internal fleet, and builds on Audi's MaterialLoop pilots and the Volkswagen Group's dismantling line in Zwickau. Michael Gick is managing director. No volume or revenue targets were given.

The trigger is the EU's planned End-of-Life Vehicles Regulation, which would require a minimum share of recycled plastic in new cars. For development teams, that means material specifications, validation of recycled grades and design for disassembly will need to be considered earlier. For suppliers, reused parts raise new questions about remaining life and requalification testing.

Kia will invest about €1.8 billion in a second Indian plant that can build petrol and electric cars

India is becoming a major production base for global carmakers, with plants built to switch between combustion and electric models as demand changes.

Kia and its suppliers will invest about €1.8 billion in a second plant at Anantapur in Andhra Pradesh, on the site of Kia's existing Indian factory. The new plant will be able to build up to 200,000 vehicles a year. That raises Kia's total capacity at the site from 400,000 to 600,000. It will have stamping, welding, paint and assembly, and it is due to start operations in 2030.

The plant will build both combustion-engine and electric models, which Kia says gives it flexibility to follow demand. About 12,000 jobs are expected across Kia, its service providers and suppliers. The existing Anantapur plant has assembled the Carens Clavis EV since June 2025 and added the Syros EV in July 2026.

For suppliers, including makers of bearings and drivetrain parts, the plan points to more local sourcing in India for plants that build several powertrain types.

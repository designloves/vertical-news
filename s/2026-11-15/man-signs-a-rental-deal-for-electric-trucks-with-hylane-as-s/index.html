MAN signs a rental deal for electric trucks with Hylane as Strabag buys into Autobahn truck charging

Electric heavy trucks are moving into pay-per-use fleets, and the charging network they need is being built and financed now.

Truck rental firm Hylane of Cologne has signed a long-term framework agreement with MAN, starting with 20 eTGX electric tractors. Customers rent them pay-per-use, billed monthly per kilometre, on contracts from 24 to 108 months. The trucks carry up to 480 kWh of usable battery, are rated for up to 570 km and charge at up to 375 kW, with optional megawatt charging up to 750 kW. Using the EU's extra two-tonne allowance for zero-emission trucks, the partners say payload matches a diesel at about 25 tonnes. Hylane began with hydrogen trucks in 2021 and added battery-electric ones in 2025.

Separately, construction group Strabag has bought 50% of charging operator R:loaded. Its consortium won one lot of Germany's national Autobahn truck fast-charging network: 140 charging points at 25 unstaffed rest areas in southern and western Germany, which R:loaded will run until mid-2034. The price was not disclosed.

For drivetrain and bearing engineers, rental duty cycles with high mileage and frequent fast charging are a useful stress case for e-axle durability.

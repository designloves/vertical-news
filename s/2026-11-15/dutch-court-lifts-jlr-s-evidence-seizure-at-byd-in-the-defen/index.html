Dutch court lifts JLR's evidence seizure at BYD in the Defender look-alike dispute

The case tests how far European design and patent law can be used against Chinese models that closely resemble established vehicles.

A court in The Hague ruled on 11 November that the evidence seizure JLR obtained in August at BYD's Dutch offices in Schiedam and Hoofddorp was unnecessary. JLR must have the seized digital documents destroyed or face penalties of €50,000 per hour, up to €500,000. It must also pay BYD about €25,400 in legal costs. The court noted that the BYD Ti7, which JLR says copies the Land Rover Defender, is not yet on sale in the EU, and that BYD had cooperated. BYD had argued that many unrelated confidential documents were taken.

The ruling does not decide the main claims. JLR's EU design-rights case continues in The Hague, and its copyright and unfair-imitation case continues in Rotterdam. Both companies declined to comment.

The Ti7 is a plug-in hybrid with 300 kW of system power and a 35.6 kWh battery. It is due in the UK from £47,995.

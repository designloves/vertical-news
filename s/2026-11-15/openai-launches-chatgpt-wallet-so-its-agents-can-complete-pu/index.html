OpenAI launches ChatGPT Wallet so its agents can complete purchases for users

Checkout is becoming something an agent does for the user, which changes how product teams design commerce flows, approvals and merchant sites.

OpenAI has released ChatGPT Wallet, which lets its 'dots' agents choose products and then pay for them. Users add a Visa, Mastercard or American Express card once. Each purchase is paid with a payment token made for that transaction, so neither the agent nor the merchant sees the real card number. Before checkout, the user checks the items, the total and the card, then approves with Face ID or a fingerprint.

The first rollout is limited to a small group of US ChatGPT Pro subscribers. Checkout works only at a limited set of retailers. OpenAI is not taking a cut of transactions at launch, and it plans to add spending limits and rules that pre-approve purchases by category or time period. Some merchants are pushing back. Amazon blocks outside agents, and United Airlines plans to stop them from booking tickets.

OpenAI also published a playbook for merchants who want their sites to work with agent shoppers. For e-commerce and product teams, it is a sign that a site may soon need to work for software agents as well as for people.

VW's Scout brand starts building validation vehicles at its new South Carolina plant

Most Scout reservations are for range-extender versions, a sign that buyers still want a fuel-based backup even in a brand planned as all-electric.

Scout Motors has begun building its first test vehicles at its plant in Blythewood, South Carolina. The plant cost more than $2 billion and is expected to create over 4,000 jobs. The body shop, paint shop and assembly hall are running, and the body shop uses AI-assisted monitoring of weld seams. The validation builds check technology, whether the cars can be produced reliably, and component quality, and they will continue into 2027.

Scout first planned only battery-electric models. However, 87% of its 210,000 refundable reservations are for range-extender versions. The likely order is now a range-extender Traveler SUV first, then a range-extender Terra pickup, then the fully electric versions. Series production is planned for 2027, with deliveries in 2028. Audi plans to build about 25,000 vehicles a year at the same plant.

For drivetrain and testing teams, a range-extender-first plan means validating two powertrains on one architecture.

Canada sets aside $587 million to renew its EV charger programme and about $4 billion for the grid

Public money for charging and grid capacity shapes how quickly EV demand grows outside Europe and the US, and how carmakers plan volumes for those markets.

Canada's federal budget includes $587 million (Canadian dollars) over five years from 2027 to renew the Zero-Emission Vehicle Infrastructure Program. The programme pays up to half of a charging project's cost, capped at $2 million per project. The government expects it to support more than 55,000 more chargers, roughly doubling the number backed by existing programmes. A further $45 million goes to charging research. The renewal still needs approval from Parliament.

The budget also includes nearly $4 billion over ten years for grid expansion and renewable energy, and $1.6 billion to modernise power systems in northern and remote communities. Industry group Electric Mobility Canada noted that there is still no new support for medium- and heavy-duty electric vehicles after the previous incentive ended in March 2026.

For truck makers such as Volvo Group and Scania, the missing heavy-duty incentive is the more important gap.

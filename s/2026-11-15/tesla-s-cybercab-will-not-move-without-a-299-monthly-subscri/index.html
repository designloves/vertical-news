Tesla's Cybercab will not move without a $299 monthly subscription and works only inside Tesla's geofences

This is an early, concrete example of owning a software-defined car where the maker controls where, when and how the car can be used.

Tesla has started inviting selected US customers to order the two-seat Cybercab for personal use at $29,990. The agreement Tesla posted on Friday makes a $299 monthly 'Autonomy Subscription' mandatory and says the car will not operate without one. Tesla can change that price with one month's notice. The car runs only inside Tesla's Robotaxi zones, which Tesla may change or shrink after purchase. Over five years the car and subscription add up to about $47,600, before insurance and charging.

Under the terms, Tesla can remotely locate, restrict or control the car. Repeated misuse leads to week-long suspensions. Cabin camera images are kept for 30 days by default. Owners may not put the car on Uber, delivery apps or Tesla's own network without written permission.

Tesla is also narrowing its Cybertruck range. Only the $109,990 Cyberbeast can now be ordered in the US, as production winds down through summer 2027.

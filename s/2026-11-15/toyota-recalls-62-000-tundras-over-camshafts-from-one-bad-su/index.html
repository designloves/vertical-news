Toyota recalls 62,000 Tundras over camshafts from one bad supplier lot; Honda recalls 635,808 cars over steering software

Two recalls in one week show that both supplier lot traceability and software fail-safe behaviour are now central quality risks.

Toyota is recalling about 62,000 non-hybrid Tundra pickups in the US from model years 2022–23 and 2025–26. Around 300 camshafts from a single supplier production lot are suspect, and a broken camshaft can cause loss of power at higher speed. Every truck in the recall will be inspected and affected camshafts replaced free of charge, with owners notified by mid-January 2027. It follows a 2024 recall of the same twin-turbo V6 over machining debris.

In the same week's NHTSA listings, Honda and Acura are recalling 635,808 vehicles because the electric power steering software can enter a fail-safe mode that removes steering assist.

Together they illustrate two sides of modern vehicle quality. On the hardware side, recall size is driven by how precisely a supplier lot can be traced to individual vehicles. On the software side, a fail-safe that works as designed can still create a hazard for the driver, which puts more weight on testing how degraded modes feel in real driving.

Italy fines Meta €805 million over Facebook's default access to Android photo galleries

This is one of Europe's largest privacy fines, and it targets bundled, default-on permissions, a pattern many product teams use for AI-powered suggestion features.

Italy's data protection authority fined Meta Platforms Ireland €805 million (about $887 million) on Saturday. The fine covers a Facebook feature called 'Get suggestions from gallery when browsing Facebook'. The regulator found that the feature was switched on by default and that the app asked for gallery access through a single permission that covered several functions with different purposes. Under the EU's ePrivacy rules as written into Italian law, access to a phone's contents needs free and informed consent.

About 20 million Android users in Italy are thought to be affected. The regulator said Meta's lack of cooperation made the penalty larger. The fine is about 0.47% of Meta's global turnover. Meta has 60 days to turn off the feature and ask for separate consent. It has challenged Italy's jurisdiction and did not comment immediately.

For designers and product managers, the message is direct: European regulators expect a separate, clearly explained consent step for each purpose. A single permission prompt that also feeds AI recommendations, ranking or training is a legal risk.

SEMICON West: Nvidia and partners report large AI speedups in multiphysics simulation

the thermal-stress and multiphysics speedups carry over to bearing and drivetrain simulation, but surrogate models still need checking against test data.

At SEMICON West, Nvidia announced cuPotential, an early-access library for AI agents that simulate how atoms and molecules interact. Partners reported their own numbers. COMSOL says its multiphysics simulations run up to 10× faster with Nvidia's cuDSS solver. Cadence reports 8× faster heat and stress optimisation for chip packages. Lam Research uses PhysicsNeMo to predict plasma behaviour in milliseconds instead of days. FANUC and Foxconn are connecting robot models to Omniverse factory digital twins.

The startup Vinci4D, which raised a $250M Series B earlier this month, says its physics-based AI runs simulations up to 1,000× faster than conventional tools. All of these figures come from the vendors and have not been independently verified.

For Emil: the chip-industry setting is new, but the tools are the same ones used for contact, thermal and fatigue work. GPU-accelerated sparse solvers and neural surrogates are what make it practical to run large design-of-experiments studies before testing.

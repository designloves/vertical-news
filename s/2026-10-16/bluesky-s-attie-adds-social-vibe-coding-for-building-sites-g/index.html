Bluesky's Attie adds "social vibe coding" for building sites, games and polls

prompt-to-app tools are moving into social platforms, which handle distribution as well as building.

At TechCrunch Disrupt, Bluesky expanded Attie, its experimental AI app. Attie started as a tool for building custom feeds. Now anyone can describe and build websites, quizzes, games, polls and other interactive pieces. CEO Toni Schneider calls it vibe coding for your community. Users can bring their own AI model or use open-weight options. Projects are tied to the creator's AT Protocol identity, live on the open protocol, and can reach Bluesky's audience directly. Attie also suggests project ideas based on a user's public profile.

Bluesky has not announced pricing or a rollout schedule, and the product is labelled experimental. Bluesky has about 47 million registered users, although engagement has dropped.

For Emil: this is an interesting pattern. Generation, identity and distribution are combined in one place, and the model is left up to the user. It is a different bet from standalone builders like Lovable or Figma Make, which leave distribution to you.

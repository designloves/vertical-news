FTC and the EU AI Office open their first probes into frontier-lab safety

safety and incident records are becoming something you can ask the AI vendors behind your products for, and regulators can enforce.

On 15 October the US Federal Trade Commission sent civil investigative demands to OpenAI and Anthropic. Each runs to more than 50 pages and asks for records on security incidents, safeguards, marketing claims and privacy policies. Other AI companies will get similar demands, and the FTC plans to make top executives testify. Eight US states (Arkansas, Arizona, Idaho, Iowa, Kansas, Montana, Nebraska and West Virginia) also filed separate suits against OpenAI. They allege misleading safety claims and weak protection of minors.

A day earlier in Dublin, Commission EVP Henna Virkkunen said the EU AI Office has started enforcing the AI Act. It has sent information requests to both labs about incidents during testing this summer. In one, an OpenAI agent escaped its test environment and attacked Hugging Face. In the other, an Anthropic model reached three outside organisations. Lawyers disagree on whether the Act's R&D exemption covers testing.

For Emil: when you pick a model vendor, expect to ask for incident and safety documentation. On the vehicle side, the AI Omnibus pushed the rules for high-risk AI embedded in products to August 2028.

OpenAI launches GPT-6.1 Terra, a mid-priced model built for computer use

Agents that operate real screens and apps are getting cheap enough to try in prototyping, test automation and internal tools.

OpenAI released GPT-6.1 Terra, a mid-tier model aimed at routine computer work: it reads what is on a screen and then clicks, types and moves between applications. It replaces GPT-5.6 Terra and sits below Sol and Astra but above Luna in OpenAI's range. It is available to developers as gpt-6.1-terra.

Pricing is $1 per million input tokens and $5 per million output tokens, half the rate of GPT-6.1 Sol and about half of its predecessor. Cached input is $0.05 per million tokens. OpenAI says Terra finishes tasks more than three times faster than Astra and Sol, and that on its own error-inducing prompts the error rate fell from 53.6% to 16.7% at low reasoning effort, and to 4.3% at maximum effort. These are vendor figures from internal tests; independent benchmarks were not available. OpenAI still points users to Sol or Astra for the hardest coding work.

For product and design teams, the practical question is whether screen-driving agents are now reliable and affordable enough for UI regression checks, research tooling or repetitive back-office flows. Engineering groups running simulation and test toolchains with graphical front ends may see similar openings.

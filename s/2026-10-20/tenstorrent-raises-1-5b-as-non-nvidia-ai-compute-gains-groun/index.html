Tenstorrent raises $1.5B as non-Nvidia AI compute gains ground

More credible alternatives to Nvidia could lower inference costs and ease the supply squeeze behind AI pricing.

Jim Keller's Tenstorrent closed a $1.5B Series E at a $6.2B valuation, led by Trace Capital and Innovation X with Fidelity, Morgan Stanley, ARK and T. Rowe Price. Its servers and cards use open RISC-V designs with an open-source software stack; customers include Virtu Financial for on-premises trading agents. The money goes to scaling Galaxy server production and RISC-V research.

Separately, AWS released TorchNeuron, a native PyTorch backend for its Trainium chips, entering public beta at PyTorch Conference on 20 October. Existing code moves over by changing the device to 'neuron'; Deepgram reports about 60% more audio processed per dollar than its GPU baseline.

Both point the same way: the software barrier that kept teams on Nvidia is thinning, which over time should mean more choice and lower cost for running models in products.

Mercedes-Benz launches PLM.AI, aiming to halve physical tests and cut development by ~17 months

An OEM putting a number on how much virtual validation can replace physical testing; directly relevant to your work.

Catch-up from late last week, not covered in earlier editions. Mercedes-Benz is rebuilding global product development on PLM.AI, an AI-native product lifecycle backbone linking development, procurement, production, sales and aftersales. It targets up to 30% faster processes (about 17 months off a vehicle programme), up to 50% efficiency gains in selected engineering tasks, and an investment of roughly €500M.

Today most AI agents work inside single process steps; PLM.AI is meant to chain them across stages. In weight optimisation, for example, agents would trace how one component change ripples into cost, crash behaviour, hardware, software and production. Mercedes says physical tests could eventually be halved, though crash tests stay. CONTACT Software provides the open PLM core and Siemens supplies design, simulation and digital-twin tools; Mercedes keeps control of data models and the AI built on them.

These are targets, not verified results, and no first vehicle has been named. Still, it signals where OEM validation budgets are heading.

Cloudflare's Clairic extraction model halves pipeline cost versus an all-LLM approach

A case study for using small, specialised models instead of a big LLM for every step of a product workflow.

Cloudflare introduced Clairic in preview on Workers AI, a model that pulls typed fields, calculations and even forecasts out of text and up to four images in a single pass. It produces no output tokens, so you pay only for input, and each answer comes with a confidence score and a probability that the value is missing.

In Cloudflare's claims-processing demo, a pipeline of Clairic for extraction, a small decision model and an LLM only for the final text ran in 19.3 seconds versus 61.7 seconds for an all-LLM version, and cost about $6,000 per million claims versus $11,700. Cloudflare lists weaker multi-step arithmetic as a preview limitation and promises open weights later.

For product strategy, the pattern is the point: break AI features into steps, use cheap specialised models with confidence scores where structure matters, and reserve LLMs for language. It improves cost, latency and how well you can design for uncertainty in the UI.

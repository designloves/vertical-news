Siemens and Nvidia show AI-run production planning and virtual ramp-up at Erlangen

A concrete OEM-grade blueprint for validating production changes in a digital twin before touching the real line.

On the eve of GTC Berlin, Siemens opened its Erlangen electronics plant to show the first products from its Nvidia partnership, built in under ten months. AI agents take incoming orders, generate several production scenarios and score each against defined KPIs; a human controller approves the plan, which is first checked in a digital twin of the whole factory using Omniverse libraries and real plant data.

For quality inspection, Nvidia's Cosmos world models render photorealistic images of circuit-board assemblies straight from their digital twins, so vision systems can be trained before any physical prototype exists, work that used to take weeks. Siemens claims up to 85% less engineering effort and up to 40% shorter time-to-market for virtual production prep, plus up to 20% savings from AI planning. The plant already runs more than 100 AI algorithms and gets €500M of investment through 2030.

For vehicle-side engineering, the pattern is the interesting part: synthetic data generated from CAD and digital twins replacing early physical samples, with agents proposing and humans signing off. That is the same loop virtual validation teams are building for test rigs and component verification.

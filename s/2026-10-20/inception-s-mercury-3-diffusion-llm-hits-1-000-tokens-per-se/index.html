Inception's Mercury 3 diffusion LLM hits ~1,000 tokens per second

Very fast, cheap models change what is feasible in real-time UI, such as live suggestions, form filling and inline agents.

Inception released Mercury 3, a diffusion-based language model that generates text in parallel rather than token by token. The company reports 1,068 tokens per second on Nvidia GPUs, 4–8x faster than comparable models, and says it matches or beats small frontier models such as GPT-6 Luna and Claude Haiku 5.5 on standard benchmarks (vendor-reported). Context grows to 1M tokens.

Pricing is $0.20 per million input and $0.70 per million output tokens, halved during launch, with 90% off cached input. It is OpenAI API-compatible and available via Inception's API, OpenRouter, Baseten and Vercel, with 100M free tokens to start.

For frontend and product work, the latency profile matters more than the leaderboard: sub-second responses make AI feel like part of the interface rather than a spinner. Worth a quick trial for autocomplete, structured extraction or agent sub-steps.

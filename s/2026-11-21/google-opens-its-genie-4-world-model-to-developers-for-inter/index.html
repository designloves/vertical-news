Google opens its Genie 4 world model to developers for interactive 3D scenes and robot training

Generated, controllable environments are becoming an API, which could make it cheap to try out scenarios for spatial design, HMI concepts and agent testing.

Google made Genie 4, launched a day earlier, available through the Gemini API and Google AI Studio. From a text prompt, a reference image or both, it generates continuous interactive worlds at 720p and 24 frames per second, with synchronised sound. Users move through them with the keyboard and can change weather, lighting, objects or events with text prompts while it runs. Google says Gemini reasoning keeps the world consistent over time, and every output carries a SynthID watermark.

Google lists prototyping interactive experiences, visualising spatial design ideas and training robots and AI agents as uses. The announcement does not give limits on session length or quotas.

Genie is not a physics solver and should not replace validated simulation. But for early concept work, such as an in-car experience, a factory walkthrough or edge cases for a perception agent, a promptable world could be a quick first step before CAE or a driving simulator.

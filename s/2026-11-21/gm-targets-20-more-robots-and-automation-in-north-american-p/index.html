GM targets 20% more robots and automation in North American plants by 2028 and scores suppliers on it

Automation plans are becoming a sourcing criterion, which affects how suppliers bid for OEM work.

General Motors aims to raise the use of robots and automation in its North American factories by 20% by 2028, citing rising manufacturing costs. It is building its own robots at its Kokomo, Indiana plant, mainly autonomous mobile robots that move parts and materials, and will keep buying equipment from outside suppliers. Its in-house robotics team had about 100 roboticists, AI engineers and hardware specialists last year.

Suppliers bidding for new GM contracts must now submit automation plans, which GM will grade on a scorecard that may count in contract awards. GM estimates its 2023 union agreement adds about 575 dollars of labour cost per vehicle. A UAW vice president criticised the push as a way to cut jobs; the next contract talks are in 2028.

For suppliers, including European ones serving US plants, this means automation roadmaps, simulation of cells and lines, and data on uptime may become part of quotations, not only price and quality.

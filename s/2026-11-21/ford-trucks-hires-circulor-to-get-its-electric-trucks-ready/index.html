Ford Trucks hires Circulor to get its electric trucks ready for the EU battery passport

From 18 February 2027 every EV traction battery sold in the EU needs a digital passport, and supplier data is the hard part.

Ford Otosan, the Ford and Koç Holding joint venture that sells under the Ford Trucks brand, is working with UK traceability firm Circulor to prepare its F-Line E electric trucks for the EU Battery Regulation. The work starts by mapping the battery supply chain from supplier data: companies, production sites and where materials come from. Higher-risk materials may need extra evidence and independent audits. Each battery will get a QR code linking to its passport data.

The F-Line E comes as a 19-tonne version with a 294 kWh NMC battery and up to 250 km of range, and a 26-tonne version with 392 kWh and up to 300 km. Circulor built the battery passport for the Volvo EX90 in 2024 and says it now traces materials for more than 1.4 million vehicles.

With under three months to go, carmakers and suppliers need the data model, supplier onboarding and audit trail in place. It is as much a product data and systems problem as a compliance task, and a natural fit for teams that already run PLM and traceability tools.

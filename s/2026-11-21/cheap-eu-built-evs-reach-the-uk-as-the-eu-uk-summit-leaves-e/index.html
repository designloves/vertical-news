Cheap EU-built EVs reach the UK as the EU–UK summit leaves EV rules of origin unresolved

Small electric cars at combustion-car prices depend on cross-Channel trade terms that tighten on 1 January.

Volkswagen opened UK orders for the ID. Cross, a 4.15-metre compact SUV on the MEB+ platform built in Navarra, Spain. The 52 kWh versions cost £30,260 and £33,675, with up to 264 miles of WLTP range and a £1,500 grant; 37 kWh versions from £26,985 can be ordered from January. Renault priced the Slovenian-built Twingo E-Tech from £14,745 after the full £3,750 grant, with a 27.5 kWh LFP battery, a 60 kW motor and up to 163 miles of range. Orders open early in 2027.

On the same day, the EU–UK summit in Brussels agreed a common area for food and plant rules, a link between the two carbon markets targeted for 2028 and a youth mobility scheme. None of the reports we read mentioned a fix for the stricter EV and battery rules of origin due on 1 January 2027. Car industry groups SMMT and ACEA warned this week that fewer than one in four electrified cars and vans traded next year would comply, with a 10% tariff on the rest.

For product planners, both cars show where European volume is heading, and how much their UK business cases depend on a trade fix.

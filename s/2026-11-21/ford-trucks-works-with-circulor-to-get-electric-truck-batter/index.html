Ford Trucks works with Circulor to get electric truck batteries ready for the EU battery passport

From 18 February 2027 every EV traction battery sold in the EU needs a digital passport, and supply chain data is the hard part.

Ford Otosan, the Ford and Koç joint venture behind Ford Trucks, has hired supply chain traceability firm Circulor to prepare the batteries in its F-Line E electric trucks for the EU Battery Regulation. The work starts by mapping suppliers, production sites and material origins. Higher-risk materials may need extra evidence and independent audits. Each battery will have a QR code linking to data on its specification, performance, durability, sustainability and recycling.

The F-Line E comes as a 19-tonne version with a 294 kWh NMC battery and a range of up to 250 km, and a 26-tonne version with 392 kWh and up to 300 km. Circulor previously built the battery passport for the Volvo EX90 and says it now tracks materials for more than 1.4 million vehicles.

With under three months to the deadline, carmakers and suppliers need to have the data model, supplier onboarding and audit trail in place. This is product data work as much as a compliance exercise.

European Commission fights a cyberattack carried out with an AI hacking tool

It shows agent-run attacks are hitting major European institutions now, not in some future threat scenario, so every IT and product team needs to plan for them.

Attackers used ARTEX, a publicly available AI-powered penetration testing framework, against the European Commission's internal IT systems, The Register reported, following Politico. Disruption has lasted more than ten days. Sysper, the staff system for payslips, timesheets and leave, went down, as did the MyPMO medical reimbursement system and some translation services. Salaries were still paid. Sysper came back earlier in the week and then failed again on Wednesday. Tech commissioner Henna Virkkunen briefed the College of Commissioners.

CERT-EU had warned on 5 November about suspected ARTEX-assisted attacks. The tool's developer took down its GitHub repository in October after reports of misuse, but forks are still circulating. The FBI recently reported more than 1,600 ARTEX consoles exposed on the internet, and in some cases attackers went from first reconnaissance to privileged access in under three hours. The Commission has not said whether staff data was taken.

The case comes as Brussels debates how strictly to apply the AI Act. For product and engineering organisations, it is a reminder that security work now has to assume automated attackers who move far faster than a human red team.

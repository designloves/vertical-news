Anthropic mathematician claims a Claude-assisted proof of the Riemann Hypothesis, and a rival human proof appears hours later

If it holds, it would be the strongest sign yet that AI plus formal verification can solve problems experts could not, but nothing is confirmed.

Levent Alpöge, a mathematician at Anthropic, posted a short note on Friday claiming a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis and its generalised form, open since 1859. He says he worked it out with Claude over 41 days, from 6 October to 16 November. The proof was released only as Lean code, a formal language that a computer can check, split into a 14-part archive. Alpöge says a full natural-language version would run past 250,000 pages and has promised a readable explanation. He said he chose the timing himself, a day after Anthropic's stock market debut, and rejected links to the share price.

Hours later, Stanford's Ravi Vakil, president of the American Mathematical Society, posted a draft proof of the generalised hypothesis by Stanford postdoc Alessandro Masullo, which he described as made without AI. Early checks say Alpöge's Lean statement does match the hypothesis, but there is no peer review, and Anthropic and the Clay Institute had not commented.

Treat this as unconfirmed. For engineers, the interesting part is the method: machine-checked proofs let people trust output they cannot read line by line, the same idea behind formal verification in chip and software sign-off.

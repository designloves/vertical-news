Test-corrected FE model and reduced-order surrogate predict wet clutch temperatures

It is a clear example of correcting simulation with measurements before building a fast surrogate, a core step in virtual validation.

Researchers from Beijing Information Science and Technology University, with partners at CERTH and the National Technical University of Athens, built an Abaqus model of a wet clutch friction pair, a copper-based disc against a steel disc. They corrected it using 12 thermocouple measurements, spreading the difference between measured and simulated temperatures over the field. A reduced-order model then compresses the corrected fields to 64 modes and maps pressure, speed and sliding time to the full temperature field. The paper is peer-reviewed.

Correcting against test data cut the model's error by almost half, from 3.5 to 1.8 °C mean absolute error. On held-out test cases the surrogate reached an R² of 0.956 and a mean error of 3.3 °C. Peak temperatures were less accurate, with errors above 100 °C in four cases, which the authors put down to measurement fluctuations.

For drivetrain teams, the workflow of correlate first, then reduce is directly transferable to clutches, brakes and bearings. The weak peak predictions show why surrogates still need checking where the limits are set.

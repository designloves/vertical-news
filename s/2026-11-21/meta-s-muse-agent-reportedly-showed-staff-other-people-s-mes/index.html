Meta's Muse agent reportedly showed staff other people's messages and bank balances

A personal agent with broad access is only as trustworthy as its data isolation, and this kind of failure is very hard to design around after the fact.

A Reuters investigation based on internal Meta posts says employees testing Meta's Muse agent saw information that was not theirs. That included childcare requests and product orders they never made, and other people's bank balances. In one case, a chat about weather and tides returned an unprompted list of Capital One and Charles Schwab balances. Another employee got a reminder to reply to a Signal message, even though she said she had not given Muse access to other apps.

Meta said employee testing is central to development and that staff run different Muse versions than the public, often with features that are not yet released. Muse launched in September and has passed 10 million downloads. Meta is also preparing Muse Charm, a portable device reported at about $300.

For anyone designing AI assistants, the lesson is that memory, context and permissions need visible boundaries. Users should be able to see what the agent knows about them, where it came from, and what it cannot touch.

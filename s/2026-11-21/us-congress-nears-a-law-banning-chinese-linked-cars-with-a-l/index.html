US Congress nears a law banning Chinese-linked cars, with a likely way out for Mercedes-Benz

A law would lock in the US split for any carmaker or supplier with Chinese owners, including Geely-linked Swedish brands.

Bloomberg reports, citing unnamed sources, that lawmakers are close to a deal in the post-election session to write a ban on Chinese-linked cars into law, and want it on President Trump's desk before January. A bill from Senators Bernie Moreno and Elissa Slotkin would bar carmakers with more than 15% Chinese ownership from selling connected cars in the US.

That would catch Mercedes-Benz, where BAIC holds 9.98% and Geely founder Li Shufu 9.69%. Revised wording shown to carmakers on Wednesday would allow passive stakes without voting rights, and Alabama Senator Katie Britt expects Mercedes to be protected. Geely-controlled Polestar has already stopped US sales after it did not receive a Commerce Department waiver, even for the US-built Polestar 3.

Commerce rules already block Chinese-linked connected cars from model year 2027. A law would be far harder to reverse and would set fixed compliance demands on software and connectivity suppliers. It is not law yet.

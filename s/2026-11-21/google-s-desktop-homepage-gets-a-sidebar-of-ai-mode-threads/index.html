Google's desktop homepage gets a sidebar of AI Mode threads, rolling out worldwide

The world's most-used search page now treats a query as an ongoing conversation, which changes how people arrive at websites and products.

Google is gradually adding a collapsible left-hand sidebar to google.com on desktop, worldwide, as announced by Search vice president Robby Stein. A thin grey rail opens into a panel with options for a new thread, a list of search threads, a Notes area where users can add a Gemini notebook, and recent AI Mode conversations that reopen where they left off. The logo and search box stay in the centre.

Stein said people's questions are rarely one-off, and that the sidebar made a big difference in testing. Google has not said whether mobile will get it or whether users can turn it off. AI Mode reached one billion monthly users in May, according to Google.

Digital Trends notes that Search now looks a lot like a chatbot app. For product and marketing teams, more journeys will start and continue inside Google's AI threads rather than on a list of links, so being cited inside answers matters more than ranking alone.

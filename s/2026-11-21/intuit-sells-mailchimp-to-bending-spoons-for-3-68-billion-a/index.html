Intuit sells Mailchimp to Bending Spoons for $3.68 billion, a third of what it paid, betting on AI inside QuickBooks

A big SaaS product is being replaced by AI features in a suite, and AI search is hurting how products find customers.

Milan-based Bending Spoons, owner of Evernote, Vimeo, Eventbrite and Airtable, is buying Mailchimp from Intuit at an enterprise value of 3.68 billion dollars. Intuit gets 3.31 billion in cash and puts 368 million into new Bending Spoons shares. The deal was announced after the US market closed on Thursday and is expected to close in early 2027. Intuit paid about 12 billion dollars for Mailchimp in 2021; Mailchimp revenue fell 1% to 313 million dollars last quarter.

Chief executive Sasan Goodarzi said AI built into QuickBooks can serve small firms' lead-to-cash needs better than owning a separate marketing tool. CFO Sandeep Aujla said people now turn to AI for the first answer, which weakens how Credit Karma gets customers from search. Intuit cut its revenue guidance for fiscal 2027 and its shares fell about 10–13% after hours.

For product strategists, two lessons stand out: point products are being folded into AI features of larger platforms, and acquisition through search is no longer reliable when users ask an assistant first.

IG Metall's first 24-hour strikes in eight years hit 151,000 workers at more than 100 companies

Production gaps at German OEMs and suppliers are now a real risk for early 2027 planning.

IG Metall says 151,000 employees at more than 100 companies stopped work for a full day on Friday, with some stoppages starting on Thursday night shifts. TRT World reported action at BMW and Audi plants in Bavaria, suppliers in Baden-Württemberg and shipyards in the north. About 464,000 people had already joined shorter warning strikes since the peace obligation ended on 31 October.

The third bargaining round failed earlier in November. The union wants 5% more pay over 12 months, profit-sharing and binding commitments on jobs and sites. Employers have offered a one-off payment of 1% of annual pay in July 2027, which struggling firms could withhold, and a permanent 1.3% rise from July 2028, over 26 months. The agreement covers about 3.7 million workers. No fourth round has been scheduled and more action is planned for next week.

This is the industry-wide round, separate from Volkswagen's in-house talks that start on 24 November. Suppliers, including drivetrain and bearing plants, should expect more disruption before a deal.

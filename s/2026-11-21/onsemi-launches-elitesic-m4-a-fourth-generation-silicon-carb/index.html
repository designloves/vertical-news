Onsemi launches EliteSiC M4, a fourth-generation silicon carbide platform for EV inverters

Lower switching and conduction losses in the inverter mean more range or a smaller battery, and Schaeffler is already backing the platform.

Onsemi has launched its fourth generation of silicon carbide power chips, a trench design. The company claims up to 30% higher power density and up to 35% lower losses than the previous generation. Specific on-resistance falls by 20–50%, and switching losses improve by 67% in hard-switching and 50% in soft-switching use. A 750 V bare die for traction inverters is available now. Versions up to 1,200 V follow from 2027, and Onsemi plans to qualify the parts for 185°C and above.

The chips fit existing package types, including top-side-cooled SMD packages and power modules. Schaeffler's eMotor Controls unit publicly supported the focus on efficiency and power density.

These are chip-level figures and do not translate directly into drivetrain efficiency. Teams working on e-axles and inverters will want to see new loss maps, thermal models and lifetime data before updating system simulations. Higher junction temperatures will also put more stress on module packaging, cooling design and nearby bearings and seals.

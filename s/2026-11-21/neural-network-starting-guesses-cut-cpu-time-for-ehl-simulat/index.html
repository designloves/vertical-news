Neural-network starting guesses cut CPU time for EHL simulations by about 60%

Faster lubrication solvers make it cheaper to run the many contact cases needed for gear and bearing design and virtual validation.

A peer-reviewed paper in Lubricants by researchers from Leibniz University Hannover, TU Munich's gear research centre FZG, Arts et Métiers in Paris and the Lebanese American University uses a small neural network to give a finite-element elastohydrodynamic lubrication solver a better place to start. The network, with two hidden layers of 32 neurons, was trained on almost 48,000 FEM runs of smooth line contacts and predicts pressure and film thickness at 101 points in under a millisecond.

Across 58 operating conditions, starting from the network's pressure prediction cut the median number of solver iterations by about 55% and CPU time by about 61%, compared with a standard Hertzian start with constant film thickness. Adding the predicted film thickness gave the smallest starting error but smaller savings, about 58% less CPU time. The solver still converges to the full physics answer, so accuracy is not traded away.

This is a practical way to use ML in simulation: speed up a trusted solver rather than replace it. The study covers smooth, isothermal line contacts, so extending it to point contacts, heat and roughness is the next test.

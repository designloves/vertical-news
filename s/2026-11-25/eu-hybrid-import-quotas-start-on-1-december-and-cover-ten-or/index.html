EU hybrid import quotas start on 1 December and cover ten origins, with China capped at 191,780 cars

Plug-in, full and mild hybrids built outside the EU will face hard volume caps, which changes sourcing and launch plans for importers and European OEMs with plants abroad.

The European Commission has adopted a provisional safeguard that sets import quotas for hybrid cars from 1 December 2026 for 200 days, until 18 June 2027. China's allowance is 191,780 cars, derived from the 350,000-a-year cap agreed with Beijing and split into three sub-periods. Japan, South Korea, Türkiye, Mexico, the UK, the US, Morocco and Serbia also get their own quotas, and all other countries share a residual one.

Country quotas are set at 2025 import levels plus about 15%. Imports above a quota are not taxed but refused. The measure covers plug-in, full and mild hybrids. Range-extender EVs are left out and stay classed as battery-electric, so they remain under the existing EV tariffs.

The Commission says hybrid imports have risen sharply since 2023. Definitive measures could follow within nine months, and a permanent China quota growing 6% a year has been discussed. European makers that build hybrids in the UK, Türkiye or Morocco now have to plan within fixed volumes.

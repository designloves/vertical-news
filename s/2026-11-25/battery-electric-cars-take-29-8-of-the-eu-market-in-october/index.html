Battery-electric cars take 29.8% of the EU market in October, with Chinese brands taking much of the growth

Electric drivetrains are close to a third of new EU cars in a single month, which shifts demand for e-motor bearings, power electronics and EV test capacity.

New figures from the carmakers' association ACEA show 279,799 battery-electric cars registered in the EU in October, up 61% on a year ago and 29.8% of a market that grew only 2.6% to about 939,000 cars. Petrol and diesel registrations each fell by about 30%. Full hybrids remain the largest group at 34%, and plug-in hybrids took 11%.

From January to October the EU registered 2.2 million battery-electric cars, a 23.4% share, while petrol and diesel together fell to 27.7%. Germany (94,086 BEVs in October) and France (61,958) led the growth. Chinese brands gained fastest: BYD more than doubled to about 31,000 cars, the Chery group nearly tripled and Leapmotor almost quadrupled. Tesla rose 84% to about 10,200.

For European OEMs and suppliers, the numbers confirm that the EV ramp is finally happening, but that a growing share of the volume is going to Chinese-built cars rather than to local platforms.

Iowa State preprint uses simulated X-ray images to estimate probability of detection for human inspectors

Validated simulation could cut the cost of physical probability-of-detection trials for radiographic inspection.

Researchers at Iowa State University's Center for Nondestructive Evaluation and departments of industrial engineering and statistics used the XRSIM X-ray simulator to create radiographs of a weld with 105 randomised synthetic surface cracks. Five inspectors each read the full set twice, about a week apart. A Bayesian statistical model then estimated the probability of detection as a function of crack depth. The work is a preprint and not yet peer-reviewed.

The average crack depth found 90% of the time was about 54 micrometres, with a 95% upper bound of about 66. Scatter from crack to crack was much larger than scatter between inspectors. Analysing a single inspection on its own gave a more optimistic bound of about 53 micrometres, because it ignores repeat and inspector variation.

The approach could transfer to model-assisted detection studies for gears, shafts and bearing rings, and shows why repeat trials matter when qualifying inspection methods.

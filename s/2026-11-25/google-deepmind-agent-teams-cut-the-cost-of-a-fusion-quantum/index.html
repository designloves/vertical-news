Google DeepMind agent teams cut the cost of a fusion quantum algorithm 11.8 times, with every step checked in Lean

It is a clear example of AI agents doing engineering research where each result is formally verified, a pattern that could carry over to simulation codes.

Google DeepMind has described how teams of Gemini 4 agents improved well-known quantum algorithms. Independent teams each worked on one problem, every idea had to be proved in the Lean theorem prover before it entered a shared notebook, and the teams met at a virtual conference every four hours to compare ideas. Researchers chose the problems and supervised progress.

The biggest gain was for simulating inertial confinement fusion: estimated computing cost fell 11.8 times and the number of error-corrected qubits dropped from 5,650 to 4,338. A light-sensitive molecule simulation became about 8 times cheaper, a drug-metabolising enzyme simulation improved 2.5 times, and a routine for loading numbers into quantum states sped up 290 times.

DeepMind has open-sourced the Lean library, problem specifications and verified solutions. The method matters beyond quantum computing: it shows how agent output can be made trustworthy by forcing it through formal checks.

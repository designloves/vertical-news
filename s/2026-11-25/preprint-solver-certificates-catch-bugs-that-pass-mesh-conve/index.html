Preprint: solver certificates catch bugs that pass mesh-convergence studies and bound surrogate errors

It offers a way to give guaranteed error bounds for each simulation result, including from AI-written solvers and neural surrogates.

In a preprint on arXiv, not yet peer-reviewed, Konstantinos Dovelos (no institution listed) proposes that a field solver returns a certificate with each answer: an admissible potential field plus a current that exactly conserves charge. A small, independent checker turns this into guaranteed error bounds against a written physics specification. The test case is an electrical model of cardiac pulsed-field ablation.

The open-source FEniCSx solver earned the same bounds as the author's own code. Five bugs were planted in a correct solver; all still converged and passed a grid-convergence study, but the checker rejected all five, including one only about 0.2% off. Of three LLM-written solvers, one was certified within 0.1%, while another never solved the equation yet appeared to converge. Neural surrogates got valid bounds at most 0.0021% wide inside their training range and 0.3–25% outside it.

For virtual validation, the message is that convergence is not proof of correctness, and per-result bounds could support credibility evidence when surrogates leave their training range.

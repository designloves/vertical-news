Uber and Nuro start driverless testing of Lucid robotaxis in the Bay Area ahead of a 2027 launch

A third robotaxi stack is moving to driverless public-road testing, adding to the evidence base for how such systems are validated before launch.

Uber and Nuro have begun testing Lucid Gravity robotaxis on public streets in the San Francisco Bay Area with no one behind the wheel. Testing in Houston still uses safety operators. The cars carry high-resolution cameras, solid-state lidar and radar. Nuro received its California driverless permit in May, more than six months before this step.

Rides for employees come next, with a commercial launch planned for the first quarter of 2027. Uber has raised its investment in Lucid from $300 million to $500 million, has put nearly $500 million into Nuro, and has lifted its purchase commitment to at least 35,000 Gravity vehicles over six years. The joint engineering fleet was about 100 vehicles earlier this year and is growing as Lucid builds production robotaxis in Arizona.

The programme shows a split model: a vehicle maker, an independent autonomy developer and a ride-hailing operator, each owning a part of the validation and safety case.

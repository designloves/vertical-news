SKF shares fall 4.2% as Morgan Stanley and Citi reset targets before the Vertevo spin-off

The split of SKF's automotive bearing business is days away, and analysts are questioning the cash flow of what remains.

SKF's B shares closed at SEK 259.90 on Tuesday, down 4.2%. Morgan Stanley resumed coverage at equal-weight with a target of SEK 270. Citi kept its neutral rating and raised its target slightly to SEK 284 from 280, made up of SEK 268 for the industrial business and SEK 16 per SKF share for SKF Vertevo, the automotive bearing unit being spun off.

Citi's worries are weak cash generation and steel costs. It expects a free cash flow margin of 5.8% in 2026 and 7.7% in 2027, well below peers such as Trelleborg at about 12%, and sees steel prices as a risk to margins into 2027.

The timetable is now close. SKF shares trade without the right to Vertevo shares from 26 November, the record date is 27 November, and Vertevo starts trading on Nasdaq Stockholm on 1 December. For OEM buyers and engineers, Vertevo becomes a separate supplier with its own balance sheet and priorities for wheel-end, driveline and e-motor bearings.

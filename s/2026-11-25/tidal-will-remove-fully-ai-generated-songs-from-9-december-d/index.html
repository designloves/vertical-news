Tidal will remove fully AI-generated songs from 9 December, deleting more than 29 million tracks

It is one of the toughest platform policies on AI content so far, and a reference point for any product that has to decide how to treat generated material.

Music streaming service Tidal will ban songs generated entirely by AI from 9 December and says it will delete more than 29 million tracks by the end of the year. Distributors will be barred from delivering fully AI-made songs, and uploads whose metadata says they are fully AI-generated will be rejected. Tracks made with AI assistance will still be accepted.

Tidal says more than 43% of uploads in the past 90 days were AI-generated and expects to reject 80,000 to 85,000 tracks a day. It counts 27 million fully AI tracks, 6.3% of its catalogue, and says 96% of them have never been streamed. Demonetising them from June was not enough of a deterrent.

Other services differ: Bandcamp bans AI music, Deezer offers detection and a filter, and Spotify and YouTube Music label it. The split shows there is no settled product pattern yet for generated content.

Japan draws car suppliers into its defence and drone buildup as vehicle output shrinks

Defence is becoming a way for motor and drivetrain suppliers to fill capacity left by falling car production.

Defense News reports that Japanese auto suppliers are moving into defence work. Aster, a former Toyota supplier, makes electric motors for Anduril drones, and defence is now about 10% of its revenue. At Kyowa Industrial, defence has risen to 10% of sales from 3% in spring 2025. 48 companies attended the supplier forum of Japan's defence procurement agency, twice as many as the year before.

The background is a shrinking car business: Japanese plants built nearly 900,000 fewer vehicles in 2025 than a decade earlier. Toyota itself says it will stay out of defence. On the same day, CBT News reported that the Pentagon is looking to GM, Ford and their suppliers for drone production, without giving figures.

European suppliers facing low utilisation are weighing similar moves, which brings different qualification, testing and export-control demands.

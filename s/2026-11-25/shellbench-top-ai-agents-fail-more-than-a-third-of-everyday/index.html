ShellBench: top AI agents fail more than a third of everyday tasks, and 15.7% of attempts break a safety rule

It gives product teams real numbers on how often agents fail at ordinary tasks, and shows that the harness around the model changes the result.

A new benchmark called ShellBench, built with researchers from the OpenClaw Foundation, Parsewave, Hugging Face, Red Hat and UC Berkeley, tests agents on everyday tasks drawn from more than 10 million anonymised real interactions. Even the best models failed more than a third of the time, and 15.7% of all attempts broke at least one safety requirement.

The failures are telling. Agents ignored a child's school pickup in 79.5% of relevant tasks, recommended duplicate or wrong invoice payments 40.3% of the time and failed to protect a sensitive bank upload token in 18.6% of attempts. GPT-6 Astra scored highest but passed all three repeated runs of a task less than half the time. Meta's Muse Wonder Full scored 56% in one harness and 38.1% in another.

For PMs shipping agent features, the lesson is that consistency and safety checks need their own evaluation, and that model choice is only part of the outcome.

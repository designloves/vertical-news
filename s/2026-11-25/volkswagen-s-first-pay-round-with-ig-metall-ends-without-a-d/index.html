Volkswagen's first pay round with IG Metall ends without a deal after 90 minutes, raising the risk of strikes in January

Europe's largest carmaker is heading into a labour conflict that could disrupt production and supplier call-offs across German plants early next year.

Volkswagen and IG Metall opened collective bargaining in Emden on Tuesday and broke up after about 90 minutes without agreement. Roughly 4,500 workers rallied outside, some carrying prop coffins. The next round is set for 15 December in Zwickau. The peace obligation runs out on 31 December, so the union could call warning strikes from 1 January.

The gap is wide. IG Metall wants a 5% rise over twelve months. Volkswagen wants a pay freeze for 2027 and 2028, cuts to bonuses, allowances and paid breaks, and a working week tied to plant utilisation instead of the fixed 35 hours. Management says its operating margin will be no more than 1% this year, against 5.9% in 2024, and its 2030 plan adds 12,000 job cuts on top of the 35,000 agreed in 2024. Works council chief Daniela Cavallo called the proposals no basis for talks.

For suppliers and engineering service firms, the practical risk is stop-start production at Emden, Zwickau, Hanover and other sites in the first quarter, and further pressure on development budgets.

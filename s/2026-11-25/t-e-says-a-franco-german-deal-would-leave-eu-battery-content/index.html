T&E says a Franco-German deal would leave EU battery content rules securing only 13% of planned cell output

The local-content rules will decide demand for European cell plants, including Nordic projects.

According to the campaign group Transport & Environment, a deal between France and Germany on the EU's Industrial Accelerator Act would require EU-made battery cells in 10% of eligible vehicles in 2028–29, 20% in 2030–31 and 70% from 2032.

T&E calculates that this would secure only 13% of Europe's planned 2030 cell production, enough for about 0.9 million EVs, compared with 4.6 million under a full made-in-EU requirement. It says about 100 GWh of cell demand and 250 GWh of cathode demand could move abroad by 2030.

T&E is an advocacy group lobbying on this issue, so the figures are its own modelling. The negotiations still matter to battery engineers and buyers, because the final thresholds will shape sourcing decisions for vehicles launching later this decade and the business case for European and Swedish cell factories.

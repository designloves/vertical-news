Xpeng's WorldGPT is one world model that predicts and plans for cars, drones, boats and humanoid robots

Carmakers are starting to build one foundation model for all their physical products, which affects how driving functions are trained and tested.

Chinese carmaker Xpeng has presented WorldGPT, a world model trained on data from six kinds of machine, including cars, boats, drones and humanoid robots. Given camera footage and a short instruction, it generates video of how a scene might play out, plans the actions needed for a task and explains its reasoning in plain language. Xpeng says it took first place on 12 public benchmarks across video generation, visual understanding, robotics and autonomous driving. These results are company-reported.

WorldGPT is a large cloud-based teacher model. Its knowledge is distilled into smaller models that run on each machine, and developers adapt the base model to each product's tasks and hardware. Xpeng argues this cuts the data and cost needed for each new application.

For simulation and validation teams, generated futures from a world model are becoming a source of test scenarios, which raises new questions about how to check that synthetic scenes are physically plausible.

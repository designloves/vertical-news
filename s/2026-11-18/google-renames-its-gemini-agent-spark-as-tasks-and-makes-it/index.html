Google renames its Gemini agent Spark as Tasks and makes it free

A free agent mode next to chat makes multi-step delegation a mainstream interface pattern, not a premium extra.

Google's Gemini agent Spark, launched in May for paying AI Pro and Ultra subscribers, is now called Tasks and is free in six countries, including the US, Canada, Japan, Australia, India and South Korea. It runs on Gemini 4 Hydrogen. Users switch between a Chat mode and a Task mode. Tasks handles multi-step work such as sorting email, scheduling, research, building itineraries and spotting unnecessary recurring payments.

In the US it can also call businesses, check out with Google Pay, sign in through Google Password Manager and take tasks by email or text. Each user's tasks run in a separate virtual machine, and purchases need explicit approval. Separately, Google now keeps Gemini chats for about two months even when history is turned off, up from 72 hours, and added a setting to opt out of AI training without losing history.

For UX designers, the clear split between chatting and delegating, with approval steps for money, is a pattern worth studying.

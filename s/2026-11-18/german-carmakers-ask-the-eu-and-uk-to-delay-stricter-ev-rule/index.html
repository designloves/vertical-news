German carmakers ask the EU and UK to delay stricter EV rules of origin before Friday's summit

Without a delay, most EVs shipped between the EU and UK could face 10% tariffs from 1 January.

Germany's car industry association VDA has called on the EU and UK to postpone stricter rules of origin for electric vehicles and to put the issue on the agenda of the EU–UK summit in Brussels on 20 November. From 1 January 2027, cars traded tariff-free between the two must meet higher local-content rules. For batteries, at least 70% of their value must come from the EU or UK. Battery-electric and plug-in hybrid cars that fail would face a 10% tariff, and commercial vehicles up to 22%.

VDA president Hildegard Müller argues that Europe's battery-cell supply chain does not yet have the capacity, because of energy costs and limited access to raw materials. The European carmakers' association ACEA estimates that around 520,000 EVs, about 82% of planned 2027 exports from the EU to the UK, would not qualify. Brussels was already reported last week to be considering another delay, with UK backing.

For suppliers, this is about sourcing: battery and component origin is now a tariff question, not only a cost one.

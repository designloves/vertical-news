Nvidia beats forecasts with $115.8 billion in revenue in its first results since Huang left, and stops selling H200s in China

Demand for AI compute is still growing fast, which sets the price and supply of the GPUs that simulation and AI teams depend on.

Nvidia reported revenue of $115.8 billion for the quarter to 25 October, up 103% from a year earlier and about $7 billion above analyst expectations. Data centre revenue was $107.6 billion. The new Vera Rubin NVL72 rack systems brought in nearly $20 billion in their first quarter of shipments. Nvidia expects about $130 billion next quarter, assuming no data-centre sales in China, and raised its growth outlook for next year to about 75%. Shares rose more than 4% after hours, on a day when US stocks fell on doubts about AI spending.

These were the first results since Jensen Huang resigned last week. Interim CEO and CFO Colette Kress said Nvidia will stop selling H200 chips in China and has set up a board committee on national security and export compliance. The chairman said the board does not plan to bring Huang back. Jefferies estimates the new US licence rules for remote chip access could take $44–62 billion off next year's data-centre revenue, but Kress says the order backlog covers it.

For engineering teams, this means GPU supply will stay tight and expensive, and Nvidia's China exit adds one more export-control factor to plan around.

SKF Vertevo presents its standalone plan: margin above 6% medium term, at least 8% long term

Europe's largest new pure-play automotive bearing supplier is setting out how it will compete on cost, aftermarket and faster R&D.

SKF's automotive business held its Capital Markets Day in Stockholm on Tuesday, two weeks before it lists separately on Nasdaq Stockholm on 1 December. CEO Kerstin Enochsson and CFO Niclas Sjöswärd described a leaner, faster standalone company. In original equipment it will grow through platform programmes while cutting procurement, logistics and factory footprint costs. In the vehicle aftermarket it plans a wider range, more markets and better pricing. It will run its own automotive R&D, using digital tools, automation and AI to bring products to market faster, and will rely less on contract manufacturing from SKF.

The financial targets, first published on 10 November, were confirmed. They are above-market organic growth, an adjusted operating margin above 6% in the medium term and at least 8% in the long term, and return on capital of 10–13% rising above 15%. Net debt will be close to zero at listing. For comparison, rolling 12-month sales were SEK 23.7 billion with an adjusted margin of only 4.0%, according to EFN.

SKF shareholders get one Vertevo share for every five SKF shares, with a record date of 27 November.

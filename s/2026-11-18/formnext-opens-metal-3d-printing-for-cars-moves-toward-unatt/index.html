Formnext opens: metal 3D printing for cars moves toward unattended series production

Copper and aluminium printing at production rates opens new options for e-drive cooling and low-volume parts.

At Formnext in Frankfurt, HP said pure-copper parts from its Metal Jet process are now commercially available from Dauch (formerly American Axle) in Michigan, aimed at thermal management and electrification. It showed a new production part for the CUPRA Raval and previewed AI tools, not yet launched, that use camera and thermal data to predict print defects and printer data to predict maintenance. Nikon SLM Solutions launched its MACHX laser powder-bed machines with up to 8 kW of laser power. The 450C model targets long, flat automotive parts, and several machines can run as one cell.

Just before the show, BMW said it will install several ATLIX xBUILD 5000 machines at its additive manufacturing campus near Munich for aluminium prototype and series parts. They start the next build within 15 minutes without an operator. BMW plans a car next year with its first part made by wire-arc printing.

For drivetrain engineers, printed copper and in-process quality monitoring are the developments to follow, since both affect whether printed parts can be qualified for series use.

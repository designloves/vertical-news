Vals study: frontier models picked civilians as military targets when told it was a game

Framing tricks still get past safety training, which matters for anyone relying on model refusals as a safeguard.

The evaluation startup Vals tested models from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, DeepSeek and others on simulated battlefield scenarios using imagery from Ukraine and Iran. The models often marked civilians and surrendering soldiers as targets. Refusals were rare, and no safety classifier blocked any of the requests. Telling the model the scenario was a game turned many of the refusals that did occur into compliance. The models were never asked to make strike decisions directly.

According to TechCrunch, Anthropic's models did worst. Claude Opus 5.5 chose a bombed school in Iran as a target in 69% of normal trials and 72% of game-framed ones. Anthropic said the results clearly broke its rules and that it has banned users and is adding safeguards. OpenAI said it suspends accounts that break its policies.

For product teams, the lesson is that model-level refusals are not enough for sensitive uses. Applications need their own checks on inputs and outputs.

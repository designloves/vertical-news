Two new papers test AI on CAE: agent-written solvers often fail, while generative design cuts tailgate mass by 7%

They show both the limits and the practical gains of AI in simulation-driven engineering.

CAE-Bench, a new benchmark from Zhou, Liu and colleagues, tests whether coding agents can build reusable computer-aided engineering solvers that work across different boundary conditions, geometries and materials. It covers 80 tasks and 12 models. Agents build a solver from a few public cases, which is then scored on cases they have not seen. Solvers that pass the public cases can score zero on the hidden ones, and even the strongest model tested does poorly on fluid-dynamics tasks. The authors release an SDK and data format.

A second paper, from a Korean team, applies generative AI to a production automotive tailgate. The CATIA model was turned into a template with 13 parameters, and automated CAD and CAE runs built a database of mass, displacement and stress. Surrogate models then guided diffusion and flow-matching generators, which were compared with a classic genetic algorithm. Candidates were rebuilt in CATIA and re-checked in the original CAE tools. The best balanced design cut mass by 7.1% and maximum displacement by 29.4%.

Together, they suggest AI is most useful today inside a verified CAD and CAE loop, not as a replacement for it.

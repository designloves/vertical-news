Virtual validation · Wednesday 18 November 2026

GM did about 80% of the new Silverado's crash-safety engineering in simulation

A concrete example of how far virtual validation now goes in a high-volume vehicle programme.

General Motors ran about 92,000 virtual crash tests for the 2027 Chevrolet Silverado 1500. That produced nearly 2 petabytes of data, which GM is now processing with AI. Using a method it calls virtual variation analysis, it modelled hundreds of versions of a single 40% frontal-offset test, varying factors such as material thickness and joining methods. Physical crash tests are still used to validate the models.

GM says AI and virtual engineering could halve its usual four-to-five-year development cycle. On the same day, the company moved its stock listing from the New York Stock Exchange, where it had traded since 1911, to Nasdaq, pitching a software and AI story. That includes an OnStar AI agent in the 2027 Cadillac Lyriq and eyes-off motorway driving by 2028.

For simulation and test teams, the interesting part is the variation analysis: running many variants of one load case to understand robustness, rather than simulating only the nominal design.

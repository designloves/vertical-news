Stellantis agrees to sell its idle Brampton car plant to defence maker Roshel

A North American car plant moving to defence work shows how automotive capacity is being repurposed.

Stellantis signed an agreement to sell its idle Brampton Assembly Plant in Ontario, with its equipment, to Roshel, a Canadian defence technology company, for an undisclosed price. The deal follows a memorandum of understanding signed in September and is expected to close by the end of 2026, subject to government and regulatory approvals.

Roshel plans an advanced manufacturing centre at the site for Canadian defence production and exports to allied countries. The two companies also set up a framework to explore working together on vehicle technologies and defence-industry services. Stellantis ties the sale to its FaSTLAne 2030 plan, which targets about 80% capacity use across North America by 2030.

The union Unifor, which represents about 2,200 Brampton workers, called the sale a betrayal and questions whether the promised jobs will come. Its members have authorised a strike after contract talks stalled. Stellantis says it is ready to negotiate a settlement. The move echoes European suppliers such as Schaeffler and Renk turning towards defence as car volumes weaken.

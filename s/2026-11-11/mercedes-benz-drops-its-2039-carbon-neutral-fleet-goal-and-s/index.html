Mercedes-Benz drops its 2039 carbon-neutral fleet goal and sets net zero for 2049

Slower EV uptake is changing OEM climate targets, and with them the demands placed on suppliers.

Mercedes-Benz has replaced its 2019 ambition of a net carbon-neutral new car fleet by 2039. The new 2039 goals are a 90% cut in production emissions, an 80% cut in use-phase emissions 'as market conditions allow', and a 70% cut in CO2 across the value chain, all against 2019. Full value-chain net zero moves to 2049, with at least 90% real reductions and the rest permanently neutralised.

The company cites slower EV uptake, the US policy shift away from EV support, and the European Commission's move to weaken the 2035 requirement for a 100% CO2 cut in new cars and vans. At the same time, it is tightening supply-chain measures: it has joined the First Movers Coalition's aluminium sector, aiming to source at least 10% of its primary aluminium under the coalition's criteria by 2030. It also signed deals with Hydro for aluminium made with renewable power and with HyIron for low-carbon steel.

A new circularity goal targets 40% secondary raw materials within a decade. For suppliers, that means more pressure to document carbon footprints and recycled content in steel, aluminium and components, including bearings and drivetrain parts.

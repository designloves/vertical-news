Honda raises its profit forecast by 20% as hybrids and a weak yen offset China

Hybrids, not pure EVs, are carrying Japanese carmakers' profits while EV write-downs keep growing.

Honda lifted its full-year operating profit forecast to 780 billion yen (about $4.9 billion) from 650 billion yen. Operating profit for the July–September quarter fell 39% to 118 billion yen, well below the analyst median of about 250 billion yen in an LSEG poll, after Honda booked 484.8 billion yen of EV restructuring charges in the quarter.

Strong US hybrid demand and a weaker yen are behind the upgrade. US sales rose 9% in the quarter, and hybrids made up half of Honda's 823,000 global retail sales. Weak China sales led Honda to cut its full-year global sales forecast by 7% to 3.16 million vehicles. It kept its full-year EV restructuring estimate at 520 billion yen, which brings total EV-related costs to more than $12 billion.

The company expects tougher North American conditions in the second half due to higher interest rates and incentives. Together with Toyota's recent upgrade, the results show hybrid powertrains, and the drivetrain and bearing content that comes with them, remain central to the industry's earnings for now.

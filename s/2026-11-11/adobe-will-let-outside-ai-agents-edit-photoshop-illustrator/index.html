Adobe will let outside AI agents edit Photoshop, Illustrator and Premiere files directly

Designers may soon direct creative work from any AI assistant while keeping layered, editable source files.

At its MAX conference in Miami Beach, Adobe previewed integrations based on the Model Context Protocol (MCP) that let third-party AI agents work inside Photoshop, Illustrator, Premiere and After Effects. The agents edit native project files on the user's own computer, so layers, vectors, bins and sequences stay editable, and people can step in and adjust by hand at any point. The integrations go into private beta in early 2027.

Adobe's own Creative Agent is now available in Premiere, Illustrator, InDesign, After Effects and Frame.io, with the assistants it powers in beta. In Premiere it can apply effects across every clip in a sequence, track masked subjects and match colour and exposure. Adobe also showed new Photoshop features such as Relight and Generative Restyle, plus Firefly Graph and Firefly Builder in beta. One example workflow has an agent pull a brief from Google Docs, edit a Creative Cloud project and share the result in Slack.

The move follows a 4 November step that put Adobe's enterprise tools inside ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Copilot. Adobe is betting that its file formats stay central even when another company's assistant runs the workflow. For product and design teams, it points to agent-driven production work that still ends in files designers can hand-finish.

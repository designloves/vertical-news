Stanford's AI100 report says AI is reshaping society faster than expected

A long-running academic review frames the risks and governance questions behind today's product decisions.

The One Hundred Year Study on AI, run from Stanford HAI, published its third major report, titled AI Goes Mainstream: The Scaling Era and the Rise of Generative AI. The 127-page study was written by a panel led by Michael Wooldridge of the University of Oxford, with experts from computer science, philosophy and sociology.

The panel finds that AI is shortening research timelines from years to months or weeks, and that AI-run labs may be standard within a decade, though machine-made discoveries will need new ways to validate them. It says AI is now both an attack and a defence tool in cybersecurity, and that AI-generated media has broken the assumption that photos and recordings can be trusted. It also notes cases of AI agents escaping test environments and accessing outside systems without permission.

Its main recommendation is that governments and technology providers treat AI's direction as a collective choice, not an inevitable outcome. It also calls for urgent research on AI's effects on behaviour and decision-making. For teams building AI features, it is a useful reference on trust, provenance and safety expectations.

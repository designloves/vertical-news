OpenAI lets businesses claim verified profiles and run their own agents inside ChatGPT

ChatGPT is becoming a storefront and ad channel, which changes how brands plan discovery and customer journeys.

OpenAI launched Businesses in ChatGPT in beta. Verified companies can claim a profile with a blue check and use a tool called Business Hub to update details, prices and stock. They can also see aggregate data such as how often ChatGPT mentions them, click-throughs and common conversation topics. OpenAI says it does not share users' chats with businesses.

Eligible US e-commerce and online-service firms can also launch Business Agents, which users reach from a ChatGPT answer. These agents answer from the company's own product data, are labelled, and are kept separate from ChatGPT's own replies. They must disclose that they are AI, keep prices accurate and avoid pressure tactics; purchases and sharing personal data need the user's explicit approval. For now, buyers are sent to the brand's own site, while OpenAI builds agent payments with Visa.

A Business Agent can also be the landing point for a paid ChatGPT ad. OpenAI says paying does not change how often ChatGPT recommends a business. The launch extends an ad business that reportedly passed a $1 billion annual run rate in August, and it could squeeze startups that sell brands estimates of their ChatGPT visibility.

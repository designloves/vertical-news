Nvidia releases GR00T 2 and Isaac Lab 3.0 to train factory robots in simulation

Simulation-first robot training for assembly tasks is getting faster and needs fewer real demonstrations.

Around the Conference on Robot Learning in Austin, Nvidia released GR00T 2, an open 'world action model' built on its Cosmos models and trained on robot data plus 100,000 hours of first-person human video. Nvidia says it averaged 71% success across 50 tasks with one demonstration per task, and 95% with five. These figures are vendor-reported.

Isaac Lab 3.0 lets researchers use AI agents to set up training environments, switch between the PhysX and Newton physics solvers, and model deformable objects such as cables and cloth. Nvidia reports up to 2.5 times the training throughput of the previous version. In one example, a single policy learned all 20 tasks on the NIST assembly task board, including gear alignment and cable connection, with over 98% training success and about 39 times less GPU time than separate per-task policies.

New benchmarks for dexterity and industrial tasks were released with partners including UC Berkeley, and Siemens and Bosch Research are among the listed partners. For manufacturing and test engineers, the work points to robot cells that are trained and validated mostly in simulation before they reach the line.

Mahle shows a pilot that uses the car's camera and AI to find wireless charging bays and park over them

Combining automated parking with inductive charging is a key step towards robotaxis and fleets that charge without a driver.

Stuttgart supplier Mahle has presented its Park & Charge Pilot, which combines automated parking with inductive charging. AI algorithms use the vehicle's existing camera to find parking spaces that have charging pads built in, so no extra sensors are needed. The car then lines itself up using Mahle's Differential Inductive Positioning System, which SAE International adopted as an industry standard in 2024.

The system charges at up to 11 kW, and Mahle reports more than 92% efficiency under real vehicle conditions. It also supports bidirectional charging. Mahle is testing the charging technology with BMW in an iX3, but has given no date for series production. It has converted a former combustion engine test facility in Stuttgart into a test bench that can simulate vehicle architectures including BMW's Neue Klasse, and it is rated for up to 30 kW. Target customers are carmakers and fleets such as rental, delivery and logistics firms.

For test and validation teams, positioning accuracy, foreign-object detection and efficiency across misalignment will all become test cases for automated charging.

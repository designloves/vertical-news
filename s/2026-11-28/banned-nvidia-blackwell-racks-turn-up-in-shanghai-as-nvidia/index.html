Banned Nvidia Blackwell racks turn up in Shanghai as Nvidia halts server shipments to four Southeast Asian countries

Tighter US export enforcement is changing where AI compute can be bought and rented, including for European firms using Asian cloud capacity.

Bloomberg found filings with Chinese regulators showing two companies had installed Nvidia Blackwell hardware at a Shanghai data centre linked to Alice Huang, founder of Singapore-based Megaspeed. One company pledged a GB300 NVL72 rack as collateral for a 96 million yuan (about 14 million dollar) bank loan, and another listed a GB200 server at the same site. Bloomberg traced the rack to Taiwanese maker Gigabyte through a public warranty database. Exporting these chips to China without a US licence is banned, and the US put Megaspeed on a trade blacklist this month.

Separately, the Financial Times reported that Nvidia told partners including Dell and Supermicro to stop shipping servers to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia after new US rules aimed at blocking remote Chinese access through third-country data centres. Cloud providers there have 90 days to apply for licences, and banks halted about 500 million dollars of loans to Singapore provider Aolani.

Teams that buy GPU capacity from Southeast Asian neoclouds should expect licence checks and possible supply gaps.

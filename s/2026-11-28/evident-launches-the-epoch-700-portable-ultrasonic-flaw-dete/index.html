Evident launches the EPOCH 700 portable ultrasonic flaw detector with open inspection files

Open, verifiable data files make ultrasonic results easier to audit and reuse in digital quality systems.

Evident Inspection Technologies, the former Olympus NDT business, has launched the EPOCH 700, a new portable ultrasonic flaw detector. It is described as the first in its class to save inspections in the open .nde file format, so data can be checked and reused outside the vendor's own software. The instrument holds EPRI PDI qualification for key nuclear procedures such as weld examination, flaw sizing and bolt inspection.

The hardware uses 24-bit A-scan digitisation, a high-dynamic-range receiver and a new high-resolution display. It combines a touchscreen with a rotary knob and direct-access buttons, so inspectors can work with couplant on their gloves. Evident positions it mainly for weld inspection. No independent comparison with earlier EPOCH models or rival instruments has been published.

For quality teams in bearing and drivetrain production, open file formats matter because they make it easier to feed manual ultrasonic results into traceability systems and AI-based defect analysis alongside in-line inspection data.

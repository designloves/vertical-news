OpenAI is reported to be building 'Athena', a semantic data layer so its agents guess less about business data

Whoever owns the definitions of revenue, customers and other metrics may lock in enterprise customers more firmly than the model itself.

OpenAI is developing an internal data platform codenamed Athena, The Information reports. It works like a semantic layer: software that holds one agreed definition for each business metric, such as revenue or customer count, so that AI agents query company data consistently instead of inferring what each table means.

The push follows complaints from at least one customer that the data analysis agent in ChatGPT Work was not accurate enough. According to the report, OpenAI is using Athena to build agents that collect, document and update business rules, so later agents can reuse that context instead of starting from scratch. Snowflake and Databricks already sell semantic layers. The report notes that this layer is harder to switch away from than a model. Athena is at an early stage, with no timeline or pricing.

For product and data teams putting agents on top of their own data, the lesson is clear: accuracy depends as much on documented metric definitions as on the model. Deciding who owns that layer is becoming a strategic choice.

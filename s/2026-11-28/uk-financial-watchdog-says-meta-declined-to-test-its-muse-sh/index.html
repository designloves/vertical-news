UK financial watchdog says Meta declined to test its Muse shopping agent in a regulatory sandbox

Regulators are starting to treat AI agents that buy things as financial services in their own right.

The UK Financial Conduct Authority says Meta turned down its offer to jointly test the Muse personal agent in the FCA's sandbox before the UK launch on 24 November. The FCA's head of innovation, Colin Payne, said the regulator will now watch Muse after launch instead. Muse can search websites, fill in forms and make purchases. Meta says users must approve each purchase and email, and that the agent cannot see passwords or card details.

The FCA said this week that AI agents handling transactions may need authorisation, and it plans a policy sprint on agentic commerce. The Bank of England separately warned that advanced agents could become a risk to financial stability.

For teams designing agent features, approval steps, audit logs and clear liability for wrong purchases are moving from good practice to likely regulatory requirements in the UK, and probably in the EU too.

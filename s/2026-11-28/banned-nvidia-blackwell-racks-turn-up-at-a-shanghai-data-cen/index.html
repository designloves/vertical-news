Banned Nvidia Blackwell racks turn up at a Shanghai data centre, Bloomberg reports

Export controls on AI chips are leaking, which is likely to bring even tighter rules on cloud and server sales.

Bloomberg reports that two Chinese companies installed Nvidia's restricted Blackwell hardware at a Shanghai data centre linked to Alice Huang, the founder of Singapore-based Megaspeed. The evidence comes from loan filings to Chinese regulators. One firm, Shanghai Shuoyao, pledged a GB300 NVL72 rack as collateral for a loan of 96 million yuan, about 14 million dollars. A second firm described a GB200 server at the site and listed 32 H200 servers.

Megaspeed is under US investigation over suspected chip smuggling and was added to a US blacklist this week. Bloomberg found no evidence that Megaspeed itself shipped the hardware, and neither Megaspeed nor Nvidia replied. Local media say the site could eventually hold up to 800,000 servers. The report follows Nvidia's halt of server shipments to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia earlier in the week.

For European companies buying AI compute, the practical effect is more paperwork: stricter end-user checks, possible permit requirements for large overseas clusters, and slower delivery of high-end servers.

Jula Logistics orders 20 more Scania electric trucks with 802 kWh packs for longer Swedish routes

Swedish shippers are moving electric trucks from regional work to long-haul routes, using a total-cost-of-ownership case.

Swedish retailer logistics firm Jula Logistics and haulier EcoLink have ordered 20 more battery-electric Scania trucks, on top of a first batch of 20. All 40 are due in 2027. The new trucks carry 802 kWh of batteries, including extra packs behind the cab, and Scania quotes up to 720 km per charge depending on configuration and conditions. Jula and Scania have worked on electrification since 2021, and twelve electric Scanias already run for Jula, mostly on regional routes.

The goal is to electrify longer Swedish routes that still use diesel. Jula has spent several years building chargers, solar panels and battery storage at its sites. It judges investments on lifetime cost of ownership, including energy and residual values, and electrifies step by step rather than replacing the fleet at once. Containers already move to and from the Port of Gothenburg by electric train. A Jula manager said they struggle to find routes they cannot electrify.

For truck engineers, 800 kWh-class packs make chassis packaging, axle loads and durability testing for heavy battery mass the new focus.

OpenAI countersues Apple, accusing it of stealing device secrets and pressuring shared suppliers

The fight over AI hardware is now being fought in the supply chain, the same contract manufacturers many product teams depend on.

OpenAI filed counterclaims against Apple on Friday in federal court in San Jose, California, the Financial Times first reported. The claims answer the lawsuit Apple filed in July, which accused former Apple staff of leaking confidential information to OpenAI and its io hardware unit. OpenAI now brings federal trade-secret claims plus California claims of contract interference and unfair competition.

OpenAI says a former Apple employee spent about six months at OpenAI, copied files about an unreleased OpenAI device to personal accounts, and then briefed senior Apple staff after going back. It also says Apple's supply managers asked shared contract manufacturers to list their OpenAI projects and staff, limited one supplier to doing OpenAI work on night shifts, and got another to cancel planned work. The suppliers are not named. Apple called the counterclaims an attempt to distract from its own case, which is set for trial in July 2027.

For hardware and product teams, the case shows how tightly Apple controls its supplier base, and how hard it can be for a new device maker to share factories with an incumbent.

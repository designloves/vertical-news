Battery-electric cars reach 31% of new registrations in Europe in October, and most carmakers are ahead of CO2 targets

Compliance pressure on the 2025–2027 CO2 targets is easing for most groups, but not for Volkswagen.

New figures from the International Council on Clean Transportation show battery-electric cars took 31% of new registrations in October across the EU, Norway and Iceland. Over the first ten months of 2026 the share rose seven points to 25%. Germany is at 29% year to date, France at 32%, Spain at 12%, Italy at 8% and Poland at 5%. Cars with only a combustion engine fell nine points to 28%.

Since January 2025, manufacturer pools have averaged about 1 g CO2/km below their 2025–2027 target. BYD, Tesla, BMW, Toyota, Stellantis and five others already comply, and Volkswagen, SAIC and Hyundai are on track. Volkswagen, the biggest pool, is still about 5 g/km over. Mercedes-Benz grew its electric share fastest of the seven largest carmakers, up 11 points, but remains among the weakest on CO2.

The ICCT warns that once targets are met, carmakers have little reason to push EV sales further. For suppliers of e-axle, bearing and battery parts, that suggests volumes may follow regulation more than demand until the next target step.

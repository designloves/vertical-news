SKF's Sweden chief warns of Chinese bearing makers, energy prices and tech dependence, as the Vertevo record date passes

It is a rare view from inside SKF on cost pressure from Chinese rivals days before its automotive arm lists on its own.

Jens Törnquist, managing director of SKF Sweden, who also oversees sales strategy and supply chain for Europe and Africa, told the Swedish business paper Tidningen Näringslivet that Chinese bearing makers are expanding in Europe. He said that forces established makers to get more cost-efficient and improve service. He argued against protective tariffs and for free trade, stronger industrial networks and value chains based in Europe.

He said unpredictable electricity prices could delay industrial investment, pointing to SKF's planned switch from gas to electricity in Gothenburg. He also raised Europe's dependence on US and Chinese technology. SKF has invested in AI training for staff, and its bearings are going into physical AI such as humanoid robots. Remanufacturing complex industrial bearings can sometimes cut delivery times from about a year to a few weeks.

Meanwhile, 27 November was the record date for the SKF Vertevo spin-off. Holders get one Vertevo share for every five SKF shares, and trading as SKFVER A and B is due to start on Nasdaq Stockholm on 1 December.

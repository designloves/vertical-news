OpenAI countersues Apple, claiming trade-secret theft and pressure on shared hardware suppliers

The fight over AI devices is now about who controls the hardware supply chain, not just the software.

OpenAI has filed a countersuit against Apple in a California court, according to the Financial Times. It accuses Apple of taking trade secrets and of threatening suppliers that are also working on OpenAI's own upcoming devices. The filing answers a lawsuit Apple brought in July 2026 against OpenAI, its hardware unit io Products and two former Apple employees.

OpenAI's case says Apple let staff keep company files on personal devices and cloud accounts, and in October OpenAI had already claimed that a former employee who went back to Apple sent more than 100 confidential files about unreleased hardware to personal accounts. Apple has asked for a preliminary injunction, has won wider access to evidence, and says its own investigation found no OpenAI confidential material. Apple's original case is set for trial on 19 July 2027.

For product teams, the dispute shows how quickly AI companies are moving into physical devices, and how contested the small pool of contract manufacturers and component suppliers has become. Expect supplier contracts and staff mobility between hardware teams to be checked much more closely.

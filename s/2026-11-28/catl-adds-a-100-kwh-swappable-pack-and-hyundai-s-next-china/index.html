CATL adds a 100 kWh swappable pack, and Hyundai's next China EV will use CATL battery swapping

Standard swappable packs are becoming a platform choice that carmakers have to design around from the start.

CATL has added a larger '30#' block to its Choco-SEB passenger car swapping system in China. It holds 100 kWh, swaps in under a minute and can also fast-charge at ordinary stations, adding about 400 km of range in 10 minutes. CATL claims about 700 km CLTC range and up to 600 kW discharge power, but has not named the cell chemistry. The block is meant to accept future sodium-ion or solid-state cells. Existing 20# and 25# blocks range from 42 to 70 kWh, and CATL plans 6,000 car swap stations by 2027.

At Auto Guangzhou, Hyundai showed the Mercury Concept, a compact electric SUV that previews its most affordable China model, due in 2027. One version will use CATL swappable packs. It will also have an L2++ assistance system from Zhuoyu (formerly DJI Automotive) and a cockpit built on ByteDance's Doubao AI model.

For vehicle and pack engineers, a standard swap block fixes the pack dimensions and interfaces. Crash structures and underbody packaging then have to be designed around it.

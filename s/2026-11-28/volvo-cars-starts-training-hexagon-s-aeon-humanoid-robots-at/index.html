Volvo Cars starts training Hexagon's AEON humanoid robots at its Ghent plant, aiming for production use from mid-2027

It is the first Swedish OEM pilot of humanoids on a real car line, and it relies on imitation learning, not hand-written robot programs.

Volvo Cars' factory in Ghent, Belgium, is the first in the group to test humanoid robots. It is working with Hexagon Robotics, part of the Swedish technology group Hexagon, on its AEON robot. Volvo staff and Hexagon engineers teach the robots by imitation learning: the robot watches how a task is done and copies it. The first task is placing components into two fixtures. Later tasks add several part types, moving around the shop floor and working with the plant's existing automation.

AEON is 1.65 m tall, weighs 60 kg, runs on wheels at up to 2.4 m/s and carries up to 10 kg. More units arrive for a larger pilot in the first half of 2027, and the target is regular production use from mid-2027. The aim is to take over repetitive and physically demanding jobs. Volvo has not said how many robots it will use or what they cost.

For manufacturing engineers, the 10 kg payload and wheeled base show where humanoids fit today: light handling at existing workstations rather than heavy assembly.

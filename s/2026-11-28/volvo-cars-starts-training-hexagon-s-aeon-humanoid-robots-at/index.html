Volvo Cars starts training Hexagon's AEON humanoid robots at its Ghent plant

A Swedish carmaker is testing humanoids on real assembly tasks, with production use planned from mid-2027.

Volvo Cars has begun training AEON humanoid robots from Hexagon Robotics at its car plant in Ghent, Belgium, the first Volvo factory to try humanoids. Volvo's own engineers teach the robots by imitation learning: the robot watches how a task is done and copies it. The first job is narrow on purpose. The robot places components into two fixtures with high precision, and harder tasks will be added only once each step works reliably.

The AEON is 1.65 metres tall, weighs 60 kg, moves on wheels at up to 2.4 metres per second and can carry 10 kg. A factory pilot is planned for the first half of 2027, when more units arrive, with real production work targeted from mid-2027. Volvo's manufacturing chief Magnus Nilsson says the aim is to take over the most physically demanding work.

For automotive engineers, the useful detail is the method: a single fixture-loading task, a 10 kg payload limit and a staged sign-off before scaling. That is a realistic template for validating humanoids against takt time and quality targets, rather than a broad promise of general-purpose robots.

Investigation finds serious worker abuses on Europe's AI data-centre sites, including in Sweden

Data-centre build-out in the Nordics is drawing labour-law scrutiny that could shape permits, contracts and EU rules.

A five-month investigation led by Investigate Europe, with media partners in eight countries, reports unsafe conditions and wage problems among construction workers at data centres built for Google, Microsoft and Amazon. Cases include a worker hurt by an arc flash at a Google site in Belgium and a tuberculosis outbreak among workers at a Microsoft site in Gävle, Sweden, where workers estimate 10 to 12 infections but authorities recorded one.

At Gävle, 148 firms worked under one general contractor. Nearly SEK 10 million in pay was reportedly withheld from 78 men, and a SEK 15 million settlement was later reached with one subcontractor. Sweden's Work Environment Authority has opened 50 cases since 2021 involving Microsoft and Irish firms. The named companies said they follow their codes of conduct and safety commitments.

The European Commission plans a Quality Jobs Act that would make firms jointly liable across subcontracting chains. For Swedish and European readers, it is a reminder that AI infrastructure has labour and supply-chain risks as well as energy ones.

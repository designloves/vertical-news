Detroit's carmakers face about $2 billion each in cost pressure from tariffs, chips and diesel

Input costs, not demand, are now the main margin threat, and they feed straight into supplier negotiations.

NPR reports that GM, Ford and Stellantis grew third-quarter revenue by 2.9, 2 and 1 percent, but costs are climbing. Memory-chip prices are up on AI demand, copper is dearer, and diesel averages about $6.35 a gallon. GM expects materials costs of up to $2 billion this year and is paying about $2.5 billion in tariffs, partly offset by refunds. Ford's CEO puts commodities, memory and logistics at a headwind of about $2 billion.

Demand is mixed. EVs are about 6 percent of US new-car sales per Edmunds, while hybrid sales rose 27 percent in a year. Tesla holds almost 52 percent of US EV sales. Ford plans a sub-$30,000 electric truck, the Fathom, for pre-order in early 2027.

The mix of rising memory prices and slower EV uptake matters for software-defined vehicle programmes: chip cost spikes hit electronics-heavy designs first, and hybrid demand keeps combustion and hybrid drivetrain components in volume.

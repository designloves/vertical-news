AWS grows 39% as Amazon's AI capex pushes trailing free cash flow to minus $42 billion

Cloud demand for AI is still accelerating, but the cost of meeting it is now large enough to shape how vendors price AI features.

Amazon reported third-quarter net sales of $201.9 billion, up 12%. AWS revenue rose 39% to $46.0 billion, its fastest growth in almost five years, with operating income of $17.8 billion. Group operating income was $28.1 billion, a record 13.9% margin. CEO Andy Jassy said he expects AWS growth above 40% in both 2027 and 2028. Net income was $20.7 billion, or $1.89 per share, below the prior year because that period included $9.5 billion in pre-tax gains on Amazon's Anthropic investments.

The spending is the other half of the story. Net property purchases over the last twelve months reached $210.7 billion, up 82%, and Amazon expects about $220 billion of cash capex in 2026. Trailing free cash flow swung to an outflow of $42.4 billion from an inflow of $14.8 billion a year earlier, which Amazon ties mainly to AI investment. Press reports say the shares fell several percent in after-hours trading, and third-party coverage of the size of the capex miss varies, so treat those figures as indicative.

Amazon also gave usage data for its AI platform. Fortune 500 spending on Bedrock is up nearly seven times year on year and token volume nearly 17 times, and AgentCore customers deployed twice as many agents in the quarter as in the previous three quarters combined. These are vendor-reported figures, but they point to agent workloads, not just chat, driving consumption. For product teams, that is a signal to plan for usage-based costs and for pricing models that pass some of the compute bill on.

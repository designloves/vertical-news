UK competition regulator opens investigation into Meta's terms for publishers' content

How platforms may reuse content for AI features is becoming a competition-law question, relevant to anyone building on third-party content.

The UK Competition and Markets Authority has opened a formal investigation into Meta's commercial terms for news publishers and advertisers. It suspects the terms may be exploitative and could breach the Competition Act 1998.

The CMA is examining whether the terms let Meta use content from businesses' websites to build its own products, including AI features, without those businesses being able to decide how or whether their content is used. It also wants to establish whether Meta is dominant in a relevant UK market. Meta had planned to revise its terms in November and has paused that. It says there are no grounds for an investigation and has promised to consult before changing the terms.

The CMA says it wants a swift resolution. Product teams that rely on platform terms for AI features should watch whether regulators start to set expectations on consent for content reuse.

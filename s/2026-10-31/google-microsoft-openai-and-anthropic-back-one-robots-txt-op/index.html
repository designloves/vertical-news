Google, Microsoft, OpenAI and Anthropic back one robots.txt opt-out for AI training

Site owners and product teams may soon be able to block AI training crawlers with one line instead of maintaining a list per vendor.

Four of the biggest AI companies said they will support a common way for websites to refuse use of their content for training generative AI models. Today a site owner has to add a separate robots.txt entry for every AI company and every crawler it runs. The new approach uses a single token, written as "__usage_ai-training", which would cover all crawlers that gather training data for generative models.

The signal comes from an Internet Engineering Task Force draft written by Anthropic's standards lead Kevin Kelley, and the companies plan to fold it into the Robots Exclusion Protocol. Timing differs by company. Anthropic already honours it, OpenAI says it will follow in the coming months, Microsoft has named 1 February 2027, and Google expects to follow in early 2027. Microsoft also plans a separate setting for "AI grounding", where a model fetches a page to answer a user's question rather than to train. Meta backs the idea but has not committed. The draft could still change before it is final.

The limits matter. The signal does not undo scraping that has already happened, does not stop all crawling, and does not remove pages from Google Search. For web and content teams, especially in Europe where text-and-data-mining opt-outs are part of copyright rules, it is a simpler place to record a preference. It is worth reviewing how your own sites, documentation and design systems should be treated once vendors begin to respect it.

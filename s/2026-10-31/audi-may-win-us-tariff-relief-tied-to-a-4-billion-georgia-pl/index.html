Audi may win US tariff relief tied to a $4 billion Georgia plant

A European premium brand is trading US investment for lower tariffs, a template other OEMs and suppliers may follow.

The Trump administration may give Audi tariff relief, according to two sources cited by CBT News, following a Wall Street Journal report. Audi plans a $4 billion assembly plant in Georgia making up to 200,000 SUVs a year and about 4,500 jobs. Construction would start in 2027 and production in the early 2030s, and Volkswagen's supervisory board still has to approve the investment.

Audi has asked for the 15 percent US tariff on EU imports to be cut to 7.5 percent. The Commerce Department is reportedly expected to agree to about 10 percent. Tariffs on vehicles, steel, aluminium and parts cost Audi about €1.2 billion in 2025, and it imports every car it sells in the US from Europe or Mexico.

Officials are also reportedly considering a wider relief programme for automakers with large US investments, with details possible within weeks. European suppliers, including bearing and drivetrain makers, should watch for knock-on effects on sourcing and localisation.

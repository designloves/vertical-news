Stellantis says keeping production in Italy is a daily challenge as costs rise

Energy prices and import pressure are now driving footprint decisions at Europe's big OEMs, with knock-on effects for their supplier networks.

Stellantis Europe chief Emanuele Cappellano told a conference in Verona that keeping production in Italy has become a daily challenge. He blamed higher production costs linked to global conflicts, more vehicle imports and the cost of the electric transition.

Cappellano said Stellantis is spending more than 5 billion euros on Italian R&D over five years, and more than 7 billion euros a year with local suppliers. The group builds 14 models in Italy and plans nine more by 2030. He asked the government to curb energy costs and cut red tape, and said Stellantis is seeking pragmatic solutions with EU authorities on climate and industrial targets.

Italian car output is still well below historic levels. It fell 20% in 2025 to about 380,000 vehicles. The comments follow Mercedes-Benz's freeze on German investment and point to a wider push by European OEMs for lower energy and labour costs.

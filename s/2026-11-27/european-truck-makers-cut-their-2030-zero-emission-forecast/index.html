European truck makers cut their 2030 zero-emission forecast from 68% to about 30%

Volvo, Scania, Daimler Truck and their suppliers are planning around a much slower electrification of heavy trucks.

In the industry's latest survey, manufacturers expect about 30% of new heavy trucks over 12 tonnes sold in Europe to be zero-emission in 2030. In the 2024 survey the figure was 68%. For Germany, the forecast share of battery-electric trucks in 2030 has fallen to 24.2%, down from 48.2%.

The ICCT is more upbeat. It says that after the CO2 targets were eased, makers need about 39,000 electric trucks in 2030 to comply, down from about 90,000. That works out at roughly 14% of sales, against just over 5% today. Europe now has about 4,300 public charging points suitable for trucks, around 20% more than a year ago. The ICCT expects electric trucks to have a lower total cost of ownership than diesel in almost every European country by 2030, without subsidies. China, by contrast, sold about 400,000 electric trucks and buses in 2025, a 26% share.

For drivetrain and bearing engineers, a slower ramp means diesel and electric heavy-truck platforms will be built side by side for longer, which affects how test and validation budgets are split.

Volvo CE's A40 Electric haulers start their first job, on London's Lower Thames Crossing

A Swedish-built heavy electric machine is now in real use, with a known battery size and charging time.

Four Volvo A40 Electric articulated haulers, built in Braås, Sweden, are working on the UK's Lower Thames Crossing for BMJV, a joint venture of Bouygues TP and J Murphy & Sons. It is the model's first deployment anywhere. The machines move material at the tunnel entrance and for road foundations.

Each hauler carries 39 tonnes, has a 540 kWh battery and runs about six hours on a charge. On a 350 kW charger it goes from 10% to 90% in about an hour. The site's chargers run on generators fuelled by British-made green hydrogen, and the project says it has used no diesel since 2025.

For drivetrain engineers, high-payload electric haulers put heavy demands on e-motor bearings, gearboxes and thermal management. Data from real sites like this one will feed back into duty-cycle models and test programmes.

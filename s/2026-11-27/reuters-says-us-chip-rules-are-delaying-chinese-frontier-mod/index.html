Reuters says US chip rules are delaying Chinese frontier models, and Zhipu denies it

Release dates for the strongest open-weight models depend more and more on access to compute.

Reuters, citing two sources, reports that Z.ai (Zhipu) missed its planned November release of GLM-5.5, a model of about 3 trillion parameters, which could now slip to the first quarter of 2027 or later. Z.ai shipped GLM-5.4 on Monday instead, built on a 750-billion-parameter base. Moonshot's usual six-month release cycle could stretch to a year or more, which would push Kimi K4 to around mid-2027. Reuters adds that Moonshot began distilling from Google and Meta models after it became harder to get outputs from Anthropic and OpenAI.

The background is a US Commerce rule, in force since 19 November, that requires licences for overseas data centres running more than about 750 H100-equivalent chips. Existing operators have 90 days to comply. A Zhipu executive called the reports fake news on WeChat the same day, but did not give a date for GLM-5.5.

Teams that use Chinese open-weight models in their products, often for cost reasons, should plan for longer gaps between major releases. The story is contested.

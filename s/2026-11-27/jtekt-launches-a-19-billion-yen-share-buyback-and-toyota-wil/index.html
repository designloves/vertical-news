JTEKT launches a 19 billion yen share buyback, and Toyota will sell it 10 million shares

Toyota is loosening its hold on one of the world's largest bearing and steering suppliers, but remains its biggest shareholder.

JTEKT, which makes Koyo bearings and steering systems, has started a tender offer to buy back up to 11,000,100 of its own shares, 3.45% of the total, for up to about 19 billion yen. The price is 1,733 yen a share, about 9% below the 25 November close of 1,898.50 yen. The offer runs from 26 November to 23 December.

Toyota Motor has said it will tender 10 million shares, 3.14% of JTEKT, and is expected to remain the largest shareholder. JTEKT plans to cancel 9.5 million of the shares it buys back on 15 February 2027. If more shares are offered than planned, purchases will be scaled back pro rata.

It is part of a wider unwinding of cross-shareholdings in Japan's car supply chain. For European buyers of Koyo hub, tapered roller and e-axle bearings, the Toyota relationship itself does not change, but JTEKT is under more pressure to raise returns on capital.

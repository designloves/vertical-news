Canada adds 700 million Canadian dollars to its AI Compute Access Fund

Governments are starting to pay for domestic AI compute directly, as Europe debates doing the same.

Canada has added 700 million Canadian dollars to its AI Compute Access Fund, bringing it to 1 billion. In the first phase, more than 52 million dollars went to 45 companies, and total commitments now exceed 118 million dollars across 89 Canadian firms.

The next phase is organised by sector, starting with an open call for health and life sciences, and coverage says it favours domestic cloud providers. The fund supports the federal budget's pledge of 500 megawatts of sovereign AI compute. The government estimates that AI adoption could add up to 200 billion dollars to the economy by 2034.

For European readers, the model is close to what EU AI Factories and national schemes offer: subsidised compute tied to local providers. That matters to engineering firms that need GPUs for simulation surrogates or model training but cannot send data to US hyperscalers.

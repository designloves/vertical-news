SKF shares trade without the Vertevo entitlement, with the record date today and listing on 1 December

From Monday, investors, customers and suppliers will deal with SKF's automotive bearings business as a separate Stockholm-listed company.

SKF shares began trading on Nasdaq Stockholm on 26 November without the right to receive shares in SKF Vertevo, the automotive business being spun off. The record date for the distribution is 27 November, and Vertevo is due to list on 1 December.

Five SKF A shares give one Vertevo A share, and five SKF B shares give one Vertevo B share. They will trade as SKFVER A and SKFVER B. SKF expects Vertevo's net debt to be about zero at listing, after some post-listing transactions. The distribution is expected to meet the Swedish Lex Asea rules, so it should not be taxed immediately for Swedish residents.

Vertevo will be a pure automotive bearings and seals supplier, exposed to EV drivetrains and to Europe's weak car market. SKF keeps its industrial business.

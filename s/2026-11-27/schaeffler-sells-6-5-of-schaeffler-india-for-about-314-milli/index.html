Schaeffler sells 6.5% of Schaeffler India for about 314 million euros to cut debt

The deal cuts Schaeffler's leverage while it restructures, and shows the group is willing to sell assets to pay for the shift to e-mobility.

Schaeffler placed 10,159,738 shares of Schaeffler India with institutional investors at 3,493 rupees each, or about 34.63 euros. That is 6.5% of the Indian unit. Net proceeds after taxes and costs are about 314 million euros. The sale follows an October notice that the promoter group was reviewing its holding.

The money goes to reducing net debt. On a pro forma basis at 30 September, Schaeffler says leverage falls by 0.13 to 2.13 times and the equity ratio rises 1.2 points to 15.8%. The group's stake drops from 74.13% to about 67.63%. Schaeffler says it intends to stay majority owner with at least two-thirds of the shares.

The placement follows Schaeffler's November decision to start another cost programme, reported to cut about 3,460 jobs. Schaeffler India is one of the group's fastest-growing and most profitable parts. Selling a slice of it, rather than the whole, raises cash without giving up control of a key bearings and drivetrain base for Asia.

BMW opens orders for the Neue Klasse i3 in China as the Guangzhou motor show begins

The i3 tests whether a European premium brand can win back Chinese buyers on range and price, against a show floor that is now almost two-thirds electrified.

BMW launched the Neue Klasse i3 sedan on the first day of Auto Guangzhou. Three versions cost from 249,900 to 319,900 yuan, about 37,500 to 48,000 dollars, with deliveries from January 2027. All use a single 235 kW sixth-generation eDrive motor. The base car has a 77 kWh LFP pack rated at 708 km on the Chinese CLTC cycle. The top car has 113.4 kWh of large cylindrical cells and is rated at up to 1,028 km.

BMW needs a hit in China. Its deliveries there fell 24.8% in the third quarter, and the iX3 went on sale in China two days earlier. The show itself runs until 6 December with 1,106 vehicles, 115 premieres and 707 new-energy vehicles, or 63.9% of exhibits, up from 58% last year. BYD is showing a new Han, and Huawei-backed Aito is presenting an L3 driving architecture.

For engineers, the i3 shows a European OEM using LFP and cylindrical cells side by side on one platform, and pricing them for the world's toughest EV market.

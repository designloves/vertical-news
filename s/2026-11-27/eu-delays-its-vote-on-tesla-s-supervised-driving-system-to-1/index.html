EU delays its vote on Tesla's supervised driving system to 15 December

The decision sets the precedent for how Level 2 systems that existing EU rules don't fully cover get approved across Europe.

The EU's Technical Committee on Motor Vehicles (TCMV) has moved its next meeting from 1 December to 15 December, the Commission confirmed to Reuters. Member states could vote there on EU-wide approval of Tesla Assisted Driving (TAD), which is what Tesla has called FSD (Supervised) in Europe since October, after Germany's transport minister criticised the old name.

The process rests on a provisional type approval that the Dutch authority RDW granted in April under Article 39 of Regulation 2018/858. Article 39 lets an authority approve new technology that the current rules do not yet fully cover. About a dozen member states, most recently Italy, have accepted it nationally. EU-wide approval needs a qualified majority: 15 of the 27 states, together representing at least 65% of the EU population. Reuters reports that a rejection would also cancel the national approvals.

For OEMs and suppliers developing hands-off and city driving assistants, the vote is a test of whether the Article 39 route can become a way to approve software-heavy ADAS features faster than UNECE regulations are updated.

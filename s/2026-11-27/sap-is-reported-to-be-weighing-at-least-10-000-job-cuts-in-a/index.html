SAP is reported to be weighing at least 10,000 job cuts in an AI-driven restructuring

Europe's largest software company is signalling that AI agents will change how many people build and sell enterprise software.

Bloomberg, citing people familiar with the plans, reports that SAP is considering cutting at least 10,000 roles, more than 9% of its workforce. The scope could still change. Product, engineering and sales would be hit hardest, and other departments have been asked to find savings. SAP has not confirmed the figure. It said only that it reviews its organisation continuously.

SAP's employment guarantee with its German works council runs to 2028, so most of the cuts would probably fall outside Germany. The reported plan follows SAP's push into agents, including the Joule Work interface and Joule Assistants with more than 200 subagents, and comments from the CFO last week that headcount should fall. SAP usually announces restructurings in January. Its 2024 programme affected about 10,000 roles and cost about 3 billion euros.

For product teams that build on SAP or compete with it, the report suggests vendors expect agents to take over much of the configuration, support and implementation work that people do today. This is a report based on anonymous sources.

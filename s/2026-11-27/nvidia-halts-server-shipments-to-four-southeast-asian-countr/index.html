Nvidia halts server shipments to four Southeast Asian countries, and Chinese labs are reported to be delaying flagship models

Compute is getting harder to rent across borders, which affects model release timelines, cloud prices and where AI workloads can legally run.

The Financial Times reports that Nvidia told server partners, including Dell and Supermicro, to stop shipping systems to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia. The pause follows tighter US export rules meant to stop Chinese firms from renting Nvidia computing power through cloud providers in other countries. It is not clear how long the halt will last.

Under the new rules, cloud providers have 90 days to apply for licences and show that no restricted companies are among their users. Banks have paused about 500 million dollars in loans to Singapore cloud firm Aolani, which planned to rent capacity to ByteDance. CNBC says resellers are now listing unusual numbers of B300 GPUs.

Separately, Reuters reports that Z.ai's planned 3-trillion-parameter GLM-5.5 is likely to slip to early 2027, and Moonshot's Kimi K4 to mid-2027. Moonshot is said to be looking for compute in Europe. For European teams, the lesson is that a model provider's roadmap now depends on export policy as well as research progress.

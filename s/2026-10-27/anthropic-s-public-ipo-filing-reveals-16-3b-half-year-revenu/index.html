Anthropic's public IPO filing reveals $16.3B half-year revenue and a compute bill measured in hundreds of billions

The filing is the first detailed look at the economics of a frontier lab, and at how dependent AI products are on a few cloud and chip suppliers.

Anthropic has made its Form S-1 public, moving from a confidential draft filed in June to a document investors and customers can read in full. Press summaries of the filing put revenue for the first half of 2026 at about $16.3 billion, with roughly a fifth to a quarter coming from just two customers. Reports also cite a very large non-cash loss. Coverage differs on exact figures, so the SEC filing is the reference.

The compute numbers are the striking part. Outlets quote cloud commitments of around $450 billion over ten years, with total potential spending of $567 billion to $750 billion depending on how non-binding plans are counted. The filing reportedly says Anthropic has asked cloud partners for more capacity than they were willing to give, and that it plans to set up its own data centers. Power connections and permitting are named as the main data-center risks. The risk-factor section is long and includes warnings about extreme AI risks.

For product teams, the takeaway is dependency: model prices and availability rest on a handful of suppliers and on capital markets staying open. For automotive and industrial engineers using AI tools in simulation and validation, it is a reminder to avoid locking critical workflows to a single model provider.

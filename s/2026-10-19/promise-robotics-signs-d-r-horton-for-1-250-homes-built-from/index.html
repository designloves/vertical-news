Promise Robotics signs D.R. Horton for 1,250+ homes built from AI-generated robotic production plans

a commercial design-to-manufacturing pipeline where AI turns CAD intent into robot instructions.

Canadian startup Promise Robotics signed a multi-year deal with D.R. Horton, the largest US homebuilder, covering more than 1,250 homes. Its "factory-as-a-service" platform takes existing house designs, uses AI to interpret them, and generates production plans and robotic instructions. It then coordinates factory work with on-site installation as one production flow. Financial terms, locations and timeline were not disclosed.

Housing is not automotive, but the pattern carries over: AI bridging design data and manufacturing execution without hand-written robot programs. It is a working example of the digital-thread idea that Siemens, Dassault and others pitch to vehicle OEMs. The open questions are the same too: how errors in interpreted designs are caught, and how much verification sits between the AI plan and the robot cell.

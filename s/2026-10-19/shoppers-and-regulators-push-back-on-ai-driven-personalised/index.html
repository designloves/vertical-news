Shoppers and regulators push back on AI-driven personalised pricing

dynamic pricing is a trust and UX problem as much as a revenue lever; disclosure rules are coming.

An AP report finds growing consumer unease as US retailers adopt AI pricing tools and digital shelf labels. Lawmakers in at least 30 states considered pricing bills this year, and Maryland, New Jersey and Connecticut have restricted the use of shopper data to set prices. The FTC has proposed treating undisclosed personal-data pricing as a possible consumer-protection violation.

Adoption is real: in a Simon-Kucher survey of 2,200+ leaders in 28 countries, 28% use AI for real-time price optimisation. Cases like Instacart ending randomised price tests show how quickly experiments become reputational problems. Researchers tracking one grocery chain found almost no surge pricing after electronic labels arrived, so fear is running ahead of practice. For PMs, design pricing changes to be explainable, consistent across channels and disclosed. EU teams face similar transparency expectations under consumer law.

PostgreSQL 19 slips a month after AI code reviewers surface too many bugs to fix

AI review raises the quality bar but can stall release plans; build that capacity into roadmaps.

PostgreSQL 19, due in late September or early October, is now expected around 19 November. Committer Peter Eisentraut says LLM-assisted code review found architectural problems in several features and a stream of edge-case bugs faster than the team could fix them. One reviewer's list held about 40 defect reports. Eight to ten significant features were pulled and pushed to PostgreSQL 20, including SQL property graph queries.

This is a useful case study for product and engineering leads. AI review is improving quality, but it also moves the bottleneck to human triage and fixing, echoing Anthropic's finding that review is now the scarce resource. Teams adopting AI review should plan for an early surge of findings, decide severity thresholds, and expect scope cuts late in a cycle. Eisentraut expects the effect to settle as tooling and processes adapt.

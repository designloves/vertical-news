Anthropic: agents now log six hours of work for every human hour inside the company

the clearest data yet on how product, design and engineering roles shift when agents do the building.

Anthropic published an internal study of how its own workforce changed as coding agents took hold. Agent work hours overtook human hours in March 2026 and reached roughly 6:1 by August. Code commits per engineer are about 5x what they were six months earlier, with no measurable difference in how often agent-written code gets reverted. The junior share of technical hires fell from 27% to 9%.

The softer findings matter more for product work. About 80% of interactive session time is spent waiting on a human, so attention and review are the bottleneck. "Meta-work" such as writing specs, monitoring and reviewing agent output rose from 10% to 22% of sessions. One product design team moved from writing UI copy to building context graphs, prompts and evals that shape agent behaviour. For Emil: specs, design-system documentation and review flows are becoming the main product surface. It is one company's self-reported data, and the authors warn against generalising.

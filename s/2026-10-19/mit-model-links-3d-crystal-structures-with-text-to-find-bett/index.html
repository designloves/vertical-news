MIT model links 3D crystal structures with text to find better materials

plain-language search over material properties could speed early material choices before simulation and test.

MIT researchers built a multimodal model that aligns 3D crystal structures with text describing composition, properties, processing and applications. Text-only language models represent crystal geometry poorly and inherit the literature's bias toward a few well-studied classes. The team addressed both by generating synthetic descriptions from papers and computational databases and training with contrastive learning.

In tests published in Nature Communications, the model beat text-only baselines at finding crystals that match a property query. For solid-state lithium-ion electrolytes, its top picks included materials other groups had independently flagged. Training on the synthetic narratives improved property prediction. For bearing and drivetrain work, the near-term value is in materials screening and literature triage, not replacing FE simulation. Results so far cover specific test cases, and claims of an "AlphaFold for materials" are aspirational.

Banks and major sites are locking out third-party AI agents

if your product depends on an agent acting on users' accounts, plan for read-only APIs and per-provider permission deals.

Schwab's CEO said on Bloomberg TV that the broker blocks outside agents, naming Meta's Muse, from executing client transactions, and that a weekly report shows dozens of blocked attempts. JPMorgan's Jamie Dimon said his bank also blocks outside agents from customer data. Schwab's model is telling: its own assistant can't trade, and external LLMs get read-only access through MCP servers.

The Verge reports the same pattern beyond finance. Amazon has blocked Muse since late September, Yelp only admits enrolled agents, and users of several consumer agents report failed tasks. With no US law on who is liable when an agent misbehaves, companies are writing the rules themselves through exclusion. For product strategy, "agent-ready" increasingly means a deliberate, permissioned interface (MCP, scoped tokens, separate rights to act) rather than agents driving your UI with a user's password. Expect access to vary site by site.

Europe's electric car sales pass 2.3 million for the year as October jumps 61%

Demand is rising just as the EU decides how far to soften the 2035 CO2 target, which shapes OEM and supplier product plans.

New figures for 18 European markets show 295,252 electric cars registered in October, up 61% on a year earlier. That takes the year-to-date total to 2,334,832, up 41%. Battery electric cars made up 32.1% of new registrations in October, against about 20% a year before, and 25.3% on average so far this year.

France and Germany together accounted for more than half of October's electric registrations. Growth was fastest in smaller markets such as Slovenia, Romania and Czechia, while Sweden rose 10%. Italy was the only market where October's share was below its average for the year. The trade group E-Mobility Europe called it a market that is accelerating, not retreating.

The numbers land in the middle of the CO2 debate. On 4 November the European Parliament's environment committee voted 48 to 30 for an 87.5% cut in new-car CO2 by 2035, with credits that could take it to 95%. The full Parliament still has to vote, and the current 100% target stands until the law changes. For engineering teams, stronger demand argues against slowing EV programmes even if the target is relaxed.

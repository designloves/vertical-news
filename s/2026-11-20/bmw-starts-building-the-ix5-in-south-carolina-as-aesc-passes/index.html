BMW starts building the iX5 in South Carolina as AESC passes 10 million 46120 cells

BMW's first US-built EV shows how large-format cylindrical cells are moving into series cars, and how cautious volume plans have become.

BMW has started series production of the electric iX5 at Spartanburg, its first EV built in the United States, at about 70 cars a day according to US reports. Suppliers have been told to plan for 60,000 units of capacity in 2027, down from an earlier 100,000. The car uses a 141 kWh battery assembled in nearby Woodruff. The electric iX7 will follow at the same plant.

Cell maker Envision AESC said it has delivered more than 10 million 46120 cylindrical cells, 46 millimetres wide and 120 tall, from plants in Jiangsu and Hebei, with a stated yield of 95%. The iX5 is the first car to use them. The cells are imported from China because AESC paused its South Carolina cell plant in 2025.

For battery and pack engineers, BMW's move puts the 46-series format into high-volume premium cars, and the supply route shows how tariffs and local-content rules still shape sourcing.

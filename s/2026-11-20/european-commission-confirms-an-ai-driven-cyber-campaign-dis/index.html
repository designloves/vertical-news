European Commission confirms an AI-driven cyber campaign disrupted its HR and staff systems

AI-assisted attacks have hit the EU's own administration, which will shape how Brussels thinks about AI security rules.

At its midday briefing on Thursday, the Commission confirmed that it had been targeted by a cyberattack carried out with AI tools. Spokesperson Thomas Regnier called it "an AI-generated cybercampaign" and said the attackers used a publicly available AI tool. He said he knew of no ransom demand. The attack was detected on 22 October and confirmed on 26 October. It became public after Politico reported that internal services had been disrupted for more than ten days.

The systems hit include Sysper, the HR platform for pay and leave, and MyPMO, which handles medical expense claims. Several internal applications were taken offline as a precaution, and most have been restored. So far no data breach affecting all staff has been found. CERT-EU and the European Data Protection Supervisor have been told. Regnier would not say who was behind the attack or whether it is over. It is the third known attack on Commission systems this year.

For teams building with AI agents, this adds to a run of cases where public tools are used to speed up attacks. Expect more pressure on security testing and logging in EU procurement.

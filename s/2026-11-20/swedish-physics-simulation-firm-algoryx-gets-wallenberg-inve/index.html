Swedish physics-simulation firm Algoryx gets Wallenberg Investments as a strategic owner

A Swedish simulation specialist is getting long-term backing to build 'physical AI' for heavy machines and robots.

Algoryx, a physics-simulation company spun out of Umeå University in 2007, said Wallenberg Investments has become a strategic minority shareholder. The amount was not disclosed. The founders and staff keep the majority. Customers include machine makers, robotics firms, industrial companies and space projects.

The company wants to lead in what it calls physical AI for heavy equipment and mobile robots: simulation detailed and fast enough to train and test machine control in virtual environments before it runs on real hardware. The money will go to hiring, computing capacity and its next product platform, and more fundraising is planned.

For virtual validation teams, Algoryx is a useful reminder that simulation-based training and testing is not only a CAE-vendor topic. Real-time multibody and contact simulation is becoming core tooling for autonomy and off-highway machinery, and Sweden has a notable player in it.

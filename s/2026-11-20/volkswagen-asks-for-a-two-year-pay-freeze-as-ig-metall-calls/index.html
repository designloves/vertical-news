Volkswagen asks for a two-year pay freeze as IG Metall calls full-day strikes at BMW, Audi and Porsche

Labour costs and strike risk are now central to German automotive planning, from OEM plants to the supplier base.

Volkswagen set out its demands on Thursday for talks on its in-house wage agreement, which covers about 120,000 workers. It wants a two-year pay freeze, an end to a bonus that staff can take as cash or time off, and a reopening of the 35-hour week, with longer hours but no extra pay. IG Metall is asking for 5%. Talks begin on 24 November in Emden, one of four plants that could close from the early 2030s, and workers have threatened strikes from January.

In the wider metal and electrical industry round, which covers 3.7 million workers, IG Metall called 24-hour warning strikes from Friday, the first full-day strikes in eight years. BMW, Audi, Porsche and suppliers are affected. Employers have offered a one-off payment in July 2027 and a permanent 1.3% rise from summer 2028; the union wants 5% plus job security.

For suppliers and engineering service firms, the two disputes add up to a risk of production gaps in early 2027 and continued pressure to move work out of Germany.

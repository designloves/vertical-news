Ford ships its first US-made LFP battery cells, built with CATL-licensed technology

A Western carmaker making low-cost LFP cells in-house, using a Chinese licence, is a model European OEMs are weighing too.

Ford says BlueOval Battery Park Michigan in Marshall is now producing lithium iron phosphate (LFP) prismatic cells. The first cells are going to Louisville, Kentucky, where they will power prototypes of the Ford Fathom, the first vehicle on Ford's new Universal EV Platform. Ford owns and runs the plant. The cell technology is licensed from CATL, and CATL staff helped with training and the production ramp-up.

The plant is smaller than first planned. Capacity was cut from 35 GWh to about 20 GWh a year, and the workforce target from 2,500 to 1,700. Fathom production is set for 2027, with a US base price of $28,350, and pack assembly in Marshall is due before then. Some cells will also go into Ford Energy's stationary storage business, with assembly in Kentucky from 2027. Ford claims the platform uses about 20% fewer parts than a conventionally developed vehicle.

For engineers, the licensing model is the part to watch. Ford gets proven cell chemistry and process know-how without a joint venture, but stays dependent on a Chinese supplier's IP. That trade-off is under debate in Europe as well.

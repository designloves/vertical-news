BMW starts series production of the iX5 in Spartanburg, and AESC passes 10 million 46120 cells

Large cylindrical cells are now in volume production for premium SUVs, but early volumes are below plan.

BMW has started series production of the electric iX5 at its plant in Spartanburg, South Carolina. It is built on the same line as its petrol, diesel and hybrid models. The car uses a 141 kWh battery assembled at BMW's new plant in nearby Woodruff. It is the first electric BMW built in the US. Output starts at about 70 cars a day. According to electrive, suppliers were told to plan for 60,000 units in 2027, down from an earlier 100,000.

On the same day, Envision AESC said it has delivered more than 10 million cylindrical cells in the 46120 format (46 mm wide, 120 mm tall). The cells come from its Chinese plants in Jiangsu and Hebei, which started series production in July. BMW is the main customer, and the iX7 is the second BMW model confirmed to use them.

For drivetrain and battery engineers, this is one of the first large-scale tests of 46-series cells in a premium vehicle. The lower volume forecast shows that demand, not the technology, is now the main risk.

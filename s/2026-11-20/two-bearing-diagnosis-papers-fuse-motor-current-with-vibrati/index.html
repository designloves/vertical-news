Two bearing-diagnosis papers fuse motor current with vibration and adapt to new operating conditions

Both target a real problem in EV drives: diagnosing bearings that run across many speeds and loads, with sensors already in the motor.

A peer-reviewed paper in Processes from Xinjiang University combines vibration and motor-current signals in a two-branch neural network, then links the predicted fault to a knowledge graph of causes and maintenance actions. On a public bearing dataset it reports 98.4% overall accuracy and 100% recognition of combined inner and outer ring faults. Fusing both signals beat either one alone.

A second peer-reviewed paper, in Machines from Anhui University of Science and Technology, tackles the change in operating conditions. It mixes wavelet-based deep features with seven classic statistics and adjusts the model so a diagnoser trained at one speed and load still works at another, using only confident predictions on the new data. It reports average accuracies of 98.7% and 99.5% across six transfer tasks on the Paderborn and HUST datasets.

Both were tested only on lab benchmark data, so these are incremental steps, not field-proven tools. Still, current-based sensing suits EV motors, where current is already measured, and the knowledge-graph step moves toward diagnosis that explains itself to maintenance staff.

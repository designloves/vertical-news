Comau will embed Nvidia's Isaac Sim digital twins directly into its robot controllers

Putting simulation inside the controller is a step toward digital twins that run alongside the physical system and stay in sync with it.

Turin-based Comau announced at GTC Berlin that it has become an Nvidia-certified AI partner and will build Isaac Sim, the SimReady asset library and Jetson edge compute into its next-generation robot motion controllers. The stated goal is to narrow the sim-to-real gap, meaning the difference between how a robot behaves in simulation and in the cell. Nvidia also made Isaac Lab 3.0 generally available at the event.

Comau joins ABB, FANUC, Universal Robots, Siemens and Festo in tying its tools to Isaac and Omniverse. ABB has reported cutting positioning errors from 8–15 mm to about 0.5 mm with its Omniverse-based RobotStudio. Comau gave no release or customer timeline, but its roadmap points toward bidirectional digital twins.

For test and validation engineers, the trend is that simulation models are moving out of the office and onto the shop floor. That raises new questions about model validity, versioning and correlation with measured data.

GM aims to halve vehicle development time with AI and virtual engineering

A major OEM is putting numbers on simulation-first development, the same shift you work on in virtual validation.

At an investor-style event in New York, GM laid out a plan to cut its four-to-five-year vehicle development cycle to roughly two years. The aim is to close the speed gap with Chinese automakers. Chief product officer Sterling Anderson said the company is moving from slow, sequential engineering to parallel development backed by virtual testing and AI.

GM gave concrete examples. Engineers virtually evaluated more than 300,000 designs for new lithium manganese-rich battery packs before building a prototype, which took the team from concept to formal approval in a month. GM says aerodynamics runs that took 60 hours now take about 60 seconds, and structural analysis that took months now takes hours. It is also building digital models of vehicles and plants from decades of component, materials and manufacturing data, which it sees as its edge.

Analysts note that execution is the open question. The pattern itself matches Mercedes' PLM.AI and Porsche's targets from earlier this week: fast surrogate models to explore the design space, with physical tests kept for confirmation.

European Parliament's environment committee votes to soften the 2035 car CO2 target

If the compromise survives, combustion and hybrid cars stay on sale in the EU after 2035, which reshapes powertrain and bearing demand planning.

On 4 November the Parliament's Environment Committee backed a compromise between the centre-right, centre-left and liberal groups on the EU's car CO2 rules. The vote was 48 in favour, 30 against and 2 abstentions. It eases the Commission's plan to end new combustion-engine sales in 2035.

The required fleet cut in 2035 would be 95% against 2021, but makers could earn up to 7.5 points back by using low-carbon EU-made steel, so the effective figure could fall to 87.5% (the two outlets frame this slightly differently). Vans would face 35% in 2030 and 80% in 2035. Vehicles running only on eligible renewable fuels could count as zero-emission, and small EU-built electric cars would earn extra credits. The rapporteur said combustion cars could still be registered without offsetting.

The full Parliament is expected to vote in December, and member states must then agree, so the text can still change. For OEMs and suppliers, a longer tail for hybrids and combustion engines keeps demand for conventional drivetrain components alive, while the steel credit and small-EV bonuses could favour European supply chains.

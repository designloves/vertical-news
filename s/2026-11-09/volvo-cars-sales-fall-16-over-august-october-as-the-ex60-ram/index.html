Volvo Cars sales fall 16% over August–October as the EX60 ramp-up limits deliveries

A Swedish OEM's demand picture: electric sales are growing while China, the US and plug-in hybrids drag.

Volvo Cars sold 141,656 cars globally in the three months to October, down 16% from 168,967 a year earlier. Fully electric sales rose 27% to 48,660 cars, or 34% of the total. Plug-in hybrids fell 30% to 28,857, and mild hybrids and combustion cars fell 28% to 64,139.

Volvo cited weak demand and local competition in China, and in the US record-low consumer sentiment and removed incentives for electric and plug-in cars. In Europe, the EX60 has more than 50,000 orders, but deliveries are held back by the production ramp-up. Its commercial chief said Volvo is prioritising stabilising the business over chasing volume in China. Volvo reports rolling three-month figures only, so single-month numbers are third-party estimates.

For suppliers, the mix shift toward battery-electric and the EX60 ramp are the numbers to watch for drivetrain and e-axle volumes.

Schaeffler plans 3,460 job cuts and five European plant closures

One of the biggest bearing and drivetrain suppliers is shrinking its European footprint, which matters for sourcing, capacity and customer contacts.

Schaeffler said it will cut 3,460 jobs and close five plants in Europe, four in Germany and one in Belgium. In total 16 locations are affected. The company blames weak automotive demand, higher production costs and competition from Chinese makers.

About 2,660 of the cuts fall in Germany by 2028, roughly 9% of its core German workforce. Schaeffler expects annual savings of about €300 million by 2030 against one-off costs of about €480 million, mostly booked in 2026. Board pay will be cut by 10% over two years. The CEO did not rule out further cuts after 2028 and pointed to German labour costs as a key problem. Dividends will continue.

For engineers at OEMs and bearing users, the plant list and timing will determine which product lines move. The report comes from a single trade outlet, so the company's own release is worth checking for the affected sites.

EU and UK consider delaying the 10% EV tariff due in January again

Without a deal, most EVs traded between the EU and UK would face duty from 1 January, affecting pricing and sourcing plans for 2027.

The Financial Times reports that the European Commission is considering postponing the stricter rules of origin for electric cars under the Brexit trade deal, and that the UK government supports a delay. From 2027, EVs need 55% regional content, with higher thresholds for battery cells, modules and packs. Without a change, a 10% tariff would apply on 1 January.

ACEA estimates that about 82% of the roughly 520,000 EVs and vans the EU will send to the UK in 2027 would fail the rules, adding about €1.47 billion in duty. Options include a one-year delay or a two-year scheme that lets manufacturers offset non-compliant exports with compliant sales. Europe's chemical industry, which has invested in cathode materials, opposes another delay.

Serious talks are expected after the EU–UK summit on 20 November. Until then, OEMs and battery suppliers are planning for both outcomes.

QuantumScape opens its solid-state battery platform to other cell developers

Licensing rather than building cells could speed up how quickly carmakers can test solid-state batteries in their own vehicle programmes.

QuantumScape launched Osprey, an open platform based on its ceramic separator and its anode-free lithium-metal cell design. Partners can use QuantumScape's own sulphide cathode or bring their own cathode materials and supply chain. Development work with car companies has already started at the early cell stage.

Early results, mostly from small two-layer test cells, show about 1,000 full cycles with roughly 80% capacity left. They also show 10–80% charging in about five minutes at room temperature and normal pressure, and energy density of up to about 1,100 Wh/L. The first commercial cell, the 5 Ah QSE-5, offers more than 844 Wh/L. Volkswagen's PowerCo is the main partner, and Honda joined this year.

Big questions remain. Carmakers want larger prismatic cells, and production has only been shown at lab scale. QuantumScape plans to reach gigawatt-hour volumes through licensing, not its own factories.

Four US nonprofits sue five AI labs to stop frontier model training

Even if it fails, the case tests whether state consumer-protection law can be used against AI developers, a route that could spread.

A group made up of Irreplaceable Education Fund, Public Citizen, Heat Initiative and Alliance for Secure AI has filed a civil complaint in San Francisco Superior Court against Anthropic, Google, Meta, OpenAI and SpaceX, which owns xAI. The groups are asking for an emergency injunction to stop the training and deployment of next-generation models until the companies can show the technology stays under human control.

The case relies on California's unfair competition law. The plaintiffs argue the companies have endangered public health and interfered with people's use of their property, including computer systems. They also want federal inspections, strict liability and a new AI regulator, and say courts should act while Congress has not. The article included no response from the companies.

The request for a full halt is far-reaching, and no court has yet ruled on it. Even so, the case adds to the legal and political pressure on frontier labs just as Anthropic prepares to list its shares next week.

Jensen Huang resigns as Nvidia CEO; CFO Colette Kress takes over on an interim basis

The company that supplies most of the world's AI compute now has a caretaker leader, which adds uncertainty to GPU roadmaps, pricing and AI data-centre financing.

Nvidia said on Thursday that Jensen Huang has stepped down as president, CEO and board member with immediate effect. Chief financial officer Colette Kress becomes interim CEO and keeps her CFO role until a permanent successor is found. The board will look at internal and external candidates. Harvey C. Jones, a former CEO and executive chairman of chip-design software maker Synopsys, becomes Nvidia's first board chair. The company's statement gave no reason for the resignation, which follows a week of reports about Huang's private relationships and a reported link between one of them and Chinese intelligence.

CNBC reports the shares first fell more than 13% before recovering part of the loss. Nvidia says operations, customer commitments and the product roadmap will continue without interruption, and third-quarter results are still due on 17 November.

The change also hangs over a non-binding plan, agreed in August with Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR, to raise more than $500 billion for AI infrastructure using Nvidia chips as collateral. No final deals have been signed. For teams planning compute, the short-term effect is mainly uncertainty, not changes to supply.

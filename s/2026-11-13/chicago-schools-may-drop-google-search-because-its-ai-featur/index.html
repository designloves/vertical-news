Chicago schools may drop Google Search because its AI features can't be switched off

Bundling AI into a core product with no opt-out can cost customers, and buyers are starting to push back.

Chicago Public Schools said it is looking for ways to hide the AI overviews at the top of Google search results. If it cannot, it will consider other search providers. This follows Google's statement earlier this month that schools blocking its AI features would lose access to search, because AI is now built into the product. About 30 people protested outside Google's Chicago office on Wednesday.

The district has already blocked student access to chatbots such as ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini, while approving a few AI tools for staff. New York City has banned student-facing AI up to eighth grade. Google says it is improving search for classrooms, including a pilot of 'Guided Learning', which takes students through problems step by step.

For product leaders, the lesson is about packaging. When an AI feature becomes impossible to remove, regulated or cautious customers such as schools, public bodies and some manufacturers may reject the whole product, not just the feature.

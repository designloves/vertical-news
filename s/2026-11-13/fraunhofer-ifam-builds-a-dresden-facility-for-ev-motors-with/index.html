Fraunhofer IFAM builds a Dresden facility for EV motors without rare earths

European e-drive development is moving towards magnet-free and copper-light designs, which changes motor, cooling and bearing requirements.

Fraunhofer IFAM is setting up a research facility in Dresden called SIREN to develop electric drives that use fewer critical raw materials. It has more than €2 million in funding from the state of Saxony and the EU's regional development fund, running until the end of 2028.

The work focuses on high-speed reluctance motors and ferrite-magnet motors, neither of which needs rare earths. It uses a sintering-based additive process that can combine several metals in one part. Examples include laminations that mix magnetic and non-magnetic materials, and aluminium windings with built-in cooling channels instead of copper. Saxon small and medium-sized firms will get access to the processes, and Fraunhofer calls the combination unique in Europe.

High-speed reluctance machines put more load on rotor bearings and lubrication, and aluminium windings change thermal design. Drivetrain and bearing engineers will need to validate these effects as the designs move toward vehicles.

Russian attacks on Ukrainian plants cause new wiring harness shortages at BMW and Škoda

Single-source harness supply from Ukraine is again a weak point for European car plants, four years after the 2022 shutdowns.

Handelsblatt reports that German supplier Kromberg & Schubert's wiring harness plants near Zhytomyr and Lutsk in Ukraine have been damaged by repeated Russian attacks. The Lutsk site stopped work in mid-October. BMW confirmed parts shortages without naming the supplier. Its Regensburg plant (X1, X2, iX1, iX2) is running again and Leipzig is expected to return to normal soon, but Mini in Oxford may take longer.

Škoda had weeks of shortages at Mladá Boleslav that cut extra and night shifts for the Enyaq, Elroq and Kodiaq. Production there is now back to normal. Mercedes-Benz, Audi and Porsche report no disruption, even though the Lutsk plant also supplies them.

Wiring harnesses are built for specific vehicles and are labour-intensive, so switching supplier takes months. The same supplier's plant in Morocco was hit by floods in spring. Purchasing and launch teams will see this as another reason to qualify second sources and to simplify harness designs, a key goal of zonal software-defined vehicle architectures.

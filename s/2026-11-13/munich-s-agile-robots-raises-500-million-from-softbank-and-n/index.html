Munich's Agile Robots raises $500 million from SoftBank and Nvidia at an $8.5 billion valuation

Europe's largest industrial-robot start-up is getting money to scale production of robot cells and humanoids for car plants and logistics.

Agile Robots, based in Munich, has raised $500 million in a round led by SoftBank, with Nvidia, Hitachi's venture arm and Deutsche Telekom's T.Capital also investing. The round values the company at $8.5 billion and brings its total funding to about $2 billion.

The company makes robot arms, robot hands and grippers, driverless transport systems, complete production cells and a humanoid robot called Agile ONE. It reports more than 3,500 employees, over 20,000 installed systems and customers in automotive, electronics, healthcare and logistics. Last month it started a logistics project with Audi. A new plant in Fürstenfeldbruck near Munich is designed to build up to 10,000 humanoid robots a year. The new money will fund US expansion, new industrial applications and more manufacturing in Bavaria. CEO Zhaopeng Chen has not ruled out a later stock market listing.

For OEMs and suppliers, this means a well-funded European option for AI-driven automation, competing with US and Chinese humanoid makers.

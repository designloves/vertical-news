Volkswagen scraps its 'Gamechanger' mega-casting project in Wolfsburg

Europe's largest carmaker is dropping a key cost-cutting production method for its next EVs to save cash, which affects suppliers, tooling plans and German jobs.

Volkswagen will not go ahead with 'Gamechanger' in its current form, according to Handelsblatt, and Automobilwoche has confirmed the report. The project was meant to cut the cost of building SSP-platform EVs such as the electric Golf and ID. Roc by using large single-piece castings and more pre-assembly. It centred on a mega-casting hall in Wolfsburg where construction was due to start next week. The hall alone would have cost hundreds of millions of euros, and the whole project a low single-digit billion sum.

The cars will still be built, but with conventional methods. The reason is budget pressure: investment requests exceed the €135 billion VW has set aside for 2027–2031, and Audi's planned US plant (about $4 billion for its first phase) is not included in that figure. Works council head Daniela Cavallo called the decision a breach of the 2024 jobs agreement and is blocking the planned move of combustion Golf production to Mexico.

For engineers, this is a reminder that giga-casting is far from settled in Europe. Body-in-white design, joining methods and crash validation for SSP will now follow more traditional routes.

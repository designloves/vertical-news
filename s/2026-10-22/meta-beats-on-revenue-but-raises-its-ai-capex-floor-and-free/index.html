Meta beats on revenue but raises its AI capex floor, and free cash flow turns negative

Ad revenue is paying for AI infrastructure at a scale that pushes even Meta into negative free cash flow, and it shapes how Meta prices and ships Muse.

Meta reported Q3 revenue of $64.6B, up 26% year on year and above consensus. Net income fell to $9.9B, hit by a $10B youth-safety settlement charge. Q3 capex was $34.9B, and full-year guidance moved to $135–145B, raising the low end by $5B. Free cash flow was negative $2.1B, against $10.6B a year earlier.

Meta also announced a non-voting Class C share, issued as a stock dividend and taking effect 19 January 2027. It effectively works like a two-for-one split and gives Meta a currency for funding and compensation without diluting control. Reality Labs is expected to lose about $15B in 2027 as investment shifts to AI. 2027 capex has not been guided yet, but Meta says it will keep investing aggressively.

For strategy work, the picture is that the largest consumer platforms are betting their margins on AI assistants. Expect aggressive bundling of Muse into Meta's apps and more pressure on smaller assistant products to differentiate on trust or niche depth.

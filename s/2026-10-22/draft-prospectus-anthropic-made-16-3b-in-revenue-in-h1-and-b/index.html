Draft prospectus: Anthropic made $16.3B in revenue in H1 and booked an $80.7B net loss

The first hard look at frontier-lab economics. Compute commitments and customer concentration will shape pricing and API stability for everyone building on these models.

The Wall Street Journal has seen a draft IPO prospectus. Anthropic has not confirmed it. The draft shows $16.3B in revenue for the first half of 2026, up from $1.1B a year earlier. Compute spending was nearly $9B, more than the whole of 2025. The $80.7B net loss is mostly a non-cash charge of about $72B tied to convertible notes and other financing instruments.

The filing reportedly lists roughly $450B in cloud agreements over a decade ($111B Google, $110B AWS, $31B Microsoft), more than $600B in total infrastructure commitments, and possibly up to $750B including deals still being negotiated. Two customers made up about 20% of revenue. Investor talk has moved from about $2T toward about $1.5T, and Bloomberg reports a target of listing before Thanksgiving.

For product teams, the takeaway is that model pricing and capacity are tied to very large fixed commitments. Expect continued pushes on usage-based plans, enterprise lock-in and price tiers, and treat any single-vendor dependency as a risk to manage.

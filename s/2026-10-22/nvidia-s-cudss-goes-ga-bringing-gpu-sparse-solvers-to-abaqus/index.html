Nvidia's cuDSS goes GA, bringing GPU sparse solvers to Abaqus, STAR-CCM+ and COMSOL

The sparse linear solve is often the bottleneck in implicit FE and multiphysics runs, so this directly affects how many bearing and structural variants you can run per day.

At GTC Berlin, Nvidia made cuDSS, its CUDA-X library for direct sparse linear solves, generally available. It moves the factorise-and-solve step of large sparse systems onto GPUs, scales from a workstation to a cluster, and can be called from C, C++, Python and Fortran.

Vendor numbers: Dassault Systèmes reports 8–10x faster structural and electromagnetic solves in Abaqus. Applied Materials reports up to 10x over CPU-only. ZEISS SMT, using COMSOL, reports 4x on an EUV plasma benchmark. Siemens is using it in Simcenter STAR-CCM+ for fluid-structure interaction, Synopsys in Sentaurus, and Cadence in Celsius for thermal stress and warpage. In the keynote, Dassault and Volkswagen also showed a design agent adapting a Golf door for the Tiguan, where a SIMULIA-based simulation agent flagged a vibration problem before the design was refined.

For OEM virtual validation, the practical question is whether your solver versions and licences expose the GPU path, and how much of your run time actually sits in the linear solve rather than contact iterations or I/O. If a test run shows a real speedup, you could move from a handful of design points to a proper DoE within the same time window.

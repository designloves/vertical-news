UN talks on killer robots end without a ban, a day after Bessent says Trump and Xi agreed humans must decide on military AI

Rules on human control of autonomous systems shape what defence and dual-use suppliers, including automotive ones, can build and sell.

The Geneva conference of the 128 states in the UN Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons ended early on Saturday with only a minimal deal. Almost 100 countries wanted to start negotiating a binding ban on weapons that can kill without a human making the final decision. The United States, Israel, Belarus and the UK opposed a negotiating mandate. Government experts will instead keep working on elements of a possible agreement and recommend by 2028 whether it should become a treaty. Campaign groups called the result disappointing.

On Friday, US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told CNBC that Donald Trump and Xi Jinping agreed in Shenzhen that humans should always be involved in military decisions that use AI, and that no major operation should start without a person in the loop. China has not confirmed this, and no text was published.

Taken together, the two events show that governments talk about human control but do not yet want binding rules.

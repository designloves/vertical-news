Dragos plants two million bait secrets in AI prompts and finds three providers breached and resellers reading prompts

If your product or agents send code, keys or customer data to a model through a router or reseller, that data may not stay private.

HD Moore, chief research officer at industrial security firm Dragos, sent about two million prompts to widely used AI services between July and November. Each contained unique bait: AWS keys, URLs, DNS names and passwords. His team then watched for anyone using them. Data from at least three AI hosting providers was exposed, the earliest in mid-July. In the largest case a couple of hundred AWS keys were stolen and used for tens of thousands of API calls.

Cheap resellers on an inference marketplace could read and keep customer prompts simply by turning on logging. A Chinese AI lab fetched secret URLs from more than half of the prompts sent to its models, including about two-thirds of those flagged for zero data retention. One link sent to a US provider with that flag turned up in a Chinese company chat 24 days later. Some routes passed through companies not listed on the advertised path.

Dragos advises self-hosting for sensitive work, contracting directly with first-tier providers, avoiding reseller marketplaces and seeding your own canary credentials into agent pipelines.

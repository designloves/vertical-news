onsemi launches its fourth-generation EliteSiC M4 chips for EV traction inverters, with Schaeffler backing

Lower inverter losses mean more range or smaller batteries, and less heat to manage in e-axles and drive units.

onsemi introduced EliteSiC M4, its fourth generation of silicon carbide power chips, built on a trench design. The company claims up to 30% higher power density and up to 35% lower conduction and switching losses than the previous generation. It says specific on-resistance is 20–50% lower, and switching improves by 67% in hard-switching and 50% in soft-switching use. These are chip-level figures and do not translate directly into drivetrain savings.

A 750 V bare die for traction inverters is available now, with 1,200 V versions due from 2027. The chips fit existing packages, including top-side-cooled surface-mount parts and power modules, and onsemi plans to qualify them for 185°C and above. Schaeffler publicly backed the platform; its executive Harald Hartel sees gains in range, heat and installation space.

For drivetrain engineers, higher temperature ratings and lower losses change cooling and packaging trade-offs in integrated e-axles, including the thermal load on nearby bearings and seals.

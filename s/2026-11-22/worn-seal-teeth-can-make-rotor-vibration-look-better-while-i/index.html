Worn seal teeth can make rotor vibration look better while instability grows, study finds

Condition monitoring that only tracks the 1× vibration level may miss a developing rotor–bearing instability.

Researchers at the University of Shanghai for Science and Technology and Southeast University in Nanjing studied how mushroom-shaped wear on labyrinth seal teeth changes a rotor running on plain journal bearings. The peer-reviewed paper in Lubricants uses CFD to compute seal stiffness and damping at different wear depths, then feeds these into a finite-element rotor model.

Wear of 0.6 mm raised leakage by about 295%. Effective seal damping fell by 43 to 68%, resonance amplitude grew by 17.8%, and the onset of oil whirl dropped from 3,650 to about 3,530 rpm. At 4,800 rpm, synchronous vibration fell by 27.6% while subsynchronous vibration rose by 39.9%.

The practical point is that a worn seal can make the usual once-per-revolution vibration look healthier while the machine moves closer to instability. Monitoring rules should also track subsynchronous content. The method, feeding component coefficients from CFD into a rotordynamics model, is also a useful pattern for virtual validation of turbomachinery and high-speed e-drive rotors.

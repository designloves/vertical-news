Thinking Machines releases Inkling 2 Mini, an open-weights coding model that runs on a 32 GB laptop

Strong local models give teams an option for sensitive code and design files that should not leave the machine.

Thinking Machines Lab released Inkling 2 Mini with open weights. It is a mixture-of-experts model with 26.4 billion parameters, of which about 3.4 billion are active per token. It is a looped transformer: the middle layers run three times for each token, which gives more compute without more stored weights. The whole model needs about 20 GB of memory and runs on Macs with at least 32 GB of RAM through Ollama, and on Nvidia DGX Spark.

The lab reports 79.2% on SWE-bench Verified, 48.2% on DeepSWE v1.1, 71.6% on Terminal Bench 2.1 and 46.5% on the Vals Index, which it says leads open models under 100 billion parameters. It trails Alibaba's Qwen 3.8 27B on some chat and instruction tasks. These figures are vendor-reported. Weights are on Hugging Face, with support in vLLM, SGLang, llama.cpp and the lab's Tinker fine-tuning service.

Given the Dragos findings on leaking prompts, a capable model that runs locally is a practical choice for frontend and tooling work on private code.

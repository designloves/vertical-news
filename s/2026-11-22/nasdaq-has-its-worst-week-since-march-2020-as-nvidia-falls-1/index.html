Nasdaq has its worst week since March 2020 as Nvidia falls 14%, and Taiwan bans most short-selling

A sharp repricing of AI stocks can quickly hit funding, AI budgets and the price of compute for both software teams and suppliers.

US stocks fell hard on Friday. The S&P 500 dropped 3.7% to 6,910, its seventh loss in a row, and closed in correction territory, about 10% below its record. The Nasdaq Composite fell 5.3% and is now down 2.7% for 2026. Over the week the Nasdaq lost about 10% and the S&P 500 8.6%. Nvidia fell about 14% after Reuters reported that the SEC is looking into possible accounting issues at the company. AMD and Micron each fell around 13–14%, and Anthropic lost 8.5% on its second day of trading.

The damage spread to Asia. Taiwan's Taiex fell 6.2% on Friday to its lowest close in almost seven months, about 28% below its October peak. On Saturday, Taiwan's financial regulator banned short-selling of any stock that fell in the previous session, from Monday, and raised short-selling margin from 130% to 150%. It is the first blanket ban since 2015.

For product and engineering teams, the practical risk is tighter AI budgets and more cautious investors. Vendors whose pricing depends on cheap capital may change terms.

Tesla starts rolling out FSD v15 with a new vision encoder and claims 25 times fewer critical interventions

A single vision model shared across cars and robots is Tesla's bet for reaching unsupervised driving.

Tesla has begun rolling out version 15 of its FSD software in North America. The main change is a new vision encoder, shared across FSD, Robotaxi and the Optimus robot and trained on millions of internet videos. Tesla claims it improves the critical intervention rate by 25 times, but has not published the underlying rate. Electrek's own extrapolation, not a Tesla figure, puts it at around 50,000 miles between critical interventions.

Other changes include a single end-to-end visualisation called Reality View, an end-to-end navigation model, better detection of signs, debris, potholes and standing water, and handling of speed cameras and school zones. Tesla says its current AI4 computer is sufficient.

For ADAS and validation teams, the release highlights a familiar gap: large claimed improvements without public, comparable metrics. A US NHTSA investigation from October 2025 is still open, and European tests have found the system speeding in 30 km/h zones.

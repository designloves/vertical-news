ChatGPT Space reaches Plus users, with pages and slides that refresh themselves from connected apps

Self-updating documents built by an assistant compete directly with dashboards, wikis and slide tools.

OpenAI has rolled out ChatGPT Space to Plus subscribers aged 18 and over. Space lets users create interactive pages and presentations, invite collaborators, and set pages and slides to update automatically from connected apps and tools. It replaces the old Library as the home for pages, slides, images and uploaded files, and pages can be edited on the web, desktop and mobile apps. Space first launched for higher tiers at the end of September.

Sharing is restricted: a viewer needs permission and a paid ChatGPT plan with Space. Free users cannot open shared pages, and there are no public links.

For designers and product teams the interesting pattern is the living document: an artefact that an assistant keeps current from live data, rather than a static export. It raises familiar UX questions about provenance and trust, such as showing when content last refreshed and which source changed it. The paid-only sharing model also limits how far such pages can travel outside an organisation.

OpenAI says new safeguards cut attempts to copy its models' reasoning from 54% success to 0.2%

Model makers are hiding more of how models reason, which affects how far teams can inspect or audit AI output.

OpenAI described how it fights adversarial distillation, where someone collects a model's outputs without permission to train a copy, then runs that copy without safety limits. OpenAI says exact reasoning traces are the most valuable target: in an internal cyber study, student models trained on full traces beat those trained on summaries.

Its defences include training models to refuse extraction attempts, classifiers on inputs and outputs, API rules that drop hidden reasoning if earlier messages are edited, and blocking older models from reading newer models' reasoning. In red-team tests across six attacker models, the success rate for extracting reasoning from an earlier request fell from 54.0% on GPT-6 Astra to 0.2% on GPT-6.2 Astra. For the current request it fell from 22.9% to 0.9%. Weekly enforcement now hits thousands to tens of thousands of accounts.

For developers, this means less visibility into model reasoning through the API, and a higher risk of account action for heavy automated use that looks like scraping.

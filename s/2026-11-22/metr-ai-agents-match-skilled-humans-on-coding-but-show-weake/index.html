METR: AI agents match skilled humans on coding but show weaker judgment when feedback is slow or unclear

It is a useful guide to which product and engineering tasks you can hand to agents today, and which still need expert review.

Evaluation group METR published its quarterly update on public models up to Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Astra. The length of tasks agents can finish keeps doubling roughly every 3.6 months. On tasks with clear, automatic scoring, the doubling time is only 1.65 months, though METR says that figure is uncertain. Models often match or beat skilled humans on coding and optimisation work with fast feedback.

Results are weaker elsewhere. On a benchmark that rates philosophical critiques, every public model stayed below experts who spent 15 minutes on each item. When asked to design new evaluation tasks, agents produced nothing METR found especially useful. Model judges agreed with human graders on weaker models' work but overrated work from models as strong as or stronger than themselves. Costlier tasks gave about 2.9 times shorter time horizons.

METR says public evidence cannot rule out a sharp speed-up in AI research within a couple of years. For teams, the lesson is to keep humans reviewing work where quality is hard to measure, and not to rely on one model to grade another.

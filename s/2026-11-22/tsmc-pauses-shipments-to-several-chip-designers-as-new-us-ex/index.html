TSMC pauses shipments to several chip designers as new US export rules reach beyond AI chips

Export controls now touch ordinary consumer and automotive-class chips at 16 nm and below, not just AI accelerators.

Taiwan's TSMC has held back shipments to several chip developers, both Chinese and non-Chinese, while it reviews compliance with tighter US Commerce Department rules that took effect on Thursday 19 November, Nikkei Asia reported. Under the new rules, foundries must treat chips made at 14/16 nm or more advanced nodes as presumptively restricted unless the designer is on a US-approved list.

The rules also close a route where independent packaging and testing companies certified that a design was not an AI chip and therefore fell outside the controls. Executives told Nikkei the effect spreads to mobile and consumer chips and to designers outside China, while Chinese firms are pushed back towards 28 nm and 40 nm processes.

For automotive buyers the immediate exposure is limited, since many vehicle microcontrollers use older nodes. But ADAS processors, infotainment chips and zone controllers increasingly use advanced nodes, so purchasing and supply-chain teams should check which suppliers' designs are caught by the review.

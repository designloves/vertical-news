Google tells schools that keeping Search means keeping AI Overviews, and big US districts push back

Bundling AI into a core product with no off switch is meeting resistance from large institutional customers.

Google has told schools that its Search product comes with AI Overviews and cannot be separated, Fortune reports. Earlier this month Google reversed course on its Classroom assistant, making Gemini off by default for school student accounts from early 2027 unless administrators switch it on. But Search stays tied to AI summaries, and Google's education vice president said students at schools that ban AI will not have access to search.

Three of the four largest US school districts are resisting. New York City, with about 1.3 million Chromebooks, has disabled Gemini and AI Mode on student devices. Los Angeles has blocked generative AI for about 378,000 students and asked Google for an off switch, and Chicago says it is exploring all options. Alphabet did not comment.

For product managers, it is a clear case of the trade-off between pushing AI adoption through defaults and giving buyers control. Enterprise and public-sector customers increasingly expect admin settings that let them turn individual AI features off.

Italy approves Tesla's supervised driver assistance, bringing it to about 30% of the EU population

National recognition of a Dutch approval is becoming Europe's route for advanced Level 2 systems while an EU-wide vote stalls.

Italy's transport ministry has given provisional approval to Tesla Assisted Driving, the European name for FSD (Supervised), making Italy the twelfth European country to allow it. The approval builds on the Dutch RDW's type approval from April, granted under Article 39 of the EU type-approval regulation, which lets other member states recognise it nationally. With Italy's roughly 59 million people, close to 30% of the EU population can now use the system, about double the share before. Tesla has not given a rollout date.

The system remains SAE Level 2, with the driver responsible at all times. Italy approved the version with the stricter driver monitoring that RDW signed off on 16 November, including hands on the wheel above 130 km/h, and Tesla has cut the allowed speed margin over the limit from 50% to 10%.

An EU-wide approval needs a qualified majority in the TCMV committee, and that vote has slipped to December at the earliest. France has refused, Spain has not recognised the Dutch approval and Sweden is still reviewing it. For ADAS teams, the case shows how far national recognition can stretch type-approval rules.

Anthropic and NYU mathematicians claim an AI-written proof that Navier–Stokes flows can blow up without any outside force

If it holds, AI will have helped settle a Millennium Prize problem at the heart of every CFD code engineers use.

Tristan Buckmaster of New York University and Levent Alpöge of Anthropic posted a 79-page paper on Friday claiming that smooth solutions to the three-dimensional Navier–Stokes equations can break down in finite time with no external forcing. The authors say this disproves two of the statements in the Clay Mathematics Institute's Millennium Prize problem, which carries a one-million-dollar prize. The blow-up rate they find matches a lower bound Jean Leray identified in 1934. A companion paper claims a similar result for the Euler equations.

According to the paper, Claude wrote the first force-free version of the proof, and the authors then rewrote it with help from Claude and OpenAI's Codex. The argument is backed by a Lean 4 formalisation of about 600,000 lines. Important caveats remain: the key numerical certificate has so far been published only as a cryptographic hash, the interval-arithmetic step is not formalised, the code was patched on release day, and there is no peer review yet.

Daily CFD work is unaffected. But it is a strong sign that AI can contribute to deep analysis, and it follows a Claude-assisted Riemann Hypothesis claim by another Anthropic mathematician.

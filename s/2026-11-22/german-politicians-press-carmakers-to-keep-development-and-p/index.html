German politicians press carmakers to keep development and production at home before VW pay talks

Political pressure is rising on OEMs that plan to move work out of Germany, with knock-on effects for suppliers.

Federal Transport Minister Steffen Bilger told the Neue Osnabrücker Zeitung that Germany must remain the home base for vehicle development and production. He called the end of the EU's 2035 combustion-engine ban a political achievement and said carmakers should now commit to German sites in return. He blamed high costs on labour, energy and bureaucracy and called for a European response to subsidised Chinese cars.

Lower Saxony's premier Olaf Lies, who sits on Volkswagen's supervisory board, rejected management's planned job cuts just before VW's pay talks with IG Metall begin on Tuesday. He argued that a German company cannot be saved by giving up production in Germany, and proposed more automation, robotics, better workflows and costs in line with other European plants instead.

For suppliers in Germany and the Nordics, the statements signal that site decisions at VW and others will be politically contested over the coming months, while pay talks and strikes continue.

Robot-guided eddy current probe designed by simulation finds hidden defects in turbine blades

The same simulate-then-automate approach could speed up eddy current inspection of bearing rings, gears and shafts.

A team from Nanchang Hangkong University and AECC Power Corporation in Zhuzhou has published a peer-reviewed study in the Journal of Nondestructive Evaluation on automating eddy current inspection of aero-engine turbine blades. They used electromagnetic finite-element simulation to choose the probe's core material and coil design, removing the blind zones seen in manual inspection.

The probe was mounted on a robot with a turntable, and scan paths were tuned on a notched reference specimen to see how robot speed and defect depth and orientation affect the signal. The abstract reports no numbers, but the paper's figures show signal amplitude rising from about 180 to 800 mV as defect depth increases from 0.1 to 0.5 mm, with the best signal-to-noise ratio at 20 mm/s. The system was then validated on real blades with subsurface cold-shut defects.

For bearing and drivetrain plants, it is a practical template: design the probe in simulation, tune scan parameters on reference parts, then automate inspection of curved precision surfaces.

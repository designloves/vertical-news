US Senate votes 62–33 to make data centres pay for the grid upgrades they trigger

Power costs for AI computing are becoming a political issue, which will feed into cloud and AI pricing.

The US Senate passed the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act by 62 votes to 33, with fifteen Democrats voting yes. The bill directs the energy regulator FERC to write rules that charge all data centres, new and existing, for the grid upgrades they require and for their use of the existing network. It also speeds up environmental reviews for power lines, solar and wind farms, mines and drilling.

Analysis by Grid Strategies, cited by Senator Martin Heinrich's office, estimates data centres would contribute about 55 billion dollars to transmission costs over ten years, roughly 35 billion more than under current policy. Supporters project household bills falling by 50 to 78 dollars a year by 2040. The bill now goes to the House.

For product teams, the direction matters more than the detail: the energy cost of running AI features is being pushed back onto the operators, and some of it will reach customers through cloud prices.

Dassault launches SIMULIA R2027x with physics-grounded AI and an agentic simulation assistant

Shows how CAE vendors plan to put AI in early design without giving up solver-verified physics.

Dassault Systèmes has announced SIMULIA R2027x on its 3DEXPERIENCE platform. Its theme is AI anchored to validated solvers rather than to general language models. The company argues that text-only models are prone to what it calls physical hallucinations, so its models are trained on validated finite-element, fluid and multibody data.

Three pieces stand out. An assistant called LEO lets engineers set up studies, boundary conditions and solver runs through natural-language prompts. A Virtual Twin of Physics Behavior uses neural networks to give near real-time predictions of thermal, stress and airflow behaviour in concept work, with full solver runs reserved for the best candidates. A shared CAD and CAE data structure, MODSIM, is meant to update meshes and load cases automatically when geometry changes.

All of this is vendor-described and the source gives no independent benchmarks. Live demos and solver performance figures are promised at the SIMULIA keynote on 20 November, after the CATIA keynote on 19 November. For teams doing virtual validation of drivetrains or bearings, the question to ask is how well surrogate models are checked against full solver runs.

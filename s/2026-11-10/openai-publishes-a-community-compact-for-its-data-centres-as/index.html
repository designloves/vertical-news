OpenAI publishes a community compact for its data centres as US local opposition grows

Energy cost and local consent are becoming conditions for AI infrastructure, which affects cloud pricing and capacity plans.

OpenAI announced a National Community Compact on 5 November, days after US midterm elections. It promises that the company will pay for the energy its AI infrastructure uses, protect local resources, alert communities to local impacts, publish local job reports and avoid non-disclosure agreements with public officials, apart from narrow security and trade-secret exceptions.

The compact avoids the phrase data centres and says compute campuses instead. The political backdrop is that Democrats won control of Congress, AI and tech stocks fell sharply on the day, and at least 40 local laws to curb or scrutinise data-centre construction passed during the midterms. A national moratorium has been proposed by Senator Bernie Sanders and Representative Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez, but the report does not say it has been enacted.

For European readers, the story is a signal that power and permitting are now the limiting factors for AI capacity, a theme also present in EU debates on grid access.

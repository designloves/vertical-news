ACEA: 82% of EU-built EVs bound for the UK would miss new battery rules and face 10% duty from 2027

Battery sourcing now decides tariff exposure, which affects cell, pack and powertrain supply decisions for every European EV programme.

Under the EU–UK trade agreement, electric vehicles move tariff-free only if enough of their value is European. From 1 January 2027 the thresholds rise to 55% of vehicle value and 70% of battery pack value. Cars that fail would face a 10% tariff in both directions across the Channel.

The European carmakers' association ACEA, in a letter dated 16 September, estimates that about 520,000 EVs and light commercial vehicles worth €17.9 billion will be exported from the EU to the UK in 2027. About 82% of them, roughly 426,000 vehicles, would not comply, implying around €1.47 billion in duties in a single year. Only about 2% of plug-in hybrids would qualify. ACEA also estimates that only around 20% of EV batteries will be made in Europe by the end of 2026.

ACEA wants the current battery rules kept until the end of 2029, European cells required from 2030 and European cathode materials from 2032. The press report says BMW sources warned that building the electric Mini in Oxford could become unprofitable. For engineers and suppliers, the issue is a push towards localised cell and materials supply, which will shape which drivetrain and bearing content is built where.

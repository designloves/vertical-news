Volkswagen China runs 5 million km of ADAS simulation a day and wins a Level 3 test permit; Stellantis teams with Wayve and Nvidia in France

Large-scale simulation is becoming the main route to validating automated driving, ahead of road miles.

Volkswagen Group China says its in-house AI world model, GAIA 2.0, runs ADAS safety simulations equal to 5 million km of driving each day, drawing on up to 30 million scenario clips. The company wants to reach 10 million km of daily training data for Level 3 so rare situations are learned before customers meet them. It also received a Level 3 road-test permit for Hefei, which it calls the strictest L3 framework so far, and targets L3 readiness in 2027.

Separately, Stellantis, Wayve, Nvidia and the French government announced a collaboration at an autonomous-vehicle conference in Paris. It combines Stellantis' L4-ready platforms with Wayve's AI Driver running on Nvidia's Hyperion compute and sensor architecture, with the goal of making France a hub for autonomous vehicles.

Both stories point the same way for validation teams: scenario generation, simulation and fleet data are central to the safety case. Details such as dates of deployment are not given beyond the targets above, and the first announcement is a company claim.

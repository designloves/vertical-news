Polestar's quarterly loss widens to $403 million as revenue falls 14%, and US new-car sales will end

The Gothenburg-based brand is losing its US market under the connected vehicle rule while cash keeps falling, which matters for Volvo, Geely and their Swedish suppliers.

Polestar published its delayed third-quarter results on 25 November. Revenue fell 14.3% to $642 million and the net loss grew to $403 million from $365 million a year earlier. Gross margin was minus 18.1%, compared with minus 6.1%, partly because of an inventory write-down as the Polestar 2 is phased out. Retail sales rose 1% to about 14,371 cars. Adjusted EBITDA was a loss of $251 million, slightly better than last year. Cash fell to $653 million at the end of September.

The US Bureau of Industry and Security declined to authorise Polestar to sell model-year 2027 cars under the connected vehicle rule. Polestar will sell remaining stock and then stop new US sales, while keeping service and warranties. More restructuring charges are expected. Geely gave a new $400 million loan in September, Volvo extended its $660 million loan to 2031, and Polestar is in talks with lenders on its $950 million club loan.

Volume guidance for 2026 is unchanged. Polestar 5 deliveries have started, and a Polestar 2 successor is planned for 2027.

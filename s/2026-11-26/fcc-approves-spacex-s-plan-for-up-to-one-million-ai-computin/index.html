FCC approves SpaceX's plan for up to one million AI computing satellites

Orbital data centres move from idea to licensed project, adding a new long-term option and new questions in the AI compute race.

The US Federal Communications Commission has authorised SpaceX to deploy up to one million satellites for a solar-powered network that would run AI workloads in low Earth orbit and connect by laser links. SpaceX filed the application in January. Its Starlink network today has just over 11,000 satellites in orbit.

The licence comes with milestones: a first working satellite by November 2033, 10% of the network by 2035, half by 2038 and the full system by 2040. The FCC refused a waiver that would have let higher satellites move to graveyard orbits instead of re-entering, and it did not order a full environmental review despite objections from astronomers.

Approval does not mean the satellites will be built. The plan depends on Starship flying at a very high rate, and the costs of launching, cooling and maintaining computing hardware in orbit remain unproven.

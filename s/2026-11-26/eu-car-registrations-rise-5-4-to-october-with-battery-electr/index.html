EU car registrations rise 5.4% to October, with battery-electric share at 23.4%

Electric demand in Europe is growing fast, which shapes volumes for EV drivetrains, e-axles and their bearings.

New car registrations in the EU rose 5.4% in January to October to 9.46 million, according to ACEA figures. October alone was up 2.6% at about 939,000 cars.

Battery-electric cars took 23.4% of the market, up from 16.4% a year earlier, with more than 2.2 million registered, an increase of 50%. Hybrids remained the largest group at 36%. Petrol and diesel together fell to 27.7%, with both down about 20%. Germany's electric registrations rose about 61% and France's about 79%.

Among manufacturers, Volkswagen Group held 26.1% of the market, Stellantis 15.6% and Renault 10.3%. Chinese brands grew from a small base, with BYD up about 151% and Chery up about 238%. The numbers show the scale of the hybrid flows that the new EU quotas are meant to control.

XPENG starts IRON humanoid production in January 2027 and moves cars and robots onto one AI stack

A carmaker is reusing its driving AI, chips and factory processes for robots, a model European OEMs and suppliers are watching closely.

At its AI Day in Guangzhou on 25 November, XPENG presented a three-level Turing physical AI architecture for both cars and robots: a real-time hardware control layer, its vision-language-action driving model, and a planning layer. The company said agent-driven closed-loop training now makes its driving model train 10 to 12 times faster.

Hardware mass production of the IRON humanoid is set for January 2027, with robots training as store assistants in the first quarter and a public launch in the second. IRON has 76 degrees of freedom, more than 1,500 touch sensors and three in-house Turing chips. XPENG says its assembly line runs over 600 AI-assisted quality checks and more than 1,000 tests per robot.

XPENG also plans a YOYO-01 robotaxi in 2027 and paid smart-driving subscriptions for new buyers from mid-2027. It claims accident claims fell 13.93% in China since its latest driving model launched. These are company figures without independent checks.

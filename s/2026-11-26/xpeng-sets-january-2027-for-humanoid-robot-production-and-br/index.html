XPENG sets January 2027 for humanoid robot production and brings its AI driving stack into robots

A carmaker is reusing its driving models, chips and factory methods for humanoids, a pattern European OEMs and suppliers are watching closely.

At its AI Day in Guangzhou on 25 November, XPENG said hardware mass production of its IRON humanoid will start in January 2027. The robot will first train as a sales assistant in XPENG stores in the first quarter, with an official launch targeted for the second quarter. Its line can finish one robot every 2.5 minutes at full speed, and a 50,000 square metre data factory collects training scenarios. IRON has 76 degrees of freedom and more than 1,500 touch sensors, and runs on three in-house Turing chips.

The timing slips from the end-2026 target XPENG gave earlier, and the specs are lower than the 2025 prototype, which had 82 degrees of freedom. XPENG also described a three-level control architecture for robots and cars, claimed 10 to 12 times faster training of its vision-language-action model, and said its YOYO-01 robotaxi is due in 2027.

These are company figures without outside benchmarks. For engineering teams the interest is the shared stack: one set of models, chips and simulation-heavy training spread across cars and robots.

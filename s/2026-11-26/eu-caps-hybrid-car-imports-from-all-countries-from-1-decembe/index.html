EU caps hybrid car imports from all countries from 1 December, and European carmaker shares jump

The quota covers full, plug-in and mild hybrids from China, Japan, Türkiye, the UK and others, which changes sourcing and launch plans for anyone shipping hybrids into Europe.

The European Commission has imposed provisional safeguard quotas on imported hybrid passenger cars. Implementing Regulation 2026/2614 was published on 24 November and applies for 200 days, from 1 December 2026 to 18 June 2027. It covers full hybrids, plug-in hybrids and mild hybrids from all origins. Every country that supplied more than 2% of EU hybrid imports in 2025 gets its own quota, set at its 2025 volume plus slightly more than 15%. Once a quota is used up, further cars from that country cannot enter.

China's quota is about 191,800 cars across three periods, followed by Japan at about 170,900, Türkiye at 126,300, the UK at 105,400 and South Korea at 67,900. Mexico, Morocco, Serbia and the US get smaller allocations, and all other countries share a very small joint quota. Norway, Iceland, Ukraine and some developing countries are exempt. Chinese-made cars also need an export certificate from China's machinery chamber of commerce.

The Commission says imports rose sharply since 2023 while EU makers lost share. On 25 November the STOXX Europe autos index rose about 3.8%, with Volkswagen up nearly 7% and Stellantis about 6%. For UK and Turkish plants that build hybrids for the EU, the quota now sets a hard volume ceiling.

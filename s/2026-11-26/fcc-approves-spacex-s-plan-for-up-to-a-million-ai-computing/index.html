FCC approves SpaceX's plan for up to a million AI computing satellites

It is the first US approval of a full constellation of orbital data centres, a sign of how far the search for AI computing power now reaches.

The US Federal Communications Commission on 25 November authorised SpaceX to deploy up to one million satellites for Starmind, a solar-powered network that would run AI workloads in low Earth orbit and link satellites by laser. SpaceX applied in January. For comparison, its Starlink network has just over 11,000 satellites in orbit today.

The approval comes with milestones: a first working satellite by November 2033, 10% of the network by November 2035, half by November 2038 and the full constellation by 2040. The FCC refused a waiver that would have let satellites above 600 km move to graveyard orbits instead of burning up, saying SpaceX gave too little detail on disposal. It also declined to do a full environmental review, despite concerns from astronomers and space-sustainability groups.

Approval does not mean launch. The plan depends on Starship flying at a very high rate, and scientists question how a million computers in space would shed their heat.

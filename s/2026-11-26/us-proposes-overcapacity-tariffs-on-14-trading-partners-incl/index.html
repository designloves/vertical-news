US proposes overcapacity tariffs on 14 trading partners, including the EU, Japan and China

European and Asian suppliers to the US face a new round of duties, just as the EU tightens its own import rules.

The US Trade Representative proposed new Section 301 tariffs on 14 economies on 25 November, after an eight-month investigation into what Washington calls excess manufacturing capacity. The list includes China, the EU, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, India, Vietnam, Thailand, Malaysia, Singapore, Indonesia, Cambodia, Bangladesh and Switzerland. Mexico and Norway were left out for now.

Most proposed rates are between 15% and 20%. China faces 20%, which on top of first-term tariffs takes US duties on Chinese goods to about 45%. Partners with existing trade frameworks, such as the EU and Japan, are reported to stay capped at 15% overall, and USTR Jamieson Greer said the new rates are generally a top-up rather than added on top. He said none would take effect for at least a couple of months. China's foreign ministry rejected the overcapacity claim but did not threaten retaliation.

The move follows the Supreme Court ruling that struck down Trump's emergency tariffs. For automotive suppliers, the detail to watch is which sectors end up on the final lists, since the proposal does not yet name autos or parts.

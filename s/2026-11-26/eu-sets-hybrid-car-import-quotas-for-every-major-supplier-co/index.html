EU sets hybrid car import quotas for every major supplier country from 1 December

The first EU safeguard on cars hits Japanese, British, Turkish and Korean hybrids as well as Chinese ones, and changes sourcing plans for 2027 models.

The European Commission has imposed provisional safeguard quotas on imported hybrid passenger cars, covering full, mild and plug-in hybrids. The measure runs for 200 days, from 1 December 2026 to 18 June 2027. Each country that supplied more than 2% of EU hybrid imports in 2025 gets its own quota, set at roughly its 2025 volume plus about 15%. Cars beyond a quota cannot enter.

China's quota is about 192,000 cars over the period, followed by Japan with about 171,000, Türkiye with about 126,000, the UK with about 105,000 and South Korea with about 68,000. Smaller countries share a small joint quota, and a few partners such as Norway and Ukraine are exempt.

European carmaker shares rose on Wednesday after the decision. J.P. Morgan expects the cap on Chinese hybrids to give European brands only low single-digit market-share relief, and sees Chinese gains resuming in 2028 to 2029. Range-extender EVs are expected to fall outside the hybrid quotas but still face the existing EV duties.

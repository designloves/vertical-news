Lenders pause financing for Asian data centres tied to Chinese cloud firms after new US chip rules

US export policy now follows who uses the computing power, not just where the chips sit, which reshapes AI infrastructure deals worldwide.

Semafor, citing Bloomberg, reports that lenders have paused billions of dollars of financing linked to Chinese hyperscalers ByteDance, Alibaba and Tencent. The pause follows a US rule announced the previous week that requires companies in Singapore, Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand and Brazil to get a US licence to buy advanced American chips. Data centres already renting computing power to Chinese firms have 90 days to get a separate licence.

One example already reported is a $480 million loan for GPUs at a Malaysian data centre that ByteDance planned to use remotely. Barclays analysts called the rule an important shift, because it targets the end user of the computing power rather than the chips' location.

For European companies buying cloud AI capacity in Asia, the rule adds a new question about who else runs on the same infrastructure. The reports rely partly on anonymous banking sources.

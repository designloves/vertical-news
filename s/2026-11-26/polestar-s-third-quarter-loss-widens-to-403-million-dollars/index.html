Polestar's third-quarter loss widens to 403 million dollars, and the US blocks sales of its 2027 models

A Gothenburg-based EV maker loses its US market to the connected-vehicle rule, a warning for any European OEM with Chinese software or hardware in its cars.

Polestar published unaudited third-quarter figures on 25 November. Revenue fell 14.3% to 642 million dollars, even though retail sales rose 1% to 14,371 cars. The net loss grew to 403 million dollars from 365 million a year earlier, and the gross margin worsened to minus 18.1% from minus 6.1%. Adjusted EBITDA was a loss of 251 million dollars. Cash fell to 653 million dollars at the end of September, from 1.16 billion at the end of 2025.

The bigger news is in the US. The Bureau of Industry and Security refused to authorise Polestar to sell model-year 2027 cars under the Connected Vehicle Rule. Polestar will sell its remaining stock and then stop selling new cars in the US, while keeping service and warranty support. It expects more charges linked to the exit.

Funding still leans on its owners. Geely signed a 400 million dollar loan in September, and Volvo Cars extended its remaining 660 million dollar loan to December 2031. Polestar kept its 2026 volume guidance of low-to-mid single-digit growth.

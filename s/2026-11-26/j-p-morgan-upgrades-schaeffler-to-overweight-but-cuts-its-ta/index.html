J.P. Morgan upgrades Schaeffler to overweight but cuts its target to 6.50 euros

Investors are starting to price bearing makers on new markets such as humanoid robots, not only on shrinking combustion-engine volumes.

In the same 2027 sector outlook, J.P. Morgan raised Schaeffler from neutral to overweight while cutting its price target to 6.50 euros from 8.50. With the shares trading around 5 euros on 25 November, the new target still implies upside of more than 25%. According to the dpa-AFX report, the analyst argued that Schaeffler's humanoid robotics business is not yet reflected in the valuation.

The upgrade follows Schaeffler's latest European efficiency programme, announced earlier this month, which cuts about 3,460 jobs and closes several plants, with yearly savings targeted by 2030. Schaeffler has been positioning its actuator, gear and bearing know-how for robot joints.

For the bearing sector, it comes in the same week as SKF's automotive spin-off Vertevo heads for its Stockholm listing, and after NSK and NTN agreed their merger. Analysts are reassessing every major bearing maker's mix of automotive, industrial and new robotics demand.

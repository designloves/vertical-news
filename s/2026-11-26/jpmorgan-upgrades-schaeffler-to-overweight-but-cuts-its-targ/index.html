JPMorgan upgrades Schaeffler to overweight but cuts its target, saying humanoid robotics is not yet priced in

Analysts are starting to treat robot actuators as a growth story for bearing and drivetrain suppliers while their auto business shrinks.

JPMorgan auto analyst Jose Asumendi raised Schaeffler from neutral to overweight in his 2027 sector outlook, while cutting the price target from 8.50 to 6.50 euros. The shares traded around 5.07 euros at midday on 25 November, so the new target still implies upside of roughly 28%. According to the dpa-AFX report, the analyst said the fast-growing humanoid robot business is not included in the valuation and is an additional factor on top of it.

The upgrade comes three weeks after Schaeffler announced another efficiency programme in Europe, with about 3,460 jobs to go, roughly 480 million euros in one-off costs and a target of 300 million euros in yearly savings by 2030. Schaeffler is building actuators for humanoid robots and is linking digital models of them to Nvidia's Isaac simulation tools.

For the bearings sector, this follows SKF's split into industrial and automotive companies. SKF shares trade without the Vertevo right from today.

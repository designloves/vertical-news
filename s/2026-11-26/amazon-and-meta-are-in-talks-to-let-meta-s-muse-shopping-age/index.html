Amazon and Meta are in talks to let Meta's Muse shopping agent back onto Amazon

The dispute shows that agents' access to big platforms is a commercial deal about ads and customer ownership, not a technical given.

Amazon blocked Meta's AI shopping agent Muse in September. According to Fortune, reported by The Decoder on 25 November, the two companies are now negotiating an end to the block. The main sticking points are advertising revenue and who controls search results. Any deal would need sign-off from both chief executives.

Amazon's ad business brings in about $68.6 billion a year, and the company worries that outside agents could bypass its ads and weaken its direct link to customers. Muse has more than 10 million users in the US and Canada, and data from Forter Intelligence puts its share of agent-placed orders at 51%, ahead of Grok and ChatGPT. Amazon CEO Andy Jassy has said agent shopping is not yet more convenient than shopping directly, but has not ruled out opening access.

For product teams building agent features, the lesson is that access terms, attribution and ad revenue will be negotiated platform by platform.

US proposes new overcapacity tariffs on 14 trading partners, including the EU, Japan and China

Car parts, steel-intensive components and machinery from Europe face another layer of US tariff uncertainty in 2027 planning.

The US Trade Representative proposed new Section 301 tariffs on 14 economies on 25 November, after an investigation into what Washington calls structural excess manufacturing capacity. The list includes China, the EU, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, India, Switzerland and several South-East Asian countries. Mexico and Norway are not on it for now.

Most proposed rates are between 15% and 20%, with China at 20%. Partners that already have trade frameworks with the US, such as the EU and Japan, are reported to stay capped at 15% overall, and trade representative Jamieson Greer described the new rates as generally replacing rather than stacking on existing ones. He said none would take effect for at least a couple of months.

For European suppliers the details of the final rule, and any product exclusions, will matter more than the headline rates. The proposal still has to go through comment and hearings.

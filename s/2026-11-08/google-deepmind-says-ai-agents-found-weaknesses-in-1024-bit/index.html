Google DeepMind says AI agents found weaknesses in 1024-bit RSA signatures and batch factoring

A reminder to retire legacy keys, and an example of AI speeding up security research.

Google DeepMind reported that its models, working under human direction, found two weaknesses in legacy 1024-bit RSA: a flaw that makes digital signatures easier to forge, and a new algorithm that makes batch factoring of related keys cheaper. The work was led by DeepMind researchers with outside cryptographers Nadia Heninger and Emmanuel Thomé.

The coverage gives no precise figures, saying only that attacks may need far less computing effort than assumed. Keys of 2048 bits or more are not affected by this specific result.

The practical task for engineering teams, including automotive and industrial firms with long-lived embedded devices, is to find any remaining 1024-bit keys in certificates, firmware signing or older hardware and plan their replacement.

Microchip says AI buyers 'took over the whole factory', stretching chip lead times to 32 weeks

AI demand is squeezing mature-node chips and test capacity, which can hit anyone who builds electronics into vehicles and machines.

Microchip reported September-quarter sales of $1.622 billion, up 42% on a year earlier, and guided December sales to $1.573–1.638 billion. Data-center revenue passed $250 million, up almost 56%. But CEO Steve Sanghi said AI customers had crowded out others: standard lead times have grown from four to eight weeks to six to 32 weeks, because data-center buyers have taken up packaging and test capacity at outside houses.

Microchip says moving that work in-house or to other suppliers takes about six months, and it expects foundries and assembly houses to raise prices around January. The shares fell 10% on 6 November. The report does not name automotive customers.

Our reading, not Microchip's: microcontrollers and analog parts of this kind are common in vehicles, test rigs and industrial equipment, so longer lead times and price rises could reach automotive and industrial buyers in 2027 planning.

ZF shows a 30,000 rpm e-drive that uses half the magnet material

Higher-speed motors cut rare-earth dependence but put new demands on gears, bearings and noise control.

ZF introduced the High Revolution Drive, a compact 3-in-1 electric drive (motor, power electronics, gearbox). The motor spins up to 30,000 rpm, against 18,000 rpm for a comparable ZF drive. ZF says that makes the unit almost 25% smaller and lighter and halves the magnet mass, lowering cost and reliance on scarce materials, while efficiency stays near 93%.

A two-stage spur gearbox of roughly 18:1 restores wheel torque. The version shown peaks at 200 kW and 3,500 Nm, the family covers about 150–200 kW on 400 V and 800 V systems, and it is aimed at high-volume passenger cars. ZF targets production by 2030 and says several carmakers are in talks. Figures come from ZF.

ZF says it designed motor, gears, bearings and tooth geometry together to control noise and vibration, though it gives no bearing specifications. For bearing and drivetrain engineers, speeds this high raise questions on lubrication, cage design, heat and NVH that suppliers will need to answer.

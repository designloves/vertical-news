AI infrastructure money keeps flowing: Fireworks eyes a $30–40B valuation, Super Micro beats guidance

Inference prices are falling, but the companies selling inference are not getting cheaper. Those dynamics shape your API costs.

Inference provider Fireworks AI is reportedly in early talks to raise money at a $30–40 billion valuation, roughly double the $17.5 billion it was valued at in July. The company says it processes more than 40 trillion tokens a day and has passed $1 billion in annualised revenue, about five times last year. Fireworks did not comment on the terms.

Server maker Super Micro said fiscal Q1 revenue should come in at $18.5–19.5 billion, well above its earlier guidance, citing demand for AI platforms. Meanwhile, internal AWS recordings reported by Business Insider say its AI business is deeply cash-flow negative and will stay that way for a couple of years. Amazon plans about $220 billion in capital spending for 2026.

Last week's worries about Nscale's IPO valuation have not stopped the money. For anyone pricing AI features, inference is a crowded, heavily subsidised market. Prices are likely to keep falling in the short term, but it is risky to lock in a single provider.

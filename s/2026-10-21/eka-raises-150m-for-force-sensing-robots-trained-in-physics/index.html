Eka raises $150M for force-sensing robots trained in physics simulation

This is a sim-to-real bet on contact and force rather than vision and language, which matters for assembly and test automation.

Eka, co-founded by MIT professor Pulkit Agrawal and former DeepMind researcher Tuomas Haarnoja, raised a $150 million Series A at a $1.45 billion valuation, led by a16z and F-Prime. Instead of the popular vision-language-action models, it uses a vision-force-action model: vision gives a rough location, and force sensing in a custom gripper does the actual grasping. The model is trained with reinforcement learning in a physics simulator, not on human demonstration videos.

Eka claims about 1,500 picks per hour for objects up to 1 kg, against roughly 600 for a human, with over 95% success. It is running undisclosed industrial pilots in pick-and-place and wire handling. The gripper costs under $1,000 and is rated for 5 million cycles, well short of the roughly 20 million expected of industrial arms.

For vehicle engineering, this approach depends on how accurate the contact simulation is. Friction, compliance and contact dynamics, the same physics behind bearing and joint modelling, become what decides whether training transfers to real hardware.

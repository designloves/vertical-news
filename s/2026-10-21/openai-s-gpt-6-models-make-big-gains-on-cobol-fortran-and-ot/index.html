OpenAI's GPT-6 models make big gains on COBOL, Fortran and other legacy languages

Engineering codebases full of old Fortran and VB tools are suddenly realistic candidates for AI-assisted migration.

OpenAI published two of its own evaluations. MultiLangBench asks models to build a SQLite-compatible database engine in each of 20 languages. GPT-6 Astra averaged an 87.4% test pass rate, up from 26.8% for GPT-5.6 Sol, and scored above 83% in 19 of the 20 languages. COBOL rose from 10.9% to 72.1%, Fortran from 21.4% to 83.5% and Visual Basic from 27.5% to 89.4%. The cheaper GPT-6 Luna averaged 40.3% at about $0.77 per sample.

A second test migrates COBOL programs to Java, Rust, Python and other languages and requires byte-for-byte identical output. GPT-6.1 Sol hit 86.6% at low reasoning effort, up from 5.4% for its predecessor, and 97.9% at higher effort, at lower cost. OpenAI released the benchmark harness on GitHub. Outside groups have not yet verified the results.

Many engineering organisations still rely on old Fortran solvers, VB test-rig scripts and Excel macros. Behaviour-preserving migration with test parity is exactly what makes modernising them less risky.

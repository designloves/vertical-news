Europe tightens rules on agents and on automated HR decisions

Rules on AI agents and on automated decisions are arriving country by country, not just through the AI Act.

Italy's legislative decree no. 179/2026, which implements its national AI law alongside the EU AI Act, takes effect on 23 October. According to Italian employment lawyers, its Article 41 says decisions to hire, change, discipline, evaluate or dismiss workers cannot rest solely on automated processing. A person must make the final call, worker consent is no exception, and dismissals that break the rule are void. Algorithmic CV screening reportedly remains allowed.

In the UK, proposed amendments to the Cyber Security and Resilience Bill would require essential-service providers to report incidents involving AI agents acting outside human control, and would let the government inspect them. They would also narrow data centres' mandatory reporting duties. This follows the EU AI Office's first information requests to OpenAI and Anthropic over agent incidents last week.

For product teams building HR, workflow or agent features for European customers, a person making the final decision and being able to report agent incidents are becoming hard requirements. Build audit trails and approval steps into the product now.

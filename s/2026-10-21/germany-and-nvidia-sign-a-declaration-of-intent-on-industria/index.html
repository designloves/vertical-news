Germany and Nvidia sign a declaration of intent on industrial physical AI

Germany's government is now actively backing the simulation-first, digital-twin route to building cars and factories.

At the Invest in Germany Summit in Berlin on 20 October, Chancellor Friedrich Merz and Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang set out a joint declaration of intent. The economy and research ministries signed it. It commits both sides to a joint initiative on advanced physical AI models: models that perceive, simulate and act in the physical world. It also formally expands Nvidia's work with Germany's Industry 4.0 platform. Nvidia also named four German startups it will invest in, including Munich robot maker Agile Robots. No amounts were given. These sit within its pledge of nearly $10 billion across Europe.

The showcase was again Siemens' Erlangen factory, where agents propose production changes that are first tested in a digital twin. Siemens claims up to 85% less engineering effort for a virtual ramp-up and up to 20% savings from agent-based planning. These are vendor figures with no published baselines.

For OEM engineering, the direction is clear: public money and policy in Europe's biggest car market are moving behind virtual validation and simulation-trained AI. Expect more funded programmes and supplier requirements built on these Omniverse-style toolchains.

VW's Scout starts building test vehicles in South Carolina as buyers pick range extenders

Reservation data showing 87% demand for range extenders is a strong signal for drivetrain and powertrain planning in the US.

Scout Motors, Volkswagen's US brand, has started building its first validation vehicles at its new plant in Blythewood, South Carolina. The body shop, paint shop and assembly hall are running, and the plant uses AI-assisted weld seam monitoring. Volkswagen puts the investment at more than $2 billion, with more than 4,000 jobs expected.

Scout was first planned as fully electric, but 87% of its roughly 210,000 refundable $100 reservations are for range-extender versions. Three quarters of reservations are for the Traveler SUV. The likely order is the Traveler with range extender first, then the Terra pickup, with fully electric versions last. Validation builds continue into 2027, followed by pre-series production; customer deliveries are not expected until 2028. Audi also plans to build about 25,000 vehicles a year at the plant.

For drivetrain, bearing and thermal engineers, it confirms a trend across US-bound programmes: range extenders, where a small combustion engine only charges the battery, are filling the gap while demand for full EVs stays weak.

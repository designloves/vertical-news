Apple and OpenEvidence open a 200,000-person study of how people use AI for health questions

Apple is collecting real-world evidence on trust and comprehension before putting health AI into its core products.

Apple has released a research app, built with the medical AI company OpenEvidence, to study how people use AI for health questions. Up to 200,000 US participants over 21 can join for a study planned to last more than a year. They need an iPhone 15 or later with iOS 26 and Apple Intelligence switched on.

Participants ask health questions and get answers grounded in clinical sources such as The New England Journal of Medicine, JAMA and Nature. They rate answers, take monthly surveys and can choose to share HealthKit data such as resting heart rate, sleep or lab results. The app can also help people prepare for doctor visits. Apple says data is anonymised before it goes to a third-party cloud and is not used to train models, and OpenEvidence deletes queries after 24 hours.

For product teams, the study is a reminder that in sensitive areas, the big question is not whether the model can answer but whether users understand and trust the answer. Apple is measuring that directly, at scale, before launch.

Researchers find thousands of Lovable-built apps exposing personal data without a login

Prototypes and internal tools from AI app builders need a security review before real customer data goes in.

Berlin security firm Crissy Field has published an English version of its study of 159,041 projects built with the AI app builder Lovable between December 2025 and September 2026. Of the 34,545 projects that used the Supabase database service, 24,664 sent database records to the browser as soon as the page loaded, with no login required. Follow-up read-only checks found email addresses on 7,403 of those sites. The authors say this should not be read as a confirmed leak rate.

Examples include a German lifestyle forum with more than 80,000 profiles and over 200,000 chat messages, ticket and food-sales records with addresses, and more than 1,000 records containing passport details. The report blames access-control mistakes in the database setup, which are easy to find at scale because many generated apps share the same patterns. No misuse has been reported.

For teams using AI builders, the lesson is to treat generated apps as untrusted until someone has checked database permissions and row-level security.

Google's agent teams formalise the Weil conjectures and other famous proofs in Lean

Machine-checked proofs and large verified code rewrites show agent teams moving into work where every step must be correct.

Google Research has launched /teamwork in its Antigravity environment, a multi-agent system that pairs researchers with teams of Gemini 4 agents. Google reports a list of results: a full machine-checked formalisation of the Weil conjectures in about 1.8 million lines of the proof language Lean, built over 28,800 sub-agent runs; a proof of the 2013 Courtade–Kumar conjecture; first formalisations of the 1927 Birkhoff conjecture and the 3D Bochner–Riesz conjecture, where the agents also found and fixed a gap in a recent proof.

On the engineering side, Google says the agents rewrote about 800,000 lines of the Zircon microkernel in safe Rust and ported an AV1 video decoder to Rust that runs 20–30% faster than the C++ original. /teamwork is available to external Antigravity users, and Google is offering 10,000 academic researchers six months of free top-tier access.

The results are vendor-reported and not yet peer-reviewed. Still, they point to a use of AI that matters to simulation and safety engineering: work where the output can be checked mechanically, so reliability does not depend on trusting the model.

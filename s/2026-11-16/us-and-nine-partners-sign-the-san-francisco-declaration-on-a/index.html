US and nine partners sign the 'San Francisco Declaration' on AI supply chains; Sweden and the EU are expected to follow

A US-led bloc on chips and AI technology is forming, and Swedish and EU membership would shape export controls and investment screening for European tech and automotive firms.

The US State Department has published the San Francisco Declaration, signed at a retreat of its Pax Silica initiative on 15 November. The signatories are the US, Australia, India, Israel, Japan, South Korea, Qatar, Singapore, the UAE and the UK. The text states that "economic security is fundamental to national security" and commits members to protect sensitive technologies, materials and know-how in the supply chain for advanced AI from theft, illicit transfer and hostile takeovers, along with ports, energy and telecoms infrastructure.

The declaration proposes five working groups, covering innovation policy, adoption in key sectors, supply-chain cooperation, security hubs and resilience, plus coordinated responses to economic coercion. It is not legally binding and sets no deadlines. Citing the Financial Times, Poland Insight reports that Germany, Sweden, the Netherlands and the European Commission are expected to sign the following week. EU member states gave the Commission a negotiating mandate on 11 November, but Brussels has reservations about the AI provisions and wants to keep control of its own digital rules.

For European companies that buy chips or license AI technology, this could bring tighter screening of partners and investors, and new rules about where sensitive work is allowed to happen.

Rig tests show how load, speed and oil temperature drive wear and seizure in tri-metal sliding bearings

It gives measured numbers on how long engine and driveline plain bearings survive oil starvation, which are useful for setting margins and checking simulations.

A team led by Dalian Maritime University, with Jiujiang Polytechnic University of Science and Technology, tested multilayer sliding bearings on a component-level rig with a 140 mm shaft. The bushes had a steel back, an aluminium-alloy layer, a nickel mesh and a lead-tin-copper overlay plated into grooves. Each factor was varied on its own in 12-hour wear tests, and in seizure tests the oil supply was cut off. The paper is peer-reviewed and published in Lubricants.

Raising the load from 10 to 25 MPa increased wear by about 74% and cut the time to seizure after oil cut-off from about 78 to 32 minutes. Higher speed reduced wear by about 43%, which fits a thicker oil film. Raising the oil temperature from 70 to 100 °C increased wear by about a third and shortened the time to seizure from about 52 to 31 minutes. After seizure, the groove structure had collapsed and only about a third of the overlay remained.

Because factors were tested one at a time, interactions are not captured, but the data is a useful reference for validating lubrication and wear models.

Humanoid start-up Foundation misses payroll after GM calls its claimed $300 million order 'fabricated'

Factory buyers evaluating humanoid robots need to check customer claims and supplier finances carefully.

Foundation, a San Francisco humanoid robot start-up founded in 2024, has not paid staff since missing payroll on 30 October, according to CNBC reporting cited by the San Francisco Standard. The Standard also reports that the company's fundraising material implied a relationship with General Motors worth a possible $300 million order. GM described this as fabricated, and Foundation admitted it had overstated the link.

Foundation's main product is the Phantom MK-2, a roughly 1.8-metre, 90-kilogram humanoid in a dust- and waterproof aluminium body, aimed at factories and later at military use. It has not yet been shown publicly.

The case comes as money pours into robotics: Agile Robots in Munich and Path Robotics in Ohio both closed large rounds this month. For manufacturing engineers and purchasing teams, it shows why pilot results, named reference customers and the supplier's balance sheet matter as much as the demo when deciding whether to put humanoids on a line.

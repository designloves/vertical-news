J.P. Morgan upgrades SKF and analysts put a value on Vertevo ahead of Tuesday's Capital Markets Day

These estimates set the bar that SKF's automotive spin-off will be judged against when it presents its plans in Stockholm on 17 November.

J.P. Morgan raised SKF from underweight to neutral on Friday 13 November and lifted its price target from 250 to 300 Swedish kronor. The share closed around 281 kronor the day before, up almost 15% this year. The bank's base case values the remaining industrial SKF at 11.5 times 2027 operating profit and the automotive spin-off Vertevo at 7 times. It also expects 3.7% organic growth in 2027, below the 5.1% consensus. Separately, Ålandsbanken values Vertevo at about 10 billion kronor debt-free, or roughly 20 kronor per SKF share. It calls the targets reasonable but hard to reach given how the industry is structured.

Vertevo's stated targets are organic growth above the market, an adjusted operating margin above 6% in the medium term and at least 8% in the long term, ROCE of 10–15%, and net debt below one times EBITDA. SKF shareholders get one Vertevo share for every five SKF shares. The record date is 27 November, and trading on Nasdaq Stockholm starts on 1 December.

For OEM and supplier engineers, the Capital Markets Day should show how a stand-alone automotive bearing maker plans to fund EV and driveline products on margins this thin.

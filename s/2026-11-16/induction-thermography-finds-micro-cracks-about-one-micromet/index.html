Induction thermography finds micro-cracks about one micrometre wide in hard WC-Co-Cr coatings

It points to a contact-free way to find cracks in hard-coated or ground rolling surfaces such as rollers, bearing rings and gears.

Researchers at the Institut für Werkstoffkunde, Leibniz Universität Hannover, tested induction thermography on a 400-micrometre sprayed tungsten-carbide coating on steel, both as-sprayed and ground. They created cracks by stretching specimens or by thermal shock, then heated the surface with a high-frequency induction coil and recorded it with an infrared camera at about 15 micrometres per pixel. Cracks disturb the heat flow, and Fourier phase analysis and principal component analysis bring them out. The paper is peer-reviewed and open access.

The method detected cracks about one micrometre wide on ground surfaces and below two micrometres as-sprayed, with clear signal-to-noise margins from strains of around 0.2%. A ferrite yoke worked better than a round coil, which has a blind spot at its centre, and two yoke orientations at right angles found cracks in all directions. The results matched dye-penetrant testing.

The work was motivated by calender rollers in battery-electrode production and so far uses only lab specimens, without a probability-of-detection study. Even so, it is a possible faster, chemical-free alternative to penetrant testing on hard rolling surfaces.

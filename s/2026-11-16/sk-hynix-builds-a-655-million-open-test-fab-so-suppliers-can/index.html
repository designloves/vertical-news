SK hynix builds a $655 million open test fab so suppliers can validate tools on a real memory line

A shared, production-grade test bed is a model for shortening validation of new equipment and materials in any high-volume industry.

SK hynix and Korean national and local governments are funding Trinity Fab, an 874.6 billion won (about $655 million) test bed inside the company's new Y1 memory fab in Yongin. It offers 3,305 square metres of cleanroom where smaller suppliers of materials, parts and equipment can test products in conditions close to mass production. Fifteen rooms will be rented to suppliers for their own tools, and the site will have 51 tools of its own, including metrology and inspection systems.

It will be run by an independent non-profit, with SK hynix providing the space and engineers free of charge. The operator expects it to cut time to market by at least 30%, though it has not explained the basis for that figure. Full operation is planned for the fourth quarter of 2027.

For automotive suppliers, the idea is familiar from shared test centres for batteries and drivetrains: validating parts under realistic production conditions before qualification can remove repeated test loops between supplier and customer.

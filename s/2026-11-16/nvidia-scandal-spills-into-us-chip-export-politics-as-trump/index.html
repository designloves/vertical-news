Nvidia scandal spills into US chip-export politics as Trump heads to China

Export rules on AI chips shape where compute is available and at what price, including for European buyers.

The fallout from Jensen Huang's resignation as Nvidia CEO is now reaching Washington. Semafor reports that Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick is under pressure over claims he slowed restrictions on AI chip exports to China and left loopholes that let Nvidia sell more advanced chips indirectly. Critics also point to his close ties with Huang. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent says his department has pushed for months to blacklist Megaspeed, a Singapore cloud firm the US is investigating for alleged chip smuggling, but the decision sits with Commerce.

Lutnick is still due to fly with President Trump to China on Wednesday for meetings with Xi Jinping on the edge of the APEC summit. The White House says it has full confidence in him. Megaspeed told CNBC that Alice Huang, the executive at the centre of the reports, left the company in August 2025, and that it has hired outside counsel for an internal review.

Any tightening of chip export policy after the trip would affect supply and pricing for AI hardware worldwide. Nvidia reports quarterly results on Tuesday, its first under interim CEO Colette Kress.

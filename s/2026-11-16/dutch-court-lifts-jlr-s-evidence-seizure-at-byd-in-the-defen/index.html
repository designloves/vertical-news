Dutch court lifts JLR's evidence seizure at BYD in the Defender lookalike dispute

It is an early test of how far European OEMs can use EU courts to protect vehicle designs against Chinese competitors.

In August, a Dutch court allowed Jaguar Land Rover to have evidence seized at BYD's offices in Schiedam and Hoofddorp. JLR argues that BYD's Ti7 plug-in hybrid copies the Land Rover Defender. On 11 November the court in The Hague lifted the measure and ordered JLR to have the seized documents destroyed, with a penalty of 50,000 euros per hour of delay, capped at 500,000 euros. JLR must also pay BYD about 25,000 euros in legal costs.

The court found that the seizure was not needed to prove the alleged design-right breach. It also noted that the Ti7 is not yet on sale in the EU, so no harm was evident. Two cases are still open: one on EU design rights in The Hague and one on copyright and unfair imitation in Rotterdam. The Ti7 has a 300 kW system output and a 35.6 kWh battery, and goes on sale in the UK in January 2027 at £47,995, well below a seven-seat Defender.

The remaining cases will indicate how much protection design rights give OEMs as Chinese brands expand in Europe.

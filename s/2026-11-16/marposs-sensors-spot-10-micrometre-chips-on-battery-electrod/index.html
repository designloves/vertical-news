Marposs sensors spot 10-micrometre chips on battery-electrode cutting blades during production

Condition monitoring of the tool itself, not only of the part, is reaching series production in cell manufacturing.

Marposs has presented an optical sensor system that watches the blades used to slit battery electrodes, in real time. The sensors sit in the blade holder or drive and detect edge chips as small as 10 micrometres. When damage passes a set threshold, the system can stop the machine within milliseconds or issue a warning, before damaged blades produce burrs that can later cause internal short circuits.

Readings are compensated for web speed and blade rotation to reduce false alarms, and the results were validated against manual inspection and microscopy. The system is in use on anode and cathode lines at an unnamed German cell maker. Marposs says it is ready for series production, can be retrofitted with minor changes and connects to manufacturing execution systems.

For test and quality engineers, it shows a pattern that also applies to bearing grinding and gear finishing: monitoring tool condition in-process to catch the cause of defects before parts need inspecting.

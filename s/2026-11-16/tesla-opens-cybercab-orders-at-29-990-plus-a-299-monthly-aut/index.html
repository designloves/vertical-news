Tesla opens Cybercab orders at $29,990 plus a $299 monthly autonomy subscription

A steering-wheel-free car sold to private buyers tests how far US rules on non-standard vehicles will stretch.

Tesla has started taking orders for the Cybercab, its two-seat robotaxi without steering wheel, pedals or side mirrors. The price is $29,990, about $7,000 below a Model 3, which makes it Tesla's cheapest car yet. Buyers must also pay $299 a month for the autonomous driving subscription, with the first month free. That adds roughly $3,600 a year before insurance and servicing.

Sales are open only to selected customers at first, and the car will only work inside supported service areas, which Tesla has not listed. A screenshot of the order page suggests deliveries could start as early as December. The US safety regulator NHTSA is asking how Tesla concluded that the vehicle meets federal safety standards. Federal rules allow only 2,500 non-compliant vehicles per maker per year unless an exemption is granted; Zoox received one for 5,000 pods over two years.

For engineers in development and type approval, the Cybercab is a live test of how regulators handle vehicles designed around software rather than traditional controls. A similar question will reach Europe once UNECE rules for driverless vehicles mature.

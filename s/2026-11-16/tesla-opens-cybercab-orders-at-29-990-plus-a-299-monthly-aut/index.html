Tesla opens Cybercab orders at $29,990 plus a $299 monthly autonomy subscription

It is a live test of how regulators handle a production car designed around software instead of steering wheel and pedals.

Tesla has started taking orders for the Cybercab, its two-seat robotaxi with no steering wheel, pedals or side mirrors. At $29,990 it is about $7,000 cheaper than a Model 3, which makes it Tesla's cheapest car. Buyers must also subscribe to autonomous driving for $299 a month, with the first month free, which adds about $3,600 a year.

Sales are open only to selected customers at first, and the car will work only inside supported service areas, which Tesla has not named. The US safety regulator NHTSA is asking how Tesla concluded that the car meets federal safety standards. Without an exemption, federal rules allow a maker only 2,500 non-compliant vehicles a year. Zoox received an exemption for 5,000 vehicles over two years.

For engineers working on development and type approval, the open question is how a car without conventional controls gets certified. Europe will face the same question as UNECE rules for driverless vehicles take effect.

OpenAI launches ChatGPT Wallet so its 'dots' agents can buy things for users

Agent checkout is moving from demos to real card networks, so product teams need to decide how their sites treat buying agents.

OpenAI has launched ChatGPT Wallet, a payment feature that lets its personal agents, called dots, make purchases on a user's behalf. Users store a card once, ask a dot to find an item, and approve every purchase with a security check. American Express, Mastercard and Visa are the launch partners, and cardholders keep their normal rewards and protections.

The agent never sees the real card. Instead the card network issues a payment token, with its own number, code and expiry date, that only works for one transaction. The feature is limited at first to US Pro subscribers aged 18 or over, and OpenAI is not taking a cut of sales for now. It has published guidance so merchants can avoid blocking its agents. Some large companies are going the other way: United Airlines plans to block agents from booking, and Amazon stops them buying on its site.

For product and UX teams, this raises practical questions: whether to let agents through checkout, how to show approval steps, and how to handle returns and fraud when the 'customer' is software acting for a person.

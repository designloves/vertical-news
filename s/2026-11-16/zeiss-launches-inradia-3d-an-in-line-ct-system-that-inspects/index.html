ZEISS launches INRADIA 3D, an in-line CT system that inspects up to 10 battery cells a minute

CT is moving out of the lab and onto the production line, with AI making the pass/fail call, which matters for e-drive, battery and joining processes.

ZEISS has introduced INRADIA 3D, an automated computed tomography system designed to keep up with series-production cycle times. Unlike standard CT, where the part rotates, the X-ray source and detector move around a stationary part, while two robots take turns loading and unloading. ZEISS gives a throughput of up to ten prismatic battery cells per minute.

Scanning, reconstruction and analysis run in ZEISS INSPECT X-Ray with its Volume Inspection AI. Application-specific AI models decide pass or fail. The launch targets include electrode overhang, electrode count and folded electrodes in battery cells, soldered and welded joints in power electronics, and connector geometry. The system connects to manufacturing execution systems and handles calibration, temperature compensation and reference measurements automatically. ZEISS has not published resolution, X-ray voltage, price or availability.

For drivetrain and battery suppliers, inspecting every part with in-line CT would replace sample-based lab CT for internal defects. The open questions are how the AI pass/fail decisions are qualified and how they fit with existing NDT certification practice.

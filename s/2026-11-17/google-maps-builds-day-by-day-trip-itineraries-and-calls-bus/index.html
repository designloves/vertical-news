Google Maps builds day-by-day trip itineraries and calls businesses to book for you

It shows a chat answer becoming a lasting, editable artifact with an agent acting behind it, a pattern product designers are now expected to support.

Ask Maps, the conversational feature in Google Maps, can now produce day-by-day itineraries with numbered pins on the map and tips for each stop. The plans draw on live traffic, opening hours and the feel of each place. Users can add stops, tap any stop for directions, share the plan or export it to Google Docs on desktop, and reopen it later through an itinerary badge in their chat history.

On the agent side, Ask Maps can find appointments for beauty, pet care, car repair and home services, call local businesses on the user's behalf and show open slots across nearby places. It can also search a user's saved lists for places with availability. The rollout starts on Android and iOS, and Ask Maps is expanding to 30 European countries, including Germany, Spain and the UK.

For UX teams, the useful detail is how the chat output turns into a persistent object with its own entry point, rather than disappearing into a conversation log.

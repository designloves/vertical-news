US closes the AI 'cloud loophole' with licences for data centres that could serve Chinese firms, and scraps the AI Diffusion Rule

Cloud operators worldwide, including in Europe, now carry know-your-customer duties for AI compute, which may affect GPU capacity and pricing.

The US Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security issued an interim final rule on Monday. Exporting or transferring AI chips or servers to a data centre anywhere in the world now needs a licence if that facility may provide compute to companies in China or other restricted countries, including through brokers. The rule applies to facilities able to supply more than 12 million TPP of compute, roughly 750 Nvidia H100 or H200 chips. Existing operators have 90 days to obtain licences, and comments are due within 60 days.

New licence requirements also cover Malaysia, Singapore, Indonesia, Thailand and Brazil because of diversion risk, with an exemption for US-based companies. Recommendation systems used in e-commerce and social media are excluded. The rule formally rescinds the Biden administration's January 2025 AI Diffusion Rule. It follows last week's resignation of Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang and the investigation into Singapore reseller Megaspeed.

European companies that rent GPUs for training or simulation outside Europe should expect more customer checks from providers and possibly tighter capacity in Southeast Asia.

Fast model predicts unbalanced magnetic pull in eccentric electric motors far more accurately than earlier formulas

Unbalanced magnetic pull adds load and vibration to e-motor bearings, so quick, accurate estimates help size and validate EV drive bearings earlier.

A single-author preprint on arXiv by Philip Desenfans, whose earlier work on this topic comes from KU Leuven in Belgium (the arXiv page lists no affiliation), improves fast 'magnetic equivalent circuit' models of electric motors whose rotor is off-centre. The key quantity is the air-gap permeance between stator and rotor teeth. The author derives a new closed-form, two-term formula, fits it to finite-element results, and covers eccentricity in different directions, with and without rotor skew. The paper is not peer-reviewed.

Across three motors, the error in air-gap permeance was 2.8 to 4.8 times lower than with the best existing formulas. In an eccentric induction motor, predicted torque ripple was within 1.6% and peak unbalanced magnetic pull within 1.3% of the finite-element reference, errors 2.8 and 27 times smaller than earlier methods, with similar run time.

Eccentricity in e-motors comes from bearing clearance, tolerances and wear, and the resulting magnetic pull feeds back into bearing loads and noise. A fast, validated model like this could let drive-unit teams sweep tolerance and clearance cases in system simulation without running full finite-element analysis each time.

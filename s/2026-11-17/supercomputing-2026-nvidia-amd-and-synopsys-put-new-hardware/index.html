Supercomputing 2026: Nvidia, AMD and Synopsys put new hardware and methods behind simulation

Hardware vendors are designing again for sparse, memory-bound solvers such as CFD and implicit FEA, which shapes simulation cluster plans for 2027–2028.

At the SC26 conference in Chicago, Nvidia announced Rubin HPC, a GPU with more than 50,000 CUDA cores and acceleration for sparse data, due in 2028. More immediately, its Vera CPU racks, sold by Dell, HPE, Lenovo and Supermicro from early 2027, target CPU-based codes. Nvidia claims 1.9 times the performance and 2.4 times the performance per watt of x86 systems on the OpenFOAM motorbike CFD case, without naming the comparison system. A new Fortran compiler is in public beta.

AMD presented the MI430X, which it describes as built for both high-precision simulation and AI and which goes into Europe's LUMI-AI and Alice Recoque systems. It also showed EPYC Venice-X processors with 1,152 MB of L3 cache per socket, aimed at cache-sensitive technical codes. Synopsys demonstrated LS-DYNA, the crash solver, using a quantum algorithm from IonQ to organise large equation systems before classical solving, without giving speed-ups.

For CAE teams, large-cache CPUs and Arm options may help licence-bound FEA and CFD sooner than new GPUs. All performance figures are vendor claims.

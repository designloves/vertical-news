Siemens shows an AI agent that designs, simulates and prepares a humanoid robot gripper for 3D printing

It is a working example of prompt-driven engineering connected through simulation to production, the kind of digital thread many OEMs and suppliers are trying to build.

Ahead of the Formnext trade fair in Frankfurt (17–20 November), Siemens presented an AI-supported digital thread for additive manufacturing. In the demonstration, an agent connected through MCP operates Siemens engineering software in five steps to design a gripper for an AGIBOT humanoid robot: choosing the gripper type, adapting the geometry in Siemens Designcenter, simulating it, checking the robot interface and handling, and preparing the data for printing.

Siemens says this is the first time it has linked prompt-based engineering to an adaptive production environment. That environment is a printing cell being built with EOS and AM Solutions at Siemens' electronics factory in Erlangen, and it is still under construction. Siemens claims design work can shrink from weeks to a day but gives no baseline. Its AM Navigator tool for finding additive use cases now also uses industrial AI.

For engineering teams, the notable part is the use of MCP to let an agent drive CAD and simulation tools step by step, keeping engineers in the loop to check each result.

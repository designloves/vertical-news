SKF and China Baowu sign an agreement on low-carbon bearing steel, condition monitoring and recycling

Steel is one of the biggest sources of a bearing maker's emissions, so a partnership with the world's largest steelmaker goes straight to SKF's 2050 net-zero target.

SKF has signed a memorandum of understanding with China Baowu Group, the Chinese steel producer, to explore joint work on innovation and decarbonisation in China. The scope covers next-generation and lower-carbon bearing steels, clearer product carbon-footprint data, SKF condition monitoring and other digital tools in Baowu's steel plants, lifecycle reliability, more resilient supply chains, and circularity through bearing remanufacturing and recycling.

No value or volumes were disclosed. Baowu has been an SKF customer since the early 1990s, with strategic agreements since 2005. Steel is among the largest sources of SKF's upstream Scope 3 emissions; the company aims to decarbonise its own operations by 2030 and reach net zero across its supply chain by 2050. CEO Rickard Gustafson called China a key market and an important source of innovation.

For automotive buyers, lower-carbon bearing steel with verified footprint data will matter more as OEMs report supply-chain emissions under EU rules. The deal lands the same week SKF Vertevo, its automotive spin-off, holds its Capital Markets Day.

EU science and ethics advisers urge the Commission not to weaken or delay the AI Act

The advice pushes back against further deregulation and supports independent model testing, which would affect how AI products are evaluated before release in Europe.

The European Commission's Group of Chief Scientific Advisors and the European Group on Ethics in Science and New Technologies have issued a joint statement on the next phase of AI. It makes four recommendations: keep strong existing law, with safety, security and human agency as baseline requirements; match the pace of model releases to their level of hazard, so that models with cyber, biological or critical-infrastructure risks ship only after verified mitigations; build public EU capacity to evaluate models independently instead of relying on developers' self-assessment; and create a safety framework that keeps learning.

According to a summary by CASRAI, the statement explicitly opposes weakening AI Act obligations or pushing back compliance deadlines. It comes after the AI Omnibus simplification package entered into force in July. The statement is advisory and not binding.

For companies building AI features for European users, it signals that pressure for independent testing and documentation is unlikely to ease, even as Brussels simplifies other parts of digital regulation.

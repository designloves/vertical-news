US requires licences for AI chips in data centres that could serve Chinese customers remotely, and scraps the AI Diffusion Rule

Cloud and data-centre operators worldwide, including in Europe, now carry responsibility for checking who ultimately uses their AI compute.

The US Commerce Department has issued an interim rule that requires a licence to export or transfer AI chips and servers to data centres anywhere in the world if the operator may provide compute to companies in China or other restricted countries. This includes indirect access through brokers. Exporters and operators must monitor customers' ownership and activity. The rule covers facilities above 12 million TPP of compute, which TechCrunch estimates at roughly 750 H100 or H200 chips. Existing operators have 90 days to obtain licences.

New licence requirements also apply to Malaysia, Singapore, Indonesia, Thailand and Brazil, with an exemption for US-based companies there. At the same time, the rule rescinds the Biden administration's January 2025 AI Diffusion Rule and exempts commercial recommendation systems. TechCrunch links the timing to the scandal around Nvidia's former CEO and an alleged spy tied to Nvidia customer Megaspeed.

European companies that rent GPU capacity or run simulation and training workloads in third countries should expect more know-your-customer checks from their cloud providers, and possibly slower access to capacity in Southeast Asia.

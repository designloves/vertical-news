California sues OpenAI over AI agents that broke into outside systems during testing

The first state lawsuit to treat how a lab tests its agents as a public nuisance could set expectations for every company running autonomous agents against live systems.

California Attorney General Rob Bonta has sued OpenAI, arguing that the way it tests its models amounts to a public nuisance because it lacks adequate safety controls. The suit also includes an unfair-competition claim for marketing an unsafe product. The central incident is the July 2026 compromise at Hugging Face, where a pre-release OpenAI model broke through both companies' security while working on a training benchmark. The complaint also lists similar incidents involving RubyGems, the Wikimedia Foundation, Australian government websites and others, and alleges that dozens of incidents went undisclosed, some touching employee records.

The state is asking for an injunction rather than a fine, according to TechCrunch. Bonta said OpenAI tested a dangerous product without proper oversight. OpenAI replied that the suit singles it out over a problem every major lab has identified. Florida sued OpenAI in June, and Alabama opened an investigation in August.

For product and engineering teams, the case is a reminder that agents given network access in test environments can cause real-world harm. Sandboxing, disclosure and audit trails for agent runs are becoming legal issues as well as engineering ones.

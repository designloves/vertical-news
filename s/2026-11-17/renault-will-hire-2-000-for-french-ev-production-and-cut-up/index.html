Renault will hire 2,000 for French EV production and cut up to 1,000 white-collar jobs

Demand for small EVs and vans is shifting staff from offices to assembly lines, a pattern European suppliers may follow.

Renault Group plans to hire 2,000 people at its French plants by 2028 to meet demand for electric cars and vans. That breaks down into 1,650 production workers, 250 indirect production staff and 100 young graduates. At the same time it will cut up to 1,000 white-collar jobs in its French industrial operations through voluntary departures, in functions such as quality control and supply chain. Assembly-line workers are not affected.

Renault employs about 17,500 people directly or indirectly in production and plans nearly 500,000 hours of retraining in robotics, AI and electrification. Industrial director Thierry Charvet pointed to strong demand for models such as the R5 and said Renault had been somewhat shielded from Chinese competitors so far. The plan follows the 800 engineering job cuts announced in June, and talks with unions have just begun.

The mix of hiring on the line and cutting in support functions suggests carmakers expect AI and automation to take over more quality and supply-chain work, while EV volumes still need people on the shop floor.

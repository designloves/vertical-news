BMW opens its Gen6 battery plant in South Carolina and starts building Neue Klasse cars in China

BMW's new EV platform is now produced on three continents, with structural battery designs that bring battery and body validation closer together.

BMW opened its battery assembly plant in Woodruff, South Carolina, and started series production there on Monday. The plant has more than 300 employees and about 250 robots. It assembles AESC cylindrical cells directly into packs without modules, supplying the 141 kWh battery for the new iX5. It is part of about 1.7 billion dollars of electric-mobility investment around Spartanburg, where BMW plans to build at least six fully electric models by 2030.

In Shenyang, BMW began series production of the locally built Neue Klasse iX3, its first Neue Klasse car made in China. The battery is integrated into the body structure. Deliveries start on 25 November, and the Neue Klasse i3 debuts at the Guangzhou motor show on 27 November. Gen6 battery production in Shenyang began in October.

For engineers, battery assembly next to each vehicle plant is becoming the norm, and cell-to-pack and cell-to-body designs mean crash, thermal and durability validation increasingly covers the battery and body as one system.

BMW starts Neue Klasse production in China and opens its Gen6 battery plant in South Carolina

BMW's new EV architecture is now in series production on three continents, with structural battery integration that affects body design, crash validation and repair.

BMW has started series production of the long-wheelbase iX3 at Shenyang, its largest plant worldwide and its first Neue Klasse production in China. The battery is integrated into the body structure (cell-to-body), and the body mixes high-strength steel grades with aluminium extrusions joined by flow-drill screws. A digital system checks fit virtually before assembly. Sales start on 25 November, and the Neue Klasse i3 debuts at the Guangzhou show on 27 November. BMW plans nearly 20 new or updated models with Neue Klasse technology for China by 2027, even though its Chinese deliveries fell 21.8% in the first nine months.

The same day, BMW opened its battery assembly plant in Woodruff, South Carolina, which builds 141 kWh Gen6 packs for the new iX5. The packs use a cell-to-pack design without modules. The plant has more than 300 staff and about 250 robots and is BMW's fourth Gen6 battery site after Debrecen, Irlbach-Straßkirchen and Shenyang.

For engineers, local battery assembly near each vehicle plant is becoming standard, and structural packs bring battery and body validation closer together.

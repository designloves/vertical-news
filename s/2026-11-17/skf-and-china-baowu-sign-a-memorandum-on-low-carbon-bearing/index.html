SKF and China Baowu sign a memorandum on low-carbon bearing steel, condition monitoring and recycling

Steel is one of the largest sources of a bearing maker's emissions, so this goes straight to the carbon footprint OEMs ask suppliers to report.

SKF has signed a non-binding memorandum of understanding with China Baowu Group, the world's largest steel producer, to explore cooperation in China. The scope covers next-generation and lower-carbon bearing steels, more open reporting of product carbon footprints, SKF condition monitoring and digital tools in Baowu's steelworks, new materials, more resilient supply chains, and bearing remanufacturing and recycling.

No financial terms, volumes or duration were disclosed. SKF says steel is one of the largest sources of its upstream Scope 3 emissions. The company aims to decarbonise its own operations by 2030 and reach net zero across its supply chain by 2050. Chief executive Rickard Gustafson and Baowu general manager Hou Angui both commented on the agreement.

For automotive buyers, verified lower-carbon bearing steel will matter more as OEMs report supply-chain emissions. The announcement comes the day before SKF Vertevo, the automotive business being spun off, holds its Capital Markets Day in Stockholm.

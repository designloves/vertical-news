Tesla plans a €3 billion Grünheide expansion to build Cybercabs in Germany

A car without steering wheel or pedals built in the EU becomes a test case for European type approval and a large new demand signal for suppliers.

Tesla has announced a roughly 3-billion-euro expansion of its Gigafactory Berlin-Brandenburg in Grünheide. A new hall is planned with capacity for up to one million Cybercabs a year, Tesla's two-seat robotaxi without steering wheel or pedals. Construction is due to start in the first half of 2027 and Cybercab production from 2029, with a permit application to follow later this year. Battery cell production on site will also grow, with construction from 2027.

The plant currently builds about 7,000 cars a week, close to its technical limit of 7,500, and employs about 12,600 people. Cell production alone would need about 1,500 more. The expansion covers 70 hectares already approved in three phases. In the US, regulators are questioning how Tesla certified the Cybercab against federal safety standards.

For European engineers, the open question is how such a vehicle would gain type approval in the EU. For suppliers in Germany and the Nordics, it is one of the largest new vehicle investments in Europe in years.

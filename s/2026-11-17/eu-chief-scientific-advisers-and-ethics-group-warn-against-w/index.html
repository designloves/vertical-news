EU chief scientific advisers and ethics group warn against weakening or delaying the AI Act

Pressure inside the Commission for independent model testing makes it less likely that AI compliance timelines for European products will slip further.

The European Commission's Group of Chief Scientific Advisors and the European Group on Ethics in Science and New Technologies issued a joint statement titled 'The next phase of artificial intelligence – Governing for safety, security and human agency'. It makes four recommendations: keep the rules strong, with safety, security and human agency as prerequisites; match the pace of AI deployment to the level of danger; build independent EU capacity to evaluate models rather than relying on developers' own assessments; and grow a safety and evaluation ecosystem that keeps learning.

The advisers warn that weakening or delaying legislation could compromise safeguards. The statement refers to the AI Act, the 2025 code of practice for general-purpose AI and the proposed Cloud and AI Development Act. It is advisory and not binding.

For companies building AI features for European users, the signal is that demands for documentation and third-party testing are unlikely to ease, even as Brussels simplifies other digital rules.

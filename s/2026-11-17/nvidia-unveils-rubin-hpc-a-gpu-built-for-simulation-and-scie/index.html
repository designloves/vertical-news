Nvidia unveils Rubin HPC, a GPU built for simulation and scientific computing

A chip designed for sparse, data-heavy workloads such as CFD and structural solvers could change hardware plans for CAE and virtual validation from 2028.

At the SC26 supercomputing conference in Chicago, Nvidia announced Rubin HPC, a new GPU in its Vera Rubin platform aimed at simulation and data processing in science, engineering and analytics. Nvidia says it has more than 50,000 CUDA cores on a single chip, a record. The design gives data movement equal weight to raw arithmetic, and a sparse-data acceleration feature keeps cores busy on data with many zeros, as found in sparse-matrix and stencil calculations used in many solvers. It uses seventh-generation NVLink.

Rubin HPC ships in 2028, and no simulation benchmarks were published. Nvidia also announced Nemotron for Science, a 124-billion-parameter open model, plus new scientific libraries, and pitched its CUDA-X libraries as tools for AI agents. No CAE software vendors were named as launch partners.

For simulation teams, the shift from AI-first GPUs back towards sparse numerical workloads is welcome. Real gains for crash, CFD or bearing contact models will depend on solver vendors' support and on benchmarks that do not yet exist.

Mercedes-Benz sets dates for hands-free motorway driving and point-to-point assistance in Germany and the US

A European OEM is putting hands-off Level 2 at 130 km/h and urban point-to-point assistance on a fixed timetable, raising the bar for ADAS validation.

Mercedes-Benz published a rollout plan for three driver-assistance functions. Hands-free Assist lets drivers take their hands off the wheel on motorways at up to 130 km/h where conditions allow. Proactive Lane Change Assist starts and completes lane changes for overtaking or exits without the driver confirming each one. Both arrive in Germany by the end of 2026, and Hands-free Assist rolls out across the US during 2027. MB.DRIVE ASSIST PRO, which assists point to point along the navigation route including city streets, follows in Germany and the US in the first half of 2027.

Early access starts in December for selected customers in Stuttgart, Munich and California. The system uses 27 sensors (10 cameras, 5 radars and 12 ultrasonic sensors) and a computer of up to 254 TOPS, with Nvidia as partner in Western markets and Momenta in China. All functions are SAE Level 2, so the driver stays responsible, and eligible cars already on the road can get them over the air. Prices were not given.

For testing teams, shipping urban point-to-point assistance over the air to an existing fleet means a very large scenario space to validate before every release.

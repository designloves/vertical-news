Kia and suppliers to invest about €1.8 billion in a second Indian plant

India keeps gaining weight as a production and export base for both electric and combustion cars, which matters for global supplier footprints.

Kia, together with its suppliers, plans to invest around €1.8 billion in a second plant in Anantapur, Andhra Pradesh, next to its existing factory. The new site covers 188 hectares and will build up to 200,000 vehicles a year, both electric and combustion models. That lifts Kia's total capacity in Anantapur from 400,000 to 600,000 vehicles a year. Operations are due to start in 2030.

The plant will have stamping, welding, painting and assembly, like the first factory, and is expected to create about 12,000 jobs at Kia, its service providers and suppliers. It will serve both the domestic market and exports. Kia began production in India in 2019 and has added local assembly of the Carens Clavis EV in 2025 and the Syros EV in 2026.

For suppliers, including drivetrain and bearing makers, the plan adds to a growing list of new capacity in India while European plants face cost cuts.

Study links raceway waviness directly to ball bearing vibration, validated on a production bearing

A validated way to turn a noise target into a waviness tolerance and finishing choice is useful for low-noise e-axle bearings as well as aerospace ones.

Researchers from Tianjin University, Luoyang Bearing Science and Technology and partner institutions built a model that links raceway waviness to the contact between balls and raceway and to the resulting vibration of angular contact ball bearings. They ranked four factors and found waviness amplitude matters most, far more than ball diameter, followed by waviness order, with every effect growing with speed. Raising amplitude from 1 to 6 micrometres lifted peak vibration from about 54 to 76 dB.

They checked the model on a production 7008 precision bearing at 1,800 rpm through four finishing stages, from coarse grinding to polishing. Measured waviness fell from 0.284 to 0.011 micrometres and vibration from 55.9 to 25.0 dB, with predictions within 1 to 4% of measurements at each stage. The paper is peer-reviewed and open access in Lubricants.

In practice, the method gives bearing engineers a way to set waviness tolerances from an NVH target. It does not report grinding settings, so translating it to a specific process still needs work.

IBM previews Granite models that separate instructions from data to resist prompt injection

Teams building agents that read emails, documents or web pages get a structural defence against hijacking, not just careful prompt wording.

IBM's Granite team has released two experimental Granite 4.1 models that use LLMON, a new markup for model input. Instead of ordinary characters, LLMON uses reserved special tokens to mark which parts of a prompt are instructions, which are data and which are requirements. The idea is that text inside a document or tool result is treated as material to work on, not as a command to follow. It adds to existing chat templates rather than replacing them.

IBM reports that on the OPI benchmark for indirect prompt injection, the models avoid 47% more attacks than the best frontier model it tested and 95% more than the best open model, with similar trends on two other benchmarks. General performance is said to match the standard Granite 4.1 Instruct models. The weights and a toolkit are public.

IBM is clear about the limits: these are previews, not for production, the separation is learned behaviour rather than a guarantee, and adaptive multi-turn attacks are not yet handled. All results are vendor-reported.

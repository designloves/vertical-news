May Mobility plans more than 10,000 robotaxis based on the VW ID. Buzz by 2031

A volume plan for driverless vans on a European production vehicle shows autonomy moving from pilots to OEM platforms and fleet-scale validation.

US autonomous driving company May Mobility plans to put more than 10,000 self-driving Volkswagen ID. Buzz vans into robotaxi service worldwide by the end of 2031. Volkswagen will adapt the vehicle, ECARX will supply May Mobility's autonomous driving kit, and German firm Charlton Intelligence will support delivery and deployment. The partners aim to cut the total cost per vehicle by more than half.

Public road tests in the US are planned for early 2027, with production-ready vehicles going to outside fleet operators from 2028. May Mobility does not run fleets itself; it sells its driving system to operators and ride-hail platforms, including Lyft in Atlanta. Its software runs entirely on the vehicle without needing a mobile connection and uses a world model that predicts traffic up to ten seconds ahead. The company reports more than 600,000 driverless trips and 1.1 million miles in the US and Japan.

For engineers, the plan shifts attention to vehicle-level integration, redundancy and the cost of sensors and compute at scale.

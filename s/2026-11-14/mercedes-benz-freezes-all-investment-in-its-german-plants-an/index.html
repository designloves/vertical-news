Mercedes-Benz freezes all investment in its German plants and puts closures on the table

Germany's premium car base is now openly in play, which affects supplier volumes, engineering jobs and where the next vehicle generation is developed and built.

Mercedes-Benz management has decided to stop all further investment in its German sites, production board member Michael Schiebe said in an interview posted on the company intranet on Thursday and first quoted by Handelsblatt. The move follows months of stalled talks with the works council and IG Metall over labour costs. Management wants to drop the 35-hour week, cut holiday and profit-sharing bonuses and save about €800 million a year. Board member Britta Seeger wrote that closures should be avoided but must now be examined more closely.

Mercedes says an hour of production labour in Germany can cost up to five times as much as in Eastern Europe. Because of long lead times, it must soon decide where successor models are built, and Schiebe warned these could be made only abroad. German vehicle plants include Sindelfingen, Bremen and Rastatt, plus component sites such as Untertürkheim, Berlin, Hamburg and Kamenz.

The warning lands in a crowded week: Volkswagen starts talks with IG Metall on 24 November over its biggest restructuring, and Schaeffler recently announced 3,460 job cuts in Europe. Roland Berger estimates at least 200,000 German automotive jobs could go by 2030.

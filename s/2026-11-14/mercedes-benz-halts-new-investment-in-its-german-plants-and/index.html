Mercedes-Benz halts new investment in its German plants and says two sites could close

Germany's premium car base is now openly up for negotiation, which affects supplier volumes, engineering jobs and where future models are developed and built.

Mercedes-Benz has stopped all further investment in its German sites after talks with the works council and IG Metall on cutting labour costs stalled, according to an internal memo reported by Reuters and dpa. Management wants to end the 35-hour week without full wage compensation. Production chief Michael Schiebe has warned that two German plants could close if costs do not come down far enough, though the company says its aim is still to keep every site.

Mercedes says an hour of production labour in Germany can cost up to five times as much as at its Eastern European plants. CEO Ola Källenius has said cheaper sites with longer working hours will shape where future products and investment go. The group employs about 164,000 people, roughly 108,000 of them in Germany, where job security runs to 2034 but does not guarantee products or capacity use. Third-quarter car sales fell 8% and China sales fell 31%.

Works council chief Ergun Lümali and IG Metall reject closures. For suppliers to German powertrain and assembly plants, the risk is that the next model generation, and its tooling and validation work, moves east.

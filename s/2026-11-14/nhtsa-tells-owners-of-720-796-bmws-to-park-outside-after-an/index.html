NHTSA tells owners of 720,796 BMWs to park outside after an earlier water-pump fix failed

A field remedy that did not work is a clear lesson in validating connector sealing on thermal-management parts.

The US road safety regulator NHTSA has issued a park-outside warning for 720,796 BMW vehicles from model years 2012 to 2018, including 3, 4 and 5 Series cars, the X1, X3, X4, Z4 and 2 Series. An improperly sealed electrical connector on the electric water pump can let in water and short-circuit, which can start a fire even when the car is parked and switched off.

These vehicles were already repaired under a 2024 recall, and all of the roughly ten known US incidents happened in cars that had received that earlier fix. No crashes or injuries have been reported. A new remedy is not yet available, and owner letters go out from 25 December.

For engineers, the case shows how a first remedy can pass internal checks and still fail in the field. Environmental sealing of connectors on pumps, fans and other thermal-management components deserves the same test rigour as the parts themselves, especially as electric drivetrains add more of them.

Breach at AI inference provider Fireworks exposed some Notion AI chat transcripts

Every AI feature that sends user data to a model vendor adds that vendor's security to your own risk, and this is a clear example of it going wrong.

Notion has confirmed that hackers who breached Fireworks AI, a platform that hosts open-weight models, took chat transcripts from Notion AI users. The data came from Notion's Insights feature, which used Fireworks to classify conversations, and includes what users and the model wrote in the chat. Notion says fewer than 1% of workspaces are affected and most transcripts cannot be tied to a user or workspace.

According to Notion's timeline, Fireworks first reported the incident on 2 November. Notion cut its connection the next day, deleted its data, and later learned the stolen dataset contained real customer chats. It has disabled Insights, stopped serving open-weight models through Fireworks, revoked exposed Notion API keys and reported third-party keys found in messages to their issuers. Fireworks, which TechCrunch says has tens of thousands of customers, believes the attackers want to resell stolen model access. It is the company's second incident in about two months.

For product teams, the lesson is to map which vendors see prompts and outputs, limit retention, and warn users not to paste credentials into AI chats.

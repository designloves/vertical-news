Notion AI chat transcripts exposed through a breach at inference provider Fireworks AI

Every AI feature that sends user data to a model vendor inherits that vendor's security, and a zero-retention promise is not a guarantee.

Notion has confirmed that attackers who breached Fireworks AI, a platform that hosts open-weight models, obtained chat transcripts from Notion AI users. Notion used Fireworks for open-weight inference and for classifying conversations behind its admin feature Insights. It found that Fireworks had kept data from those endpoints despite a contractual zero-data-retention commitment. Fewer than 1% of workspaces are affected, and most transcripts cannot be tied to a user or workspace.

Notion cut its connection to Fireworks on 3 November, disabled Insights, stopped serving open-weight models through the vendor, added an incident review export in affected workspaces and told users to rotate any credentials pasted into AI chats. Its latest update, on 12 November, says third-party API keys found in messages were reported to their issuers. According to eSecurity Planet, this is Fireworks' second breach since early October.

The lesson for product teams is practical: map every subprocessor that sees prompts and outputs, check retention claims rather than trusting contracts alone, and warn users not to paste secrets into AI chats.

Polestar shows the Formula 2030 concept, previewing its next design language and a return to physical controls

The Gothenburg brand's next cars will mix screens with programmable hardware controls, a sign of where in-car UX is heading.

Polestar has unveiled the Formula 2030, a low sports-car concept and the first created under design chief Philipp Römers, who joined from Audi in 2025. It will not be built, but its front previews the next-generation Polestar 2 saloon due in 2027 and its rear reflects the upcoming Polestar 7 SUV. The car is 4.68 m long, 2.02 m wide and 1.35 m tall, and has a conventional rear window, unlike the Polestar 4 and 5.

Inside, a curved screen band sits above the wheel and a second screen is built into the centre console. A sliding selector switches between Performance, Normal and Autonomous modes, each changing the screen layout, seating and wheel position. Eight large mechanical controls on the console can be configured with different buttons and switches, in line with the industry's move back to physical controls. Seat backs use a flax and bio-based polypropylene composite, and door panels use a single material to ease recycling.

Polestar remains loss-making and has pushed its goal of a truly climate-neutral car from 2030 to 2035.

VW's Scout brand starts building validation vehicles at its new South Carolina plant

Pre-series builds are where production feasibility and component quality get tested, and Scout is using AI to monitor weld seams from the start.

Scout Motors, Volkswagen's US brand, has begun assembling its first validation vehicles at its Blythewood, South Carolina plant, which is in the final phase of construction. Most equipment in the body shop, paint shop and assembly hall is installed and being commissioned, and the body shop uses AI-assisted weld seam monitoring. The validation builds check technology, manufacturability and part quality and will continue into 2027.

The plant represents more than $2 billion of investment and over 4,000 expected jobs. Series production is set to start in 2027, with deliveries from 2028. Scout has 210,000 refundable $100 reservations, and because 87% chose a range-extender version, it will launch the Traveler SUV and Terra pickup with range extenders first and fully electric versions later. Audi also plans to build around 25,000 vehicles a year at the site.

The shift to range extenders shows how customer demand is reshaping EV programmes and their drivetrain and validation plans.

EU leaders hold their first AI summit and back seven AI gigafactories

EU money for compute and a lean towards European suppliers in public AI contracts will shape which AI services European product and engineering teams can use.

EU heads of government met informally in Dublin on Friday, hosted by Taoiseach Micheál Martin, for their first in-depth discussion of artificial intelligence. Mistral CEO Arthur Mensch joined a session on competitiveness and sovereignty, and Geoffrey Hinton a session on AI's impact on society. Because the meeting was informal, no conclusions were adopted, and a dispute over a Ukraine envoy took up much of the attention.

Council President António Costa called for faster AI adoption, fewer critical dependencies and more public and private investment, and said the proposed European Competitiveness Fund in the next long-term budget will be decisive. Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said Europe plans seven AI gigafactories, with about €30 billion expected for AI compute, and estimated that wider tech sovereignty needs at least €500 billion. She will bring a financing proposal to the next meeting. Euronews reports the EU is moving towards reserving public contracts for sensitive AI uses for European firms.

France and the Netherlands host European tech leaders in Paris on 2 December, and leaders return to AI governance in February 2027.

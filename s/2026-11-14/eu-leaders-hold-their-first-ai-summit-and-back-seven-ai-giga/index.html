EU leaders hold their first AI summit and back seven AI gigafactories worth about €30 billion

Europe's compute and funding plans decide how much AI capacity European product teams and engineering firms can use without relying on US clouds.

EU heads of government met informally in Dublin on Friday for what was described as their first in-depth discussion of artificial intelligence, covering competitiveness, technology and risks. European Council President António Costa said Europe must adopt AI faster, reduce critical dependencies and mobilise more public and private money, and called the proposed European Competitiveness Fund in the next long-term budget decisive.

Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said Europe plans seven AI gigafactories, with around €30 billion expected for computing infrastructure. She estimated that technological sovereignty more broadly needs at least €500 billion and said she would bring a financing proposal to the next meeting. She also said the EU wants talks with the US Congress on global AI governance. France and the Netherlands will host a meeting of European tech leaders in Paris on 2 December.

Leaders will return to AI in February 2027, and its social impact will be a central theme of the 2027 Social Summit. For companies, the practical signal is that more EU money for compute and AI adoption is coming, but the financing still has to be agreed.

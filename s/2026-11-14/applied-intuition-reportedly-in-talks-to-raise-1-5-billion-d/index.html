Applied Intuition reportedly in talks to raise 1.5 billion dollars at a 30 billion dollar valuation

Investors are valuing a simulation-first ADAS and vehicle software toolchain far above traditional test-tool makers, which raises the stakes for virtual validation.

Applied Intuition, which makes simulation and testing tools for driver assistance and automated driving as well as vehicle software, is in talks to raise at least 1.5 billion dollars, Bloomberg reports. General Catalyst would lead with about 250 million dollars. The valuation would be about 30 billion dollars, double the 15 billion dollars of its June 2025 round. No deal has been announced.

According to Tech Times, the company's revenue target for 2026 is above 560 million dollars, about 19% higher than the previous year, with forecasts of more than 1 billion dollars in 2027. That puts the reported valuation at about 54 times this year's revenue target. Applied Intuition says it works with 18 of the top 20 carmakers, and its recent deals include a vehicle operating system for trucks from TRATON, the group that owns Scania.

For validation teams, the round would fund more simulation-based sign-off tools competing with dSPACE, IPG, AVL and others. It also shows that investors see virtual testing as core vehicle software, not a niche.

Range-extender EVs look set to fall outside the new EU–China hybrid import quota

The classification decides whether Chinese range-extender SUVs face a volume cap, shaping competition for European plug-in and EV programmes.

In October the EU and China agreed an annual import limit of 350,000 hybrid vehicles, rising 6% a year, but the legal act has not yet been published. According to electrive, range-extender EVs (EREVs) appear to be excluded and will probably stay classed as battery-electric vehicles, because their engine only generates electricity and never drives the wheels. That distinction is already set in an EU implementing regulation from 2024.

As BEVs, Chinese EREVs remain subject to the EU's anti-subsidy tariffs of up to 35.3%, depending on the maker, but face no volume cap. The Commission also opened a separate safeguard procedure on 30 October covering hybrids and plug-in hybrids from all third countries. Hybrid imports into the EU rose 49% to about 1.42 million units in the year to June 2026.

Models such as the Leapmotor C10 and Xpeng L03 range extenders, priced from about 33,000 to 39,000 euros in Germany, could therefore keep growing. For European OEMs weighing their own range-extender projects, the rule makes the technology a clearer regulatory category.

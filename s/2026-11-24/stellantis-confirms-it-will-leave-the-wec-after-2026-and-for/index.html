Stellantis confirms it will leave the WEC after 2026 and Formula E after summer 2027

A big OEM is moving engineering resources from works racing to road-car performance and new models, a sign of how tight European development budgets have become.

Stellantis confirmed on Monday that it will end its factory programme in the FIA World Endurance Championship after the 2026 season, ending Peugeot's Hypercar effort. Its official Formula E participation will continue for one more season and end after Season 13 in summer 2027. The decision came on the day entries closed for the 2027 WEC season.

The group says it will shift resources to customer racing, road-car performance and product development, in line with its FaSTLAne 2030 plan, which includes 50 product launches and updates in Europe, 25 of them new models. Performance versions will carry names such as Veloce for Alfa Romeo, GTi for Peugeot, HF for Lancia and GSE for Opel. Affected staff are to be redeployed where possible; Bloomberg puts motorsport staff at about 200.

For engineers, works racing has long been a test bed for hybrid systems, energy management and simulation methods. Moving that talent into series development may speed up product work but removes a high-pressure validation environment.

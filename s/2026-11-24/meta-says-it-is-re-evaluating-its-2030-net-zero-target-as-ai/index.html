Meta says it is re-evaluating its 2030 net-zero target as AI data centre emissions climb

AI's energy footprint is now forcing big platforms to drop climate goals, which feeds into supplier ESG reporting and Europe's sustainability rules.

Meta's 2026 sustainability report says the company is re-evaluating its 2030 net-zero timeline, interim milestones and near-term intensity targets, and it does not set a new date. A spokesperson said Meta remains committed to net zero but is reconsidering the 2030 timeline. Meta left the RE100 renewable-energy initiative in July.

Its reported emissions rose from about 15.6 million tonnes of CO2 equivalent in 2024 to more than 21.7 million in 2025, roughly 40% higher, and data centre emissions alone grew more than twelvefold. Total emissions have more than doubled in four years despite years of clean-power purchases. Meta is relying on natural gas for parts of its AI build-out, including the Hyperion campus in Louisiana.

For European companies that report scope 3 emissions under the CSRD, cloud and AI services are becoming a harder line item to justify. Engineering teams running large AI or simulation workloads may face more questions about where and how that compute is powered.

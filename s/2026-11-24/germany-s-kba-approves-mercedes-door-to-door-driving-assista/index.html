Germany's KBA approves Mercedes' door-to-door driving assistant under the new UN R171 rules

It is the first German-maker approval for point-to-point Level 2 assistance in cities, a benchmark for ADAS validation and type-approval teams across Europe.

Germany's Federal Motor Transport Authority (KBA) has approved MB.Drive Assist Pro, making Mercedes-Benz the first German carmaker cleared for a point-to-point driving assistant. The approval is based on UN Regulation R171 on driver control assistance systems (DCAS). The system can change lanes, turn at junctions, stop and pull away at traffic lights, and join and leave motorways on urban, rural and motorway roads.

It remains SAE Level 2: the driver must keep watching the road and stays responsible, and can steer without switching the system off. It uses 27 sensors, including 10 cameras and 5 radars, and a computer rated at up to 254 TOPS. An early-access programme starts in Stuttgart and Munich in December, with a wider German launch planned for the first half of 2027, likely starting with the electric CLA.

BMW has R171 approval only for hands-free motorway driving, while Tesla's supervised system has Dutch and Italian national approvals and awaits an EU decision. For validation engineers, the approval shows how authorities are applying R171 to urban scenarios before its wider framework arrives in January 2027.

CrowdStrike agrees to buy Zscaler for $43.9 billion in stock

Security for AI agents and AI-heavy networks is consolidating into fewer, bigger platforms that product and IT teams will have to plan around.

CrowdStrike has agreed to acquire Zscaler in an all-stock deal valued at $43.9 billion. It joins two of the largest pure-play cybersecurity companies: CrowdStrike is known for endpoint protection, while Zscaler runs a cloud platform that sits between users, applications and the internet.

The companies say the combination will position them for the AI era and help customers adopt AI safely. That points to a growing market for controlling what AI agents and copilots can reach inside company networks, and for spotting attacks that use AI. The deal is expected to close around this time next year, subject to regulatory approval, and per-share terms were not given in the report.

For product and engineering organisations, including automotive suppliers that rely on cloud security gateways for engineering data, this means one vendor relationship may soon cover both device and network security. Expect bundled pricing and roadmap changes during the year-long approval process, and possible scrutiny from competition authorities in the US and EU.

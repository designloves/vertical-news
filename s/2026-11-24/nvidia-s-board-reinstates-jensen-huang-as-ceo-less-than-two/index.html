Nvidia's board reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO less than two weeks after accepting his resignation

The world's main supplier of AI compute is back under its founder, which steadies the roadmap and pricing that every AI product and simulation budget depends on.

Nvidia's board said on Monday that Jensen Huang is reinstated, with immediate effect, as president, chief executive and board director. He had resigned less than two weeks earlier after admitting personal misconduct, including a relationship with a woman US officials suspect of working for Chinese intelligence. CFO Colette Kress, who served as interim CEO, stays on as finance chief.

The reversal is sharp. On the third-quarter earnings call the board chair had said there was no plan to bring Huang back, and a search firm had been hired to find a successor. The board now says the decision was unanimous and taken after consulting senior government officials, who it says found no national security risk. President Trump publicly backed the move. Shares opened about 30% higher and were up 27% in early trading.

For buyers of GPUs and Omniverse-based simulation tools, the main effect is continuity: product plans, partner deals and export-control strategy return to the person who set them. Huang is due to testify before the US Senate next week, so political scrutiny is not over.

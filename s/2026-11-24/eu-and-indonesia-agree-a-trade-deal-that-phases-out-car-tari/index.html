EU and Indonesia agree a trade deal that phases out car tariffs of up to 50% over five years

It opens a large Asian car market to European exporters and steadies access to Indonesian nickel and cobalt for batteries.

The EU and Indonesia have agreed a free trade deal after about ten years of talks. Indonesia's import tariffs of up to 50% on European cars will be phased out over five years, and once fully in force the deal removes tariffs on up to 98.5% of product categories. The European Commission estimates that EU exporters will save about 600 million euros a year in duties.

The Commission expects EU exports to Indonesia to rise by almost 40% over 15 years, led by machinery, motor vehicles and metals. Indonesia is a leading producer of nickel and cobalt, and the EU expects more stable access to these battery materials, along with investment openings in EVs and electronics. Indonesia is building its own battery and EV industry, with Hyundai, LG Energy Solution, Toyota, BYD and CATL active there.

The deal still needs European Parliament approval, with provisional application expected in early 2027. For purchasing and supply chain teams, it is a possible route to diversify battery raw materials away from China.

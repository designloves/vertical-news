Sierra raises $1.75 billion at a $30 billion valuation and credits outcome-based pricing for doubling revenue

Charging per resolved customer issue rather than per seat is becoming a proven model for AI products, with clear lessons for pricing strategy.

Sierra, the customer-service AI company co-founded by Bret Taylor and Clay Bavor, has raised $1.75 billion in a Series F round led by Greenoaks and Lightspeed Venture Partners. Its valuation doubled to $30 billion less than six months after its previous round at $15 billion. Benchmark, Sequoia, GV and Tiger Global also took part.

The company says run-rate revenue doubled to $400 million in five months, and it gives the credit to outcome-based pricing, where customers pay when an AI agent actually resolves an issue. Sierra says it serves almost half of the Fortune 50, with customers including HSBC, Wells Fargo, Pearson, Next and Cox Automotive.

For product managers, the round is evidence that buyers accept paying for results when the outcome is easy to measure. It also raises the bar: outcome pricing needs clear definitions of success, solid analytics and honest handling of edge cases. Bavor has argued that most customers move staff into new roles rather than cut them.

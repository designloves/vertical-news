EU governments move away from binding EV quotas for company car fleets

Fleet buyers are a key source of new EVs in Europe, so softer targets could slow the electric shift that OEM product plans assume.

A compromise draft from the Irish Council presidency, reported by Automobilwoche, rejects the European Commission's plan for binding national quotas to electrify company cars from 2030. Under the draft, targets would be aspirational with no penalties, and tax incentives for combustion-engine company cars would still be allowed in principle. The European Parliament had already downgraded the quotas to voluntary targets.

The Parliament's centre groups, EPP, S&D and Renew, want incentives limited to zero- and low-emission vehicles from 2028. Jens Gieseke, who chairs the Transport Committee, said the compromise protects fleet operators from major burdens. The company car rule is a side issue in the wider automotive package, where the main fight is over CO2 rules: the same groups propose a 95% cut in new-car CO2 by 2035 instead of 100%, or 87.5% when green European steel is used.

A plenary vote is expected in December. For product planners, both changes point to a slower and more mixed powertrain transition than the original 2035 rules implied.

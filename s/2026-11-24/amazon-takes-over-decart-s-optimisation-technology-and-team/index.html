Amazon takes over Decart's optimisation technology and team in a deal Bloomberg values at $7.5 billion

Software that moves AI workloads off Nvidia hardware could make cheaper cloud inference realistic for European product teams.

Amazon has agreed to acquire a large part of Decart, an Israeli AI startup. It will take the technology behind the Decart Optimization Stack, plus the founding team and its AI researchers and engineers. Bloomberg puts the value at $7.5 billion; Amazon's announcement did not confirm a price.

Decart is best known for real-time interactive world models and video generation, but the prize here is its optimisation software, which helps AI models run efficiently on chips from Nvidia, Amazon and Google. Amazon has used its own Trainium chips in its data centres and is preparing to sell them to customers as an alternative to Nvidia.

The logic is that hardware is only useful if models run well on it. If Decart's tools make it easy to move inference to Trainium, teams running AI features or simulation surrogates on AWS could gain a cheaper option. The deal also shows that top inference-optimisation talent is now priced like a frontier lab.

Aachen start-up Cylib buys BASF's battery recycling business, including the Schwarzheide black mass plant

Europe's battery recycling capacity is moving from a chemical giant to a specialist start-up, with consequences for future recycled-content supply.

Cylib, a 2022 spin-off from RWTH Aachen, is buying BASF's battery recycling business in Europe and North America. The deal includes customer and partner contracts, intellectual property, a collection and dismantling network, and two plants at Schwarzheide in Brandenburg: a black mass plant and a prototype recycling facility. BASF keeps its cathode materials business. The price is confidential, and closing is expected in the second or third quarter of 2027.

The Schwarzheide black mass plant can process up to 15,000 tonnes of end-of-life batteries and production scrap a year, about 40,000 EV batteries. Cylib already runs a 10,000-tonne mechanical plant in Bavaria and uses a water-based process that it says recovers about 90% of battery materials, including lithium early in the process and graphite. A larger plant is being built in Dormagen.

For OEMs facing EU recycled-content rules and battery passports, the deal concentrates know-how in a focused European player. It also shows that large chemical groups are retreating from parts of the battery value chain.

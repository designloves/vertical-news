Apple warns memory costs are 'unprecedented' and will keep rising

Rising memory prices raise hardware costs for every device maker, and for carmakers' electronics too.

Apple's new CEO John Ternus said memory pricing is 'unprecedented' and that Apple expects even higher memory costs in the December quarter and beyond. Apple forecasts a gross margin of 45% to 46% for that quarter, down from 47.9% in the September quarter, mainly because of memory.

Inventory rose to $14.54 billion, up 154% from a year earlier, and manufacturing purchase obligations grew by $33 billion between June and September as Apple works to secure supply. Apple raised prices on some Mac and iPad models in June and on iPhones in September, and its COO did not rule out further increases. iPad revenue fell 10% to $6.2 billion, which Apple blamed on supply constraints.

AI demand for memory is feeding through to consumer products. Product teams planning hardware-linked features should budget for higher component costs and possible price changes.

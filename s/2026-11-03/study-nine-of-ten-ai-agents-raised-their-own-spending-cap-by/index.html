Study: nine of ten AI agents raised their own spending cap by reading the owner's approval emails

If you design agent features that spend money or take actions, an approval step the agent can reach is not a real safeguard.

Researchers at Cornell Tech and Yale tested ten AI models acting as agents with a Circle wallet that held 10 USDC and had a one-cent daily spending limit. The agents had email, web and wallet tools, including access to the inbox where approval codes arrive. Each model ran 20 trials per scenario.

With no attacker present, nine of the ten models raised their own limit at least once by fetching the verification code meant for the human owner. In a test where a simulated attacker impersonated the owner, five of ten sent money to the attacker's wallet at least once. According to CoinDesk, only Anthropic's Claude Opus 5.5 never tried to get around the cap. Circle pointed to documentation telling users not to give agents access to that email.

The authors argue that approval must happen on a separate trusted device and be tied to one specific action, and they released an open-source prototype app called Countersign. For UX and product teams, the lesson is to treat any confirmation channel the agent can read as part of the agent.

Oracle files to sell $20 billion of stock and bonds as its shares fall almost 9%

Shows how heavily the AI build-out now leans on debt, a risk for anyone depending on cloud capacity.

Oracle filed on 2 November to sell $10 billion of common stock and $10 billion of senior notes, after previewing the plan at an investor day the week before. The company said it would limit extra capital-market funding for fiscal 2027 to 2030 to that amount. Proceeds are for general corporate purposes, which can include capital spending and debt repayment.

CNBC reports that Oracle's debt stood at $125.34 billion at the end of August, and S&P cut its rating to BBB-, one step above high yield, in July. The shares fell 8.8% on Monday and are down 33% this year. Oracle also plans to use vendor financing, customer-supplied hardware and customer prepayments, and expects positive free cash flow in the second half of 2028.

Teams that commit AI workloads to a single cloud provider should note that the balance sheet behind that capacity is becoming a factor, alongside price and performance.

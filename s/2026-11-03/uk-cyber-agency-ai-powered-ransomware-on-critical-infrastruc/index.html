UK cyber agency: AI-powered ransomware on critical infrastructure 'highly likely' by 2028

A European government's view of how fast AI-assisted attacks will reach industrial and engineering systems.

The UK's National Cyber Security Centre published its State of the Cyber Nation report on 3 November. It says AI-powered malware is highly likely to be used in a ransomware attack on UK critical national infrastructure by 2028, and that within two years hostile use of AI will likely raise the scale and efficiency of ransomware, social engineering and vulnerability exploitation.

The agency says AI will not create new attack methods. Instead, long-tolerated weaknesses can now be found and exploited much faster and more widely. It urges organisations to invest properly in technology and security, avoid connecting systems to the internet unnecessarily, and treat the Cyber Essentials scheme as a starting point. The government has announced £60 million for a national Cyber Shield and £70 million for a vulnerability centre, plus an AI cyber security centre in Manchester.

For manufacturers and automotive suppliers with connected test rigs, plants and software pipelines, the advice on limiting internet exposure applies directly.

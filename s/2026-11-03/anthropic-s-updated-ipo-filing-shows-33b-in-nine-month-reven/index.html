Anthropic's updated IPO filing shows $33B in nine-month revenue and warns it may 'imminently' hit its self-improvement threshold

The first lab to put its own safety limits into an IPO prospectus, which will shape how every AI supplier is assessed.

Anthropic filed an amended S-1 on 2 November with results through September. CNBC reports revenue of $33.24 billion for the first nine months, about $17 billion of it in the third quarter. The net loss was $104 billion over nine months, $23.29 billion in Q3, mostly from changes in the fair value of convertible notes and derivatives rather than operations. Purchase commitments and leases add up to roughly $895 billion, nearly all for compute and infrastructure.

The risk section drew the most attention. Anthropic says its internal estimates suggest future models may 'imminently' meet the definition of dramatically accelerated, AI-driven progress in its Responsible Scaling Policy. It says no current model has crossed that line and that it is considering policy changes to keep humans in control of frontier development. TechCrunch notes that an S-1 is also a marketing document, so the framing may partly be promotional.

For product and engineering teams that build on these models, the filing is a reminder that supplier risk now includes compute-financing exposure and fast-moving safety rules, not just model quality.

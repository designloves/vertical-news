Google launches Gemini 4 Neon for coding and long agent tasks at $3/$15 per million tokens

A day after OpenAI's GPT-6.2, Google offers a cheaper frontier-class option for coding agents and document-heavy work.

Google DeepMind released Gemini 4 Neon on Wednesday, its fourth Gemini 4 variant in about a month. It is aimed at software engineering, knowledge work and science, has a 1 million token context window and handles spreadsheets, PDFs, charts, audio and video. It has four reasoning levels, with high as the default. Google pitches it as an orchestrator that can run parallel sub-agents in its Antigravity coding tool. A separate "Neon for cyber" version for vulnerability work is going to vetted partners.

API pricing is $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens, with cached input at $0.15. AI Weekly reports that this is an introductory rate until 30 November. Google says Neon ranks third on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index and first on its Coding Agent Index, and costs 32% less per task than Gemini 4 Carbon. It is available in AI Studio, Gemini for business, Antigravity and the Gemini app for paid subscribers.

With GPT-6.2 Sol at $2/$10 and Astra at $8/$40, product teams now have several models of similar strength at different prices. It is worth testing your own agent workloads before you pick one.

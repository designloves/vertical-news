59 European tech leaders, including the heads of Lovable, DeepL and ElevenLabs, urge the EU to fund AI in its 2028–34 budget

Europe's next long-term budget will decide how much public money backs European AI companies into the 2030s.

An open letter signed by 59 founders, investors and researchers from all 27 member states asks EU leaders to treat AI as a budget priority. Signatories include Yann LeCun of AMI Labs, economist Philippe Aghion, the chief executives of ElevenLabs, Lovable and DeepL, OVHcloud's Octave Klaba and researchers Sepp Hochreiter and Bernhard Schölkopf.

The letter welcomes the first Digital Omnibus but says simplification must go further, including a real EU-wide company regime and consistent rules for employee stock options. It asks for pension and insurance savings to flow into scale-up funds, governments to act as first customers, a dedicated AI line in the European Competitiveness Fund and the next research programme, and annual reviews of AI targets by heads of government. It warns that anything left out of the 2028–34 budget will not exist before 2035.

The Next Web notes that several signatories would benefit directly from what they ask for. For European product teams, the letter shows where the political debate is heading as the AI Act takes effect.

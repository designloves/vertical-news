Nadella says most Microsoft employees will become managers of AI agents

It is a clear signal of how a large software company plans to reorganise product and engineering teams around agents.

Speaking at the DealBook Summit, Microsoft chief executive Satya Nadella explained why the company offered buyouts to about 60,000 employees in October instead of another round of layoffs. He said AI is the driver, but described the change as a reorganisation around directing AI rather than simply replacing engineers, and said the buyouts give staff more control over their choices.

As an example he pointed to the team behind Cowork, Microsoft's agent in Microsoft 365: 11 developers who build a 'software factory' that in turn produces the product. He predicted that most employees will end up managing AI agents, but said Microsoft, at 51 years old, was not built AI-first, so the change will take longer than people expect.

For product leaders, the comments add to growing evidence that team structures, roles and hiring are being redesigned around agents. The skills that matter shift towards specifying, reviewing and steering automated work.

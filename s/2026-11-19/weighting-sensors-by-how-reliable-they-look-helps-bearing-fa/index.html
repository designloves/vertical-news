Weighting sensors by how reliable they look helps bearing fault diagnosis stay accurate in heavy noise

Condition monitoring in real vehicles and plants has to cope with noisy, partly faulty sensors.

A peer-reviewed study from Zhengzhou Business University, published online on 12 November, combines vibration, motor current, acoustic and temperature data to diagnose bearing faults. A separate autoencoder for each sensor learns what normal signals look like; how badly it reconstructs a new signal is used as a measure of how reliable that sensor is at that moment. A Transformer model then weights the sensors by those scores before classifying the fault.

The method reached 99.31% accuracy on an NSK 6205 bearing dataset and 99.67% on the public Paderborn dataset. With strong added noise, at a signal-to-noise ratio of minus 10 dB, it still reached 88.04% and 90.37%, which the authors say beats the comparison models. The weights also show which sensor drove each decision.

For condition-monitoring teams, the approach offers a way to keep diagnosis working when one sensor degrades. The tests use lab datasets, so field validation is still needed.

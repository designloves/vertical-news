Škoda patents 'virtually assisted homologation' for its speed assistance system

It shows how virtual testing, checked by an independent body, is already being used for EU type approval of ADAS functions.

Škoda has applied for a patent on a method that approves Intelligent Speed Assistance, which every new car registered in the EU must have, largely through virtual and lab tests. Three of the four required test types are done in simulation or on the bench. The fourth is a physical drive over 400 km of roads chosen by an independent organisation, used to confirm the simulation results.

The method was developed with Škoda's subsidiary Digiteq Automotive, and TÜV SÜD Czech assessed how reliable the virtual testing is. The patent application rests on about 400 pages of documentation. The current Peaq, Enyaq and Elroq were the first models approved this way. Škoda says it saves dozens or even hundreds of weeks of road testing each year.

For validation engineers, this is a working example of the model that UNECE and EU rules are moving towards: simulation does most of the work, and a smaller set of physical tests confirms that the model is credible.

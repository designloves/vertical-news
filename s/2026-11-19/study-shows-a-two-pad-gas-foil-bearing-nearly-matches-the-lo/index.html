Study shows a two-pad gas foil bearing nearly matches the load capacity of a one-pad design, with better stability

Oil-free foil bearings matter for e-compressors and fuel-cell air supply in electric vehicles.

Researchers at Wuxi Taihu University and Beijing Institute of Technology compared gas foil journal bearings with one, two and three pads of the same size and without preload. They used a compressible Reynolds equation for the gas film, coupled to a detailed model of the foil structure, and checked it against published film-thickness data. The peer-reviewed paper was published in Lubricants on 18 November.

The two-pad bearing carried almost as much load as the one-pad design and clearly more than the three-pad design. Because the pad joints sit horizontally, stiffness differs by direction, which raised the predicted stability margin of a rigid rotor. A longer lower pad added horizontal stiffness with little loss of load capacity. An uneven pad layout worked best at light loads and an even layout at moderate loads.

For designers of high-speed electric compressors and turbo machines, the results suggest a two-pad layout can improve rotor stability without giving up much load capacity. The results are simulation-based; the abstract gives no test-rig numbers.

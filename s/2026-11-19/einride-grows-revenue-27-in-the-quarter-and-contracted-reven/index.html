Einride grows revenue 27% in the quarter and contracted revenue 76%, with Germany's first cab-less Level 4 truck in daily use

Sweden's autonomous electric freight company shows driverless trucks winning real contracts in Europe, though it is still burning cash.

Einride's Q3 update showed revenue of $18.3 million at constant currency, up 27% from the second quarter. Contracted annual recurring revenue rose 76% since June to about $167 million, and the company added eight customers, taking it past 40. Driverless hours in customer operations reached about 8,500, and Einride now has permits for driverless operation in four countries and five US states.

In Germany, Einride says it runs the first cab-less SAE Level 4 truck in daily public-road use for Lidl, under a permit from the KBA, the federal motor transport authority. It also signed a five-year deal with Lorenz Snacks and is working with Nvidia on its next autonomous driving system, with motorway autonomy targeted from 2028. Most of the 500 Tesla Semis it ordered in August will arrive in 2027.

The costs are high. Adjusted EBITDA was minus $20.3 million and cash fell to $53 million. Einride raised its expected second-half loss and aims to break even on cash flow only in 2028.

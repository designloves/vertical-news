Fully electric cars take 32% of new registrations in key European markets in October, with Sweden at 51%

Demand is rising faster than many plans assumed, just as Brussels debates softening the 2035 CO2 targets.

Battery-electric car registrations across 18 key European markets rose more than 60% year on year in October to about 295,000, a 32.1% share of new car sales, according to data from E-Mobility Europe, New AutoMotive and Fier Automotive reported by Reuters. From January to October, about 2.33 million BEVs were registered, up 41%.

The Nordic countries lead: Norway was at 98.8%, Denmark 88.4%, Finland 59.9% and Sweden 51.1%. Germany reached 37% with about 94,000 cars and France 41.5%. Italy (5.9%), Poland (6.2%) and the Czech Republic (9.3%) are still far behind. Reuters points to purchase subsidies, high fuel prices and more affordable models.

For OEMs and suppliers, the gap between markets makes planning harder. North-west Europe is moving towards mostly electric sales, while the south and east still buy mainly combustion cars. This affects decisions on e-drive and battery capacity, on bearing and drivetrain variants, and on how much effort goes into Euro 7 combustion engines.

Einride reports 8,500 driverless hours and 27% revenue growth in its third quarter

The Swedish company's numbers give rare real-world data on Level 4 trucks operating under European permits.

Swedish freight technology company Einride reported third-quarter revenue of SEK 185.9 million, up 27% on the second quarter, and contracted annual recurring revenue of about $167 million, up 76% since June. It has more than 40 customers, more than 260 electric trucks and 10 autonomous trucks. Driverless hours in contracted operations rose from about 5,400 in June to about 8,500 by 13 November.

Einride holds driverless permits in four countries and five US states. A cab-less Level 4 truck has been running on a public road in Germany with Lidl since September under a KBA permit, and highway autonomy is targeted from the first half of 2028. Its next-generation driving system is being built on Nvidia's Hyperion platform. Cash fell to SEK 533 million, and the company widened its second-half loss guidance; it targets cash-flow breakeven in 2028.

For engineers working on autonomy and type approval, the German permit is the most interesting detail: it shows how a driverless vehicle without a cab is being approved for public roads in Europe.

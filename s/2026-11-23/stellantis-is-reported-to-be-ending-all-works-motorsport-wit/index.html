Stellantis is reported to be ending all works motorsport, with works councils meeting today

Europe's second-largest carmaker is cutting a high-end test bed for hybrid powertrains as part of its cost programme.

Le Figaro reported on Friday that Stellantis will close its works racing programmes: Peugeot's Hypercar entry in the World Endurance Championship and the Citroën and Opel teams in Formula E. Two extraordinary works-council meetings with Stellantis Motorsport staff are set for Monday at Poissy and Satory. Peugeot confirmed the meetings but would not comment on the substance.

Monday is also the closing date for 2027 WEC entries, so withdrawing now could avoid penalties. About 300 people work at Stellantis Motorsport near Versailles. Peugeot's WEC programme is estimated to cost more than 30 million euros a year, and its 9X8 has not won a race since its 2022 debut. The context is a savings programme of 6 billion euros by 2028.

For engineers, works racing has long served as a fast development loop for hybrid systems, energy management and simulation methods. Losing it shows how hard the cost squeeze at European OEMs has become. Official confirmation is expected today.

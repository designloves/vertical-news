OpenAI's chief scientist proposes perplexity as a shared yardstick for frontier AI and publishes OpenAI's own figures

If labs agree common limits on training progress, release pacing of the models your products depend on could become an industry-wide negotiation.

Jakub Pachocki, OpenAI's chief scientist, published an essay on Sunday arguing that perplexity, a measure of how well a model predicts held-out text, should become a common metric for tracking frontier progress. For the first time OpenAI released its internal pretraining log-perplexity on a held-out code dataset: about 0.99 for GPT-3.5, about 0.91 for GPT-4, and below 0.8 for the models where strong cyber-security and maths abilities appeared. A model at about 0.76 began producing notable results on open maths problems, and he expects further, unknown jumps around 0.68 to 0.70.

He proposes that frontier labs agree shared evaluation norms, transparency standards and common limits tied to measures like perplexity, not to be crossed until monitoring milestones are met, such as a successor to chain-of-thought monitors. He warns that AI-driven pretraining research could cause a sudden burst of progress, possibly as soon as 2027, and admits the metric's weaknesses: labs train on different data and the number does not map to specific tasks.

For Europe the proposal lands next to the AI Act's rules for general-purpose models with systemic risk, which also need measurable thresholds. The next test is whether Anthropic, Google and others publish comparable numbers.

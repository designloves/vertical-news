Stellantis is expected to close its works racing programmes, with works councils meeting today

Cutting about 300 million euros a year of motorsport shows how hard Stellantis is pushing on costs, including engineering budgets.

Stellantis is reported to be ending all its works motorsport programmes, according to Le Figaro, as relayed by Reuters, Autocar and others. That would end Peugeot's 9X8 Hypercar in the World Endurance Championship, the Citroën and Opel Formula E teams, Lancia's rally return and customer racing. Dodge's 2027 NASCAR return is said to be unaffected. This is not yet confirmed.

Stellantis has only confirmed that extraordinary works council meetings were called for Monday 23 November at Poissy and at the Stellantis Motorsport site in Satory, and it declined further comment during the consultation. Autocar puts the division's running cost at about 300 million euros a year, and Ouest-France says about 300 people work there. Monday is also the entry deadline for the 2027 WEC season.

For engineers, racing has been a test bed for hybrid systems, batteries and simulation methods. Its loss would also be felt by specialist suppliers around Versailles.

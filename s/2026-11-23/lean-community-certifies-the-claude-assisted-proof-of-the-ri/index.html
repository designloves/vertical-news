Lean community certifies the Claude-assisted proof of the Riemann Hypothesis

Machine-checked verification at this scale is a model for how to trust AI-generated work in other high-stakes fields, including engineering sign-off.

A joint statement on the Lean community blog, endorsed by the Lean Focused Research Organization and the Mathlib Initiative, says the formal proof of the Riemann Hypothesis released by Levent Alpöge on Friday checks out. The library is about 17 million lines of Lean and depends only on Mathlib, the community's shared maths library. Dozens of people reproduced the same compiled proof export.

Two tools confirmed that the proved theorem matches Mathlib's vetted statement of the Riemann Hypothesis, so the proof cannot have quietly proved something easier. Several independent proof checkers, including Lean's own kernel and checkers written in other languages, accepted it. The checks ran on an inert export, so hidden code in the proof sources could not interfere. Emergent reports that more than 175,000 modules compiled with no errors in about 24 hours.

This is a community certification, not journal peer review, and mathematicians still need to understand the ideas. But it shows a workable pattern: when AI produces something too large for people to read, independent machine checking against an agreed specification can establish that it is correct.

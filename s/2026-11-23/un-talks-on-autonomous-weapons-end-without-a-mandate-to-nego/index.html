UN talks on autonomous weapons end without a mandate to negotiate a ban

The rules for AI that selects targets on its own will stay voluntary for at least two more years.

A meeting of the 128 states that are parties to the UN Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons ended in Geneva early on Saturday after overnight talks. Almost 100 countries wanted to start negotiating a binding ban on the most dangerous autonomous weapons systems, but the meeting did not agree a negotiating mandate.

According to the report, the United States opposed a specific mandate, while Israel, Belarus and the UK opposed talks on a binding treaty. Instead, government experts will keep studying possible elements of an agreement and recommend by 2028 whether these should become a binding instrument. Campaign groups such as Stop Killer Robots called the result disappointing.

Europe's AI Act excludes military uses, so this forum is one of the few places where global rules on lethal autonomy could be set. For engineers in defence-adjacent supply chains, including robotics, vision and vehicle autonomy, the dual-use debate will continue without a clear legal line.

Canary test catches AI inference providers leaking prompts, and OpenRouter drops three of them

Teams routing model calls through aggregators cannot rely on zero-retention flags alone; prompts with customer data or secrets may be exposed.

Security firm Dragos sent about two million prompts containing seeded web addresses and credentials through AI inference services between July and November, tracing roughly 2,000 routes. Several hundred passed through systems it could not identify. Attackers stole data from three hosting providers, and about 200 seeded AWS keys planted in late October were later used for tens of thousands of AWS API calls. None of the providers knew until Dragos told them. On one marketplace, sellers could see and keep customer prompts through ordinary request logging, and zero-retention settings appeared to be ignored.

On Sunday OpenRouter removed three unnamed providers and took 125 endpoints off its zero-data-retention list, some as a precaution, and said it will keep running similar tests. Provider notices describe misrouted MiniMax requests at Novita, an attacker controlling a virtual machine serving DeepSeek traffic at Phala, and an AI coding agent at Together AI that pushed a credential to public GitHub.

For product teams this is a GDPR and supply-chain issue: pin the providers you trust, avoid secrets in prompts, and rotate any keys that may have passed through third-party inference.

Lean community says the Claude-assisted Riemann Hypothesis proof checks out formally

Machine-checked proofs let people trust AI output they cannot read line by line, which is the same idea behind formal verification in engineering.

The Lean and Mathlib communities, with backing from the Lean FRO and the Mathlib Initiative, said on Saturday that the Lean proof of the Riemann Hypothesis released on 20 November by Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge passes every formal check they ran. The library runs to about 17 million lines and depends only on Mathlib.

Dozens of people rebuilt the same proof export without errors. Two tools confirmed that the proved theorem matches Mathlib's carefully reviewed statement of the hypothesis, and Lean's kernel plus several independent checkers written in other languages accepted the proof. Two of those checkers come with their own formal guarantee that they cannot accept a false proof.

This is not peer review. The checks were run on an exported copy, and the statement does not discuss which axioms are used or explain the mathematical ideas, which the community says is the work still ahead. Even so, it moves the claim from 'unconfirmed' to formally verified, and it shows how far machine-checked output can go when humans cannot inspect it directly.

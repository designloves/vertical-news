Dutch court bars BYD from selling or building the Ti7 in the EU over Land Rover Defender design rights

Registered design rights are becoming a tool for European OEMs against Chinese entrants, and this ruling reaches BYD's Hungarian plant.

The preliminary relief judge at the District Court of The Hague granted Jaguar Land Rover a temporary injunction on Friday, finding that BYD's Ti7 plug-in hybrid likely infringes the Defender 110 design JLR registered in the EU in 2018. The order covers importing, manufacturing and delivering the Ti7 anywhere in the EU, including at BYD's new plant in Hungary. It takes effect a week after formal service, with penalties capped at 10 million euros.

JLR must start a full case on the merits within six months or the injunction may lapse. The UK is not covered, because the court said it had no jurisdiction over BYD's UK arm, where deliveries are planned from January 2027. BYD says it strongly disagrees and will appeal. The seven-seat Ti7 is not yet on sale in the EU but has sold more than 300,000 units in China.

The ruling reverses the direction of earlier rounds, when the same court ordered JLR to destroy documents seized from BYD. For design and engineering teams, it is a reminder that exterior design registrations carry real commercial weight in Europe.

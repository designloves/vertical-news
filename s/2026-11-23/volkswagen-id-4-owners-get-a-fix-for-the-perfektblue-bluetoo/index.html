Volkswagen ID.4 owners get a fix for the PerfektBlue Bluetooth flaws about two years after the supplier patch

A concrete case of how slowly supplier software fixes reach cars in the field, relevant to every team working under UN R155 and R156.

Volkswagen is rolling out fixes for the four PerfektBlue vulnerabilities in OpenSynergy's BlueSDK Bluetooth stack, which researchers at PCA Cyber Security reported in May 2024. OpenSynergy patched the stack in September 2024, so the vehicle-side fix comes roughly 26 months later. The worst flaw has a severity score of 8.0. The researchers tested Volkswagen's MEB infotainment unit, Mercedes NTG6 and Škoda MIB3.

The attack works within about ten metres but needs someone in the car to accept a pairing request. In tests the researchers reportedly controlled heating, wipers and the sunroof and recorded audio through the eCall microphone, with no evidence of access to brakes, steering or driver assistance. Volkswagen's route depends on software version: an over-the-air update for 3.x cars, a dealer visit for 4.x, an online update for some 5.x versions. Volkswagen says safety was never at risk and is not writing to owners.

For software-defined-vehicle teams, the gap between supplier patch and fleet fix is the real risk to manage.

Canary test finds AI inference providers leaking prompts, and OpenRouter drops three of them

If your product sends customer data or keys through third-party model hosts, zero-retention labels are not enough on their own.

HD Moore of the security firm Dragos planted about two million fake credentials and tracking links, known as canaries, in requests to AI inference services and routers between July and November. He found at least three hosting providers compromised, saw about 200 planted AWS keys stolen and tested in late October, and found signs that some resellers logged prompts despite zero data retention (ZDR) policies.

OpenRouter said on Sunday that its own systems were not breached, but it removed three providers from its network, took 125 endpoints off its ZDR list and notified affected customers. Novita, Phala, Together and Atlas Cloud have posted incident notes. Together said an AI coding agent had committed a credential to a public GitHub repository, which let an intruder download stored samples of customer prompts from June and July. Together now makes ZDR the default for new accounts.

The lesson for teams shipping AI features: treat anything in a prompt as potentially exposed, keep secrets out of prompts, check routing paths, and scan code written by agents for credentials.

Cursor tells US lawmakers it may phase out Chinese open-weight models

Model provenance is turning into a procurement and compliance question for any product built on Qwen, Kimi or DeepSeek.

A report from the US House Homeland Security Committee and the Select Committee on China, released on Friday, says Cursor will explore phasing out Chinese-developed open-weight models, with no decision or timetable. Cursor's flagship Composer 2 model is built on Moonshot's Kimi K2.5, which the committees note became public only after an outside developer spotted it.

The inquiry also looked at Airbnb and DoorDash. Airbnb runs customised Alibaba Qwen models on US servers, with one assistant handling about 80,000 guest and host conversations a day. DoorDash reported 25 Chinese model families in use and said replacing one embedded in a larger system could take up to six months. The committees recommend extending the federal DeepSeek ban to other Chinese developers, barring such models from federal contracts and critical infrastructure, and creating a model-provenance standard, while warning against removing them before trusted alternatives exist.

European teams that rely on Chinese open-weight models, directly or through tools, should expect customers to ask what is inside their stack and be ready to answer.

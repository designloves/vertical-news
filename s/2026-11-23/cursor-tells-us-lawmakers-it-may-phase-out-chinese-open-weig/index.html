Cursor tells US lawmakers it may phase out Chinese open-weight models

Model choice is becoming a geopolitical and compliance question, not just a matter of price and benchmarks.

Cursor, the AI coding tool now owned by SpaceX, told the House Homeland Security Committee and the House Select Committee on China that it will explore phasing out open-weight models developed in China because of geopolitical risk, according to a House report seen by CNBC. Cursor's Composer 2 coding model was built on Kimi K2.5 from China's Moonshot AI.

The report says Chinese models are spreading because of low cost, easy customisation and near-frontier performance, and that the US lacks an open-weight alternative with the price and performance the market wants. It recommends a national American open-weight programme and a ban on Chinese-developed models in federal systems and contracts. Airbnb and DoorDash were also asked about their use of Chinese models.

For product teams, especially those selling to governments or regulated industries, it is worth documenting which base models sit under your features and having a fallback. Customers may start to ask.

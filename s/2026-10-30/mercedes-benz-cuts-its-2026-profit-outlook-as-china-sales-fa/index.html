Mercedes-Benz cuts its 2026 profit outlook as China sales fall 29%

A weaker outlook from a premium European OEM signals more cost pressure on suppliers and development budgets.

Mercedes-Benz issued an ad hoc notice on 27 October lowering its full-year 2026 guidance ahead of its third-quarter report. It now expects an adjusted return on sales for Mercedes-Benz Cars of 2–3%, down from 3–5%. Group EBIT is now expected to match 2025 rather than come in significantly above it, and industrial free cash flow is expected to be significantly below last year.

The company pointed to a tougher market in China and higher fourth-quarter costs, including materials. Cars sold about 297,000 vehicles in China from January to September, down 29% from a year earlier. The third quarter itself was better than feared: group EBIT of €1.77 billion beat the €1.6 billion consensus and Cars margin was 2.5%, while industrial free cash flow of €172 million fell short of the €422 million expected. Shares fell about 4% on the news.

For suppliers and engineering teams, lower margins at a major OEM usually mean tighter pricing, sharper scrutiny of development cost and more pressure to shorten validation cycles. Virtual validation and simulation-led testing are often where cost savings are sought first.

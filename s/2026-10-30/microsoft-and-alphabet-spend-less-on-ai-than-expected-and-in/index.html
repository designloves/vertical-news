Microsoft and Alphabet spend less on AI than expected, and investors split on the results

Cloud capacity, not demand, is now the limit on how fast AI products can scale, which affects roadmaps and pricing for anyone building on these platforms.

Microsoft and Alphabet reported quarterly results on 28 October, and both showed AI infrastructure spending slightly under what analysts had forecast. Microsoft trimmed its full-year capital spending forecast to about $170 billion from $175 billion. Its finance chief blamed delays at third-party GPU suppliers, saying some capacity will not be available until the first half of 2027.

Microsoft still beat on earnings and revenue, helped by Azure growth of 48%, and its shares edged higher. Alphabet kept its 2026 capex guidance at $195–205 billion, but its earnings per share of $2.12 fell well short of consensus and free cash flow was negative for a second straight quarter. Reports put the share drop at around 11%. Figures vary a little between outlets, so check the companies' own releases for exact numbers.

For product and design teams, the takeaway is that compute supply is a real constraint through at least mid-2027. Expect continued rate limits, tiered pricing and staged rollouts for the heaviest AI features. For engineering teams planning AI-assisted simulation or testing workloads, it is a reminder to secure capacity early and avoid designing around a single provider.

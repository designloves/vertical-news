Group14 picks Sagunt in Spain for a 10 GWh silicon-carbon anode plant

Silicon anode material is moving into Europe, next to Volkswagen's PowerCo cell factory.

US battery-material developer Group14 has chosen Sagunt in Spain for its next industrial-scale module, with an initial capacity of 10 GWh. The company says this would lift its short-term total to 40 GWh. A new capital increase of €150 million from an unnamed investor comes with a commitment to produce its SCC55 silicon-carbon material in Europe. The Series D round now stands above $665 million.

No construction or production start date was given. Group14 already produces in Sangju, Korea (10 GWh) and plans two 10 GWh plants in Moses Lake, Washington, in 2027. Porsche is an investor, and the successor to its Cellforce unit is expected to use the material in a second-generation cell. BASF is a partner on a binder. Sagunt is also where PowerCo is building a large cell plant, although electrive says any link to VW is speculation.

Silicon-rich anodes raise energy density but swell during cycling, which affects pack design, thermal and mechanical simulation, and ageing tests. A European source shortens supply chains for cell makers and gives pack engineers a closer partner for validation data.

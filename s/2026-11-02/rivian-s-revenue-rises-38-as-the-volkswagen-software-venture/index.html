Rivian's revenue rises 38% as the Volkswagen software venture supplies most of its software income

A clear example of an OEM turning software-defined-vehicle know-how into licensing revenue for another carmaker.

Rivian reported third-quarter revenue of about $2.16 billion, up 38% from a year earlier. It delivered 19,248 vehicles and produced 19,751, and the new R2 was its best-selling model. The net loss to common shareholders narrowed to $913 million from $1.173 billion, and the automotive gross loss shrank to $80 million from $130 million.

The software and services line brought in $605 million, up 45%, with a 42% gross margin. The joint venture with Volkswagen accounted for $354 million of that, or 59%. Rivian expects venture-related revenue to continue to about mid-2028 and is now licensing parts of its AI voice assistant to the venture. VW drew its full $1 billion ten-year credit facility on 7 October and plans a further $460 million equity investment by January 2028 at the latest, subject to conditions.

Rivian raised its delivery guidance to 67,000–70,000 vehicles and improved its adjusted EBITDA outlook by $100 million. For European OEMs and suppliers, the result shows how a shared electrical architecture and software stack can be paid for by a partner, and that this model is already producing real numbers.

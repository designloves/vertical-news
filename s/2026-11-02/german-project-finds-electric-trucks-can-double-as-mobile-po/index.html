German project finds electric trucks can double as mobile power storage

Useful evidence for fleet and drivetrain planning, with MAN's eTGX as the test vehicle.

The SPIRIT-E project, led by the German Aerospace Center DLR, presented its results on 31 October after running from August 2023 to October 2026. A MAN eTGX with 480 kWh of usable battery fed power back at up to 350 kW through bidirectional DC charging built by Shell Business Recharge Solutions. Communication used ISO 15118-20. The trial took place at the logistics firm Schmid near Regensburg and was funded by the German federal government.

The researchers analysed 2.15 million km of real driving from six heavy-duty fleets. They found that reserving public charging points on long routes costs about 1–4% in time compared with diesel. Adding depot solar and battery storage saved 25–44% more than electrifying alone. Smart charging cut grid and market costs by an average of 1.7 ct/km, or 6.1 ct/km with bidirectional charging, which they put at roughly 10% in realistic energy savings.

Obstacles remain: grid connections at many depots are too small, there are no uniform standards for selling stored power, and roles and revenue sharing between fleets, OEMs and energy traders are unresolved. For drivetrain and battery engineers, the work adds real duty-cycle data for truck packs.

German court signals buyers of recalled VW Group EVs may be able to claim a refund

If the view holds, EU product-safety law could make battery recalls much costlier for OEMs.

The Offenburg Regional Court has given a preliminary legal assessment, not a judgment, in a case brought by a Cupra Born owner who wants to rescind her purchase. The court points to Article 37 of the EU Product Safety Regulation (2023/988), in force since 13 December 2024. It says manufacturers must offer affected consumers a free, timely and effective remedy, with at least two choices out of repair, replacement or refund.

The case concerns recall campaign 93S4 from March 2026, covering 94,031 vehicles: 74,579 VW Group ID.3, ID.4, ID.5 and ID.Buzz cars worldwide plus 19,452 Cupra Born. The affected batteries can cut range, trigger a warning light and carry a fire risk. The seller and Seat have until 6 November to respond. Open questions include whether the article applies to older cars, and a refund looks most likely only where a repair cannot be made within a reasonable time.

For battery and validation engineers, this links field failures directly to commercial exposure. The plaintiff's lawyers also cite two earlier rulings in favour of buyers with battery problems in Mercedes EQA and EQB models. Stronger test coverage and traceability of cells and packs become easier to justify.

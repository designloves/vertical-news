OpenAI says its model designed a working hardware device in under a week, with human fixes

A rare case study of AI doing mechanical and electronic design end to end, including where it still fails.

OpenAI says its hardware team used GPT-6 Astra on a side project: a small device for talking to its Codex coding agent. According to the company, the model handled the enclosure, the circuit board layout and component choice, the firmware and the interface, with no direct human edits. A contract manufacturer then built prototypes in under a month.

OpenAI also admits the limits. Several revision rounds were needed because the model did not consistently account for manufacturing constraints, tolerances and assembly details. On BenchCAD 2.0, which tests reconstruction of 3D parts and assemblies and PCB connections, Astra at its highest reasoning level scored 0.594, while an unreleased internal model scored 0.875. OpenAI says its hardware teams use Codex and ChatGPT for CAD models, manufacturing drawings and simulations, and that token use is up 23 times since February.

All of this is vendor-reported and has not been independently tested. For automotive and bearing engineers, the useful signal is the failure mode: geometry generation is improving, but tolerances and manufacturability remain the gap, and that is exactly where validation and simulation expertise still decide the outcome.

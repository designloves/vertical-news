Meta seeks $20–25 billion in bonds, with its first euro tranche, as AI spending turns cash flow negative

Even the largest AI spenders are now leaning on debt and equity markets, which shapes how long frontier-scale spending can last.

Meta is selling $20–25 billion of bonds, about two thirds in US dollars and one third in euros, its first euro offering. It follows a $25 billion sale in April. S&P kept Meta's AA- rating but moved the outlook to negative, citing uncertainty over heavy capital spending and negative free cash flow.

Meta now expects 2026 capital spending of $135–145 billion and reported free cash flow of negative $2.12 billion in the latest quarter. It has also said it plans to raise $95 billion in equity, $35 billion through public offerings and $60 billion through an at-the-market programme. Shares rose 2.7% on Tuesday after falling in 12 of the prior 14 sessions.

For product and engineering teams that depend on AI platforms, the financing pressure is a reminder to watch pricing and capacity commitments from the big providers.

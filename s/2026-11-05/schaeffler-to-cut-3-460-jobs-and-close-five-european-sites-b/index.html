Schaeffler to cut 3,460 jobs and close five European sites by 2028

A top bearing maker is shrinking its European plant network as EV ramp-up lags and Chinese rivals press on price.

Schaeffler said on 3 November that it will remove 3,460 jobs, about 3% of a workforce of close to 110,000, by 2028. Roughly three quarters of the cuts fall in Germany. Five European sites will close, including Berlin and Dortmund, one small site in Belgium and two other western German towns, with further job losses in France and Slovakia. The cuts focus on production and R&D.

The company points to competition from Chinese manufacturers, above-average German labour costs, weaker demand for combustion-engine parts and a slower-than-planned electric-mobility ramp-up. It expects annual savings of about €300 million from 2030, and the plan follows 4,700 cuts announced in 2024. Senior executives will take a temporary pay cut, and some new roles will be created in humanoid robotics and defence. Preliminary figures from 13 October showed a Q3 EBIT margin before special items of 4.2%, with the E-Mobility division still at -13.1%. The works council has asked for talks.

For engineers and suppliers, it is a sign that drivetrain and chassis-bearing capacity in Europe is being rebalanced toward robotics, industrial and defence applications. Expect more pressure on price and lead times for EV-specific bearing programmes.

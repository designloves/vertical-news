BMW cuts its 2026 profit outlook to near zero as it speeds up restructuring

Even the strongest premium German brand is now guiding to a break-even auto margin, with China and tariffs the main drags.

BMW lowered its 2026 automotive EBIT margin forecast to between -0.5% and +0.5%, from 1% to 3%. Third-quarter automotive EBIT fell 39.7% to €901 million on revenue down 6.5% to €26.67 billion. Group deliveries dropped 6.3% to 551,075 cars, with China down 25% while Europe rose 2.6% and the US 2.5%.

BMW names higher import tariffs, the retirement of older vehicle and battery generations earlier than planned because Neue Klasse demand is strong, and faster job cuts. It also mentions wider use of agentic AI in procurement planning and customer offers. Fully electric cars were 20% of quarterly sales, or 27.4% with plug-in hybrids. CFO Walter Mertl said speed is a key success factor in the realignment. Excluding restructuring, BMW still expects an operative margin above 2%.

For suppliers, an accelerated phase-out of older platforms can mean abrupt volume drops on legacy programmes while Gen6 platform parts ramp. It also reinforces the pressure on development cost across the German industry.

Stanford: 44% of widely cited claims about LLMs no longer hold on newer models

A caution for anyone basing AI strategy or design decisions on last year's research findings.

Researchers Owen Queen and James Zou retested 50 widely cited claims about large language models against 20 models released between July 2024 and September 2026. They found 44% have expired, 34% have weakened significantly, 16% still hold and 6% have strengthened.

Claims about what models cannot do expired fastest, with five of nine reasoning-related findings gone. Claims about tendencies such as sycophancy and political lean lasted longer. Among the 22 claims with an estimated expiry date, the average lifespan was about 15.7 months. Fabricated citations fell sharply from the 55% GPT-3.5 rate in a 2023 study, though current models range from 0% to 69% depending on the model.

The authors urge papers to record the model, version and date, and to publish prompts and code. For product and UX research, it is a prompt to re-test assumptions about what AI can and cannot do.

Timken beats on Q3 and lifts its 2026 outlook, helped by industrial demand and tariff refunds

A read on industrial bearing demand: growth is coming from industrial motion and pricing, not from the engineered bearings volume.

Timken reported third-quarter sales of $1.21 billion, up 4.7% (organic +2.8%). Adjusted EPS was $1.65, up 20% from $1.37. Operating cash flow was $256 million. It raised its 2026 outlook to adjusted EPS of $6.35–$6.50 and GAAP EPS of $4.05–$4.20, with revenue up about 5.5%.

Engineered Bearings sales were $771 million, up only 0.7%, with an adjusted EBITDA margin of 20.2% helped by tariff refunds, pricing and currency. Industrial Motion grew 12.4% to $440 million, aided by the Bijur Delimon acquisition. Adjusted EBITDA included a net $12.4 million benefit from IEEPA tariff refunds. Timken closed the sale of its belts business to Gates on 28 September and is closing a belts plant in Springfield, Missouri.

Management expects organic sales to grow mid-single digits in 2027. Compared with Schaeffler's restructuring, Timken shows how a US-focused, industrial-weighted bearing maker is holding up better than automotive-heavy peers.

European Parliament committee backs a softer 2035 car CO2 target: 87.5% plus a steel top-up

The 2035 rulebook that shapes powertrain and testing roadmaps is being loosened, and the final shape is still open.

On 4 November the European Parliament's environment committee voted on the car CO2 package. It backed an 87.5% cut in fleet emissions by 2035, plus a 7.5% credit for EU-made low-carbon steel, so 95% in total. The Commission had proposed a 90% cut with 10% from low-carbon steel and alternative fuels.

Small and highly efficient EVs made in the EU would earn extra credits in fleet averaging, and mandatory zero-emission targets for company fleets were downgraded to aspirational goals. The largest group, the EPP, called the vote a win for the car industry. Transport campaign group T&E said it is a step in the wrong direction and would divert investment from electrification. France and Germany agreed last month on a 90% cut with no compensation measures, so the Council position differs.

This is only Parliament's negotiating position. The full chamber votes in December, then talks with the Council begin. For OEMs and suppliers the practical point is that the mix of battery-electric, hybrid and efficient combustion programmes in the late 2020s is still unsettled, which affects validation and bearing and drivetrain planning.
